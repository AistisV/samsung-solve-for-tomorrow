# Round 6 generator: sustainability inside a student's own week

**Lens:** where waste, money or carbon hides in an ordinary Lithuanian teen's week: getting to school, the canteen, school events (šimtadienis, išleistuvės, Kalėdų balius, fairs), clothes and Vinted, phones, paper, textbooks, lost property, gifts, chores and younger siblings.

**Method:** I brainstormed about 30 ideas and scored them on the 8 round-6 filters (0–2 each, /16): **F1** we feel it, **F2** a juror gets it in 10 s, **F3** not a clone, **F4** a 2-day prototype, **F5** the AI is useful, **F6** traction by Nov 5, **F7** reach, **F8** measurable impact. I made 8 web searches, 7 of them Lithuanian-language prior-art checks. Claims are tagged [verified: source] or [unverified]. "No LT prior art found" always means *not found in one or two searches*. It never means "proven absent".

Already rejected or explored in earlier rounds, so scored low or left out: canteen plate-waste cameras (Minova won in 2022, and Alytus-region canteens already run smart scales [verified: round4/scout-teen-green.md, alytusplius.lt]), shower meters, CO₂ meters, step and points apps, "a map of X".

## Top 4 in depth

### 1. Radinukas: AI lost property that ends in reuse, not the bin (15/16)

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. On stage, the caretaker role puts a black hoodie on a table and takes one phone photo. In about 3 s the item appears in the catalogue: *"juodas džemperis su gobtuvu, Nike, ~M, rasta: sporto salė, 11-03"*.
2. A "student" types, in Lithuanian, *"pamečiau juodą džemperį per kūno kultūrą"* ("I lost a black hoodie in PE"). The hoodie comes up first, ahead of 40 other items. The student taps "Mano" and gets a pickup code.
3. The dashboard shows the item's 30-day clock and what happens to unclaimed items: clothes go to the school swap rail or a charity, and textile-bin waste is the last resort. Separate textile collection is mandatory in LT from 2025 [verified: round4/scout-teen-green.md].
4. The closing number on screen: "Our school's lost-property box on Nov 3: N items, about €X. Before: Y% returned. In the test week: Z%."

**What the AI does (useful, not decoration).**
- **Vision to structured tags:** item type, colour, brand, size, distinctive marks, and a name written on a label. A name is used only to *notify* that student, never shown publicly.
- **Semantic search** over messy student descriptions in Lithuanian ("mėlyna gertuvė su lipdukais" should match "blue bottle with stickers"), using embeddings plus a re-rank.
- **Proactive match:** when a student files "I lost X" before the item is found, a new photo that matches it triggers a notification.
- **Value estimate** (rough €, from type and brand) so the school can report "€ returned and € reused this month".

**How to build it in 2 days.**
- **Stack:** a web app (Next.js, or plain HTML+JS on Vercel), Supabase (Postgres + pgvector + image storage + auth by school email), and one multimodal LLM API call per photo for JSON tags. Embeddings come from the same provider. No native app: the caretaker uses the phone browser, as a PWA.
- **Day 1:** an upload page that calls the vision model and stores the tags; a catalogue grid; text search with embeddings.
- **Day 2:** a "lost report" form with matching and notification (email), the claim code, the 30-day status and routing, and an admin stats page. Then load the real Nov 3 box photos.
- **Privacy:** no faces (only items are photographed); blur name labels in public view; the school owns the data.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- With the director's permission, **photograph and catalogue the school's actual lost-property box** on Nov 3. That gives a real count and a € estimate, and it becomes the demo's dataset.
- A **survey of 100–150 students**: "Lost something at school this year? Did you get it back? Did your family buy a replacement?"
- A **quote** from the budėtoja/caretaker or deputy director on what happens to the box today (in many schools it is emptied at the end of the year [unverified: ask them]).
- If time allows, a 2-day live test: posters with a QR code by the box, and count the claims.

**The impact number.** The **share of found items returned to their owner** (before vs after), plus **€ and kg of items kept in use** (returned or reused rather than binned). If the team wants a CO₂ number, take a per-garment factor from a published LCA source and cite it. Don't make one up.

**LT hook.**
- The separate textile collection obligation (2025) [verified: round4 sources].
- Every LT school has a "radinių dėžė", and Tamo/e-dienynas could carry the notification as a later integration.
- It scales through the municipality's education department, one school after another.

**Prior-art check (LT).**
- Lithuanian searches for a school lost-property app built by students, or a national lost-items platform, found **nothing similar**. The results were unrelated school documents, a commercial "lost items search" listing ([paslaugos.lt](https://paslaugos.lt/isgelbeti-artefaktai-ip501)) and metal-detector services ([skelbiu.lt](https://m.skelbiu.lt/skelbimai/pamestu-daiktu-paieska-lietuvoje-30456761.html)). There was no radiniai.lt-style platform with AI ([tv3.lt tag page](https://www.tv3.lt/gaires/radiniai)).
- Abroad, lost-and-found SaaS for venues and airports exists [unverified: from general knowledge, e.g. hotel/airport lost-and-found software]. The school + reuse + AI-Lithuanian-search angle wasn't found.
- **Verdict:** no LT clone found. Check the Baltic SfT past finalists list before Nov 6.

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Isn't this just a box and a Facebook group? Is it even sustainability?"* The box is invisible: nobody digs through 80 items, so items get rebought. Our measured before/after return rate plus the 30-day route to reuse is the sustainability. Every returned hoodie is one that isn't bought new. The routing step stops the end-of-year dump.
2. *"Will the caretaker really photograph everything?"* It is one photo and no typing; the AI fills in everything else. In the pilot, student council volunteers do a 10-minute daily round. The test-week numbers show it is sustainable as a routine.

### 2. Proginė spinta: a school wardrobe for šimtadienis, išleistuvės and the big nights (15/16)

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. A 12th grader from last year uploads two photos of her išleistuvės dress. The AI writes the listing: type, colour, length, style, estimated size from the label, and condition notes. She marks it "lend, free / for the class fund".
2. An 11th-grade boy types: *"Šimtadienio tema 'Gatsby', ūgis 182, dydis 50, renginys vasario 14"* ("Šimtadienis theme 'Gatsby', height 182, size 50, event Feb 14").
3. The AI returns 3 matches available on that date. It explains the fit ("sleeves may be short at 182 cm: request alteration") and suggests how to adapt it to the theme: braces and a bow tie from the shared accessories box.
4. A class view shows: "Our class's 28 outfits: 17 borrowed, €X not spent, Y garments not bought new."

**What the AI does.**
- **Photo to listing:** garment type, colour, style, and the size read from the label photo.
- **Fit reasoning:** your height and measurements against the garment's size and length.
- **Theme matching:** free-text themes ("80s", "Gatsby", "Hogwarts") matched to items and accessory combos.
- **Date-availability scheduling** across schools.

The catalogue and the booking are plain CRUD. The AI is what makes listing take 20 s instead of 10 minutes.

**How to build it in 2 days.**
- **Stack:** the same as #1: Next.js or HTML, Supabase (items, bookings, storage), and a multimodal LLM for listing + matching (a JSON output schema). Optional: a "virtual try-on" is **not** needed and would break the 2-day limit.
- **Day 1:** upload + AI listing, catalogue, filters.
- **Day 2:** the request form with AI ranking + explanation, booking calendar, a class impact counter. Seed it with real items pledged by current 12th graders and alumni.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- A **survey of the 11th and 12th grades** (60–150 students) on what they spent, or expect to spend, on šimtadienis and išleistuvės outfits, how many times they wore them, and whether they would lend or borrow.
- A **pledge drive:** "post your old prom dress, suit or costume". Count the items listed by Nov 5, including from older siblings and alumni.
- A **quote** from the class tutor (auklėtoja) or the student council president who organises šimtadienis.

**The impact number.** **€ not spent and garments not bought new per graduating class.**
- **Price anchors:** in LT, renting a šimtadienis dress costs about €70–120 and buying one €70–250. A boys' suit is €125–300 (the source table is ambiguous on whether that is renting or buying), and šimtadienis show costumes cost €25–50 [verified, low-quality source: [finonamai.lt šimtadienis](https://finonamai.lt/informacija/sukneliu-nuoma-simtadieniui/), [finonamai.lt išleistuvės](https://finonamai.lt/informacija/isleistuviu-sukneles-nuoma/)]. Replace these with your own survey numbers.

**LT hook.**
- **Šimtadienis** (100 days before the final exams) is a specifically Lithuanian school ritual with themed costumes, which jurors from LV and EE will find charming.
- Išleistuvės are a national event too, and Vinted (LT) proves that Lithuanians resell clothes. 71% of Lithuanians buy something second-hand at least once a year [verified: BNS via round4/scout-teen-green.md].

**Prior-art check (LT).**
- Commercial rental salons exist (Unicum Boutique, Santa salonas, A. Grinkevičienė's salon, and others on [paslaugos.lt](https://paslaugos.lt/proginiu-drabuziu-nuoma)).
- A **school-run, peer-to-peer lending wardrobe was not found in LT**. A search for "drabužių mainai mokykloje" turned up only general school sustainability plans.
- Abroad, US schools run "Cinderella shop" prom-dress lending ([WDRB](https://www.wdrb.com/news/new-albany-high-schools-cinderella-shop-lets-students-rent-dresses-suits-for-prom/article_4d95dd50-a0dc-11ed-b063-1ff3a6769805.html)) and a UK pop-up lends 400 suits ([Lincs Online](https://www.lincsonline.co.uk/spalding/news/pop-up-prom-shop-allows-students-chance-to-hire-400-suits-a-9311246)). That is useful as proof of demand. It is not digital/AI and not in the Baltics.
- **Verdict:** a proven concept abroad that is offline-only, with no LT digital version found.

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Vinted and rental shops already do this."* Vinted sells, doesn't lend, and doesn't know your event date, theme or class. Rental salons charge €70–120 per night. Ours is free or costs a small fee for the class fund, and it lives where the demand is: in the school, with last year's class as the supply.
2. *"It's twice a year: will anyone use it?"* Šimtadienis (winter), išleistuvės (June), Kalėdų balius, school plays, Užgavėnės, debate and interview suits, and the PE and uniform hand-me-downs (#8) add up to a monthly need. Each graduating class also refills the stock automatically.

### 3. Dovanėlė be šlamšto: class Secret Santa without junk (13/16)

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. The class tutor creates "10B Kalėdų senelis, €10". Students join with a code and give 3 interests, 1 "please not" and optional sizes.
2. The draw runs, and I get "Gabija".
3. The AI turns her answers ("tinklinis, K-pop, piešiu" — volleyball, K-pop, I draw; "please not: candles") into 3 gift ideas under €10: second-hand K-pop albums (with a Vinted search link), a handmade bookmark kit for drawing, or "2 tickets to the school volleyball match + hot chocolate". Each idea gets a "lasting use" badge.
4. In January, one tap asks "Still using your gift?" The class sees "82% still used vs X% in last year's class".

**What the AI does.**
- **Personalised ideas under a budget**, steered towards used, handmade, consumable or experience gifts.
- **Generating real search links** (Vinted, local shops).
- Respecting the "please not" list.
- **Summarising the follow-up results.**

The draw itself is trivial. The useful part is the gift reasoning.

**How to build it in 2 days.** A web app (HTML/JS or Next.js) + Supabase or Firebase for groups, the draw and answers, plus an LLM call per pair. Day 1: groups, join, draw, private reveal. Day 2: AI ideas, the follow-up survey and the class dashboard.

**Traction by Nov 5.** **The strongest of the four on real use.** Classes start organising Secret Santa in November, so run real draws in 2–4 classes (60–100 students) by Nov 5 and count the participants. Also run a 1-question survey: "Of last year's Secret Santa gifts, how many do you still use?" That gives the baseline number.

**The impact number.** The **% of gifts still used after 1 month** (baseline vs ours), and the share of gifts that were second-hand, handmade or experiences.

**LT hook.** Secret Santa (*slaptasis Kalėdų senelis*) is a common class ritual [unverified: confirm in the survey]. It extends to the pooled teacher gift for Mokytojų diena and the end of the year (#23), and to birthdays.

**Prior-art check.**
- The Lithuanian search found **no LT Secret Santa app**. The results were only Santa-performer listings on [paslaugos.lt](https://paslaugos.lt/kaledu-senelis-kp426) and a school Christmas script from [vu.lt](https://www2404a.vu.lt/uploads/gerosios_patirties_darbai/kaledine_popiete.pdf).
- **But global apps exist** (DrawNames, Elfster, both with wish lists, and some now with AI gift suggestions) [unverified: from general knowledge, not searched]. So F3 is only 1.
- The differentiation has to be the **no-junk rule + the measured follow-up**.

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Elfster and DrawNames exist."* They optimise for buying stuff, and some are shop-affiliate driven. Ours optimises for gifts that get used and *measures* that. It is in Lithuanian and built for a school class with a tutor.
2. *"Is a €10 gift really a sustainability problem?"* Per gift, no. But it is a habit every class repeats every year, and it is the cheapest way to teach "buy less, choose better". The follow-up data makes the effect visible. Pitch it as the entry point to the school's other gift moments.

### 4. Taisyk, ne keisk: repair-or-replace advisor for teens' phones (13/16, Samsung-native)

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. A student photographs a cracked Galaxy and adds a screenshot of the battery health or the Samsung Members diagnostics result.
2. The AI reports: *"Corner crack, touch OK, battery 78%. Option A: screen protector now, €8. Option B: screen replacement, about €X at 3 shops near you. Option C: battery + screen, €Y. A new phone would cost €Z and about N kg CO₂. Recommendation: B. This phone can do 2 more years."*
3. A **class "crack census"** dashboard: "41% of 10B's phones are cracked; 9 are worth repairing for less than a quarter of the price of a new phone."

**What the AI does.**
- **Grades the crack and damage from a photo:** location, spread, and whether the display or touch is affected.
- **Reads the diagnostics screenshot.**
- **Weighs repair cost against phone age, value and battery** to recommend an option, using a hand-collected Lithuanian price table.
- **Explains it to a parent** in plain Lithuanian.

**How to build it in 2 days.** A web app + a multimodal LLM with a strict JSON schema, and a hand-built table of 5–10 local repair-shop prices for the 15 most common models. Phone CO₂ comes from manufacturers' published product environmental reports; cite them. Day 1: photo + diagnosis + price table. Day 2: recommendation, the class census page and a shop list.

**Traction by Nov 5.** A **crack census** in 3–4 classes: count and photograph cracked phones and log the model, age and battery. Survey 100 students on "when your phone breaks, what does your family do?" and get a quote from one local repair shop.

**The impact number.** The **number of phones repaired instead of replaced**, and the months of phone life added per class.

**LT hook and Samsung angle.**
- Samsung's LT site already offers **self-repair with original parts for some Galaxy models** ([samsung.com/lt](https://ushl.samsung.com/lt/support/mobile-devices/kaip-savarankiskai-sutaisyti-telefono-ekrana)) and **Members-app diagnostics** ([samsung.com/lt](https://www.samsung.com/lt/support/mobile-devices/how-to-use-the-phone-diagnostics-feature-on-your-samsung-galaxy-device)) [verified].
- This app is the decision layer on top of those tools, which a Samsung jury may like.
- EU right-to-repair is a policy tie-in [unverified detail].

**Prior-art check.**
- LT repair shops advertise quotes and free diagnostics ([paslaugos.lt](https://paslaugos.lt/uzklausos/mobiliuju-telefonu-remontas)), and phone insurance is growing ([finonamai.lt](https://finonamai.lt/informacija/sudauzytam-telefonui-galioja-turto-ar-civilines-atsakomybes-draudimas/)).
- **No LT repair-or-replace advisor app was found.**
- Samsung Members diagnoses hardware but does not recommend repair vs replace with local prices [verified: the Samsung LT support page describes diagnostics only].

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"It's a wrapper around a chatbot and Samsung Members."* The value is the local price data, the crack grading tuned on real class photos (the census becomes the test set) and a decision that parents trust. Show its accuracy against a repair shop's verdict on 10 phones.
2. *"Repair shops give free quotes, so why the app?"* Teens don't go. They live with the crack and then beg for a new phone. The app catches the moment while a €8 protector or a €40 screen still saves the phone, and it gives the class a number it can act on.

## Summary: top 4
1. **Radinukas (15/16):** photo-and-search AI lost property, where unclaimed items go to reuse instead of the bin. The best combination of "everyone feels it", a 10-second pitch and a real dataset by Nov 3 (the school's own box). No LT prior art found. Weakness: jurors may ask "is it green?", so the before/after return rate and reuse routing must be the headline.
2. **Proginė spinta (15/16):** last year's class lends šimtadienis costumes, išleistuvės dresses and suits to this year's class, with AI listing, fit and theme matching. Šimtadienis is a uniquely Lithuanian hook. Rental costs €70–120 a night [low-quality source]. Proven offline abroad (US "Cinderella shops"); no LT school P2P version found.
3. **Dovanėlė be šlamšto (13/16):** a class Secret Santa where AI suggests used, handmade or experience gifts and measures "still used?". It has the most real users obtainable by Nov 5, but it is a clone risk (DrawNames, Elfster abroad) and seasonal.
4. **Taisyk, ne keisk (13/16):** AI crack grading + battery + LT repair prices gives a repair-or-replace verdict, plus a class crack census. It is Samsung-native (it builds on Samsung LT self-repair and diagnostics), but there is wrapper risk.

**Suggested pairing:** #1 or #2 as the main pitch, with the other "school reuse" ideas (#8, #18, #28, #30) as the roadmap. That turns one app into a "school circular-economy layer" and answers the "development plan" criterion.
