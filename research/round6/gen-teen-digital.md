# Round 6 generator: hook onto what teens already use

**Lens:** don't ask a 15-year-old to install a new app. Start from the places they already are: Messenger and Discord group chats, Instagram, Vinted, Tamo/e-dienynas, Wolt, Spotify, games, the deposit machine and the school itself. Then hook sport or sustainability onto that habit.

**Method:** I generated about 30 ideas and scored each 0–2 on the 8 round-6 filters ([`BRIEF.md`](BRIEF.md)): **F1** we feel it · **F2** juror gets it in 10 s · **F3** not a clone · **F4** 2-day prototype · **F5** AI is useful · **F6** traction by Nov 5 · **F7** reach · **F8** measurable impact. I spent all 8 web searches on prior-art checks, 7 of them in Lithuanian.

**A warning about the evidence:** Lithuanian-language search through this tool is weak. Most results were tv3.lt/lrt.lt tag pages. "No Lithuanian prior art found" therefore means **absence not proven** [unverified absence]. The team must spend 15 minutes on Google.lt, the App Store/Google Play (LT region), and Facebook groups before committing.

## Top 4 in detail

### 🥇 1. Radinių lentyna: AI lost & found that empties the pile (Sustainability) — 16/16

**Pitch.** Every school has a box or rack of lost hoodies, water bottles, gym shoes, PE kits and calculators. Nobody searches it, because you'd have to go and dig. At year end it gets binned or given away. We put the pile online: a teacher or a duty student snaps each item in 5 seconds. The AI writes the tag, and students search "juodas džemperis" or get pinged when something matching their description turns up. Whatever is unclaimed after 30 days is listed on Vinted or donated, and the money goes to the class fund.

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. Hold up a real hoodie from your school's pile and photograph it with the Samsung phone. Two seconds later the card appears: *"Juodas Nike džemperis su gobtuvu, M dydis, raudona dėmė ant rankovės, rastas sporto salėje, 11-02"*.
2. A juror types "pamečiau juodą džemperį su gobtuvu" in plain Lithuanian. The match comes up first and a "claim" button appears.
3. Show the counter: *"This week: 47 items logged, 19 returned to owners, 6 listed for the class fund, est. X kg not binned."*
4. Show the 30-day timer: an unclaimed item turns into an auto-drafted Vinted listing or a "donate" label.

**What the AI actually does.**
- **Vision tagging:** a multimodal model (GPT-4o-mini / Gemini Flash / Claude Haiku via API) returns JSON with type, colour, brand, size, distinguishing marks, and any name written on the label (OCR).
- **Semantic matching:** text embeddings match a student's free-text "lost" description to item tags in Lithuanian or English, typos included ("džempėris"). Keyword search fails here; that's why AI is needed.
- **Auto-notify:** a standing "I lost my…" request triggers a ping when a new item matches above a threshold.
- **The end-of-life step:** for unclaimed items, the AI drafts a Vinted title, description and price band.

**How to build it in 2 days (stack).**
- **Day 1:** a Next.js or plain HTML+JS web app (a PWA, so no install), with Supabase or Firebase for storage and photos. A `/log` page takes the photo, sends it to the vision API, and saves the JSON. A `/search` page runs embedding search (Supabase pgvector, or just cosine similarity in JS over fewer than 500 items).
- **Day 2:** a "lost request" form plus email or Instagram-DM notification. A Telegram/Discord bot is optional, and so is auto-posting to an @radiniai_[school] Instagram story. Add the stats counter, then log the real pile.
- **Cost:** under €5 in API calls.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- Log your school's actual pile on Nov 3–4. That's the strongest evidence possible: "we photographed 60 items in 40 minutes".
- Count how many get claimed by Nov 5.
- Survey 100+ students via the class chats: "Have you lost something at school this year? Did you get it back? Would you search a feed?"
- Get a quote from the vice-principal or the cleaning/administration staff about what happens to the pile today.

**The impact number.** The **% of lost items returned** (baseline: measure it now, or ask staff for an estimate) and **kg of textiles kept out of the bin** per school per year. Scale it: × ~1,000 general-education schools in LT [unverified count; check ŠMSM].

**The LT hook.**
- Separate textile collection became mandatory in LT in 2025, and textile in mixed waste hit a record [verified, in round4/scout-teen-green.md].
- Vinted is a Lithuanian company [verified: round4]. The end-of-life channel is literally a Lithuanian circular-economy success.
- The rollout channel is the school administration, then the municipality's education department.

**Prior-art check.**
- **LT:** two Lithuanian searches ("mokykla pamestų daiktų radiniai programėlė", "pamestų ir rastų daiktų platforma Lietuvoje dirbtinis intelektas") found **no Lithuanian school lost-and-found app** [unverified absence]. A third ("pamestų daiktų" mokykloje … labdarai) found only foreign schools.
- **Abroad:** schools do it by hand, with Google Slides catalogues, photo folders and email claims ([Brunswick CSD](https://www.brunswickcsd.org/article/689901), [Columbia HS / EGCSD](https://egcsd.org/2023/03/30/columbia-launches-lost-and-found-website/)). End-of-year donation is standard ([Ingunnarskóli, Reykjavík](https://ingunnarskoli.reykjavik.is/en/news/2025/lost-and-found), [EdWeek on the "lost-and-found mound"](https://edweek.org/leadership/what-happens-to-the-lost-and-found-mound-at-the-end-of-the-year/2024/05)).
- **Commercial:** AI lost-and-found SaaS for venues and airports exists (e.g. Boomerang, Chargerback, Crowdfind) [unverified, from memory; check before the pitch]. It isn't aimed at schools and doesn't close the loop to Vinted/donation.
- **Verdict:** the idea isn't novel in the world, but it is untaken in LT schools as far as we can tell, and the circular "unclaimed → Vinted → class fund" twist is ours.

**The top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"That's just a photo gallery; why AI?"* With 200 grey hoodies, a gallery is useless; you'd scroll for ever. AI makes the pile **searchable by description** and **pushes** the match to the owner. Show the demo: free-text "lost grey hoodie with a cat patch" finds it among 60 items in 1 second. Without AI, the duty student would have to write tags by hand, and that's exactly why no school keeps it up.
2. *"Is this really sustainability or just convenience?"* Every returned hoodie is one not re-bought. Textile production is one of the highest-footprint consumer categories [verified framing: EU textile strategy; cite it properly]. Unclaimed items go to reuse, not to the bin. The number we report is kg of textiles kept in use, plus an estimate of € saved for families.

### 🥈 2. Išaugau: the hand-me-down chain for kids' sport gear (Sport + Sustainability) — 15/16

**Pitch.** Kids in sport clubs outgrow skates, football boots, ski boots, hockey gear, karate gi and swim fins almost every season. Parents buy new; the old pair sits in the hallway. **Išaugau** is a club-level swap: photograph the outgrown gear, the AI reads the size, brand and condition and suggests a fair price (or "free"), and it **pre-matches** the item with a younger kid in the same club whose size is coming up. It lowers the cost of sport (the access angle) and cuts waste (the sustainability angle). It hits both themes.

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. Photograph a real pair of outgrown football boots, including the size label inside the tongue. The card appears: *"Adidas Predator, EU 36, studs 80% left, minor scuffs → suggested €15 / swap"*.
2. The dashboard for the team's U11 group shows *"3 kids will likely need EU 36 within 3 months"*, based on the size history parents entered once.
3. A parent gets a ping, claims the boots, and hands-over happens at the next training session. No shipping, no Vinted haggling.
4. The counter: *"Club X, this season: 34 items passed down, €620 saved by families, ~Y kg not landfilled."*

**What the AI actually does.**
- **Vision:** item type, brand, model, size read from the label (OCR) and a condition grade (stud wear, sole separation, blade rust).
- **Price suggestion:** a rule-based anchor (new price × condition factor), with an AI explanation.
- **Matching and forecasting:** a simple growth model (the child's age and size history) predicts who needs the next size up and when. That's the non-trivial, "this is why it isn't Vinted" part.
- **Safety flag:** for gear where wear matters (helmets, hockey protection), the AI warns "check the expiry/impact history" and won't list helmets with visible cracks.

**How to build it in 2 days (stack).** The same base as #1 (a PWA plus Supabase plus a vision API), so the team could even build one engine for both. Day 1: logging and tagging. Day 2: kid profiles (age and current size only, no names, which matters for GDPR), the matching table, and Messenger/email notifications.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- Pick one club a team member belongs to (football, hockey, figure skating, karate).
- Survey the parents' group chat: "How many items did your child outgrow this year? What did you do with them?"
- Log the real items from 5–10 families.
- Get a coach's quote.
- The November timing is good: the winter-sport season (skates, skis) is starting now.

**The impact number.** **€ saved per family per season** and **items passed down per club**. Secondary: kg of sport goods reused.

**The LT hook.** Sport clubs and NVŠ (non-formal education) programmes are the channel [verified that the NVŠ system exists, via local-data.md: mokausi.lt]. Cost is a known barrier to youth sport [unverified for LT; find an LSU or ŠMSM source or ask a coach]. Decathlon opened in Vilnius [verified: [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/decathlon-enters-the-baltics-and-kazakhstan/63006.article)], but its BuyBack/Second Life scheme shows up only in FR/UK/BE/DE/ES coverage, **not LT** [unverified absence; check decathlon.lt].

**Prior-art check.**
- **LT:** the Lithuanian search ("vaikų sportinio inventoriaus mainai pačiūžos slidės išaugo platforma Lietuvoje") found **only classifieds and rentals**: [skelbiu.lt children's skis](https://www.skelbiu.lt/paieska/slides-vaikams/), [skelbiu.lt ski poles](https://www.skelbiu.lt/paieska/slidinejimo-lazdos/), and a [ski-boot rental guide](https://finonamai.lt/informacija/slidinejimo-batu-nuoma/). No club-level swap was found [unverified absence].
- **Abroad:** Decathlon BuyBack/Second Life ([BusinessGreen](https://www.businessgreen.com/news/4200926/decathlon-rolls-buyback-service-wider-range-sports-equipment), [Decathlon Spain](https://www.sgieurope.com/csr-and-sustainability/decathlon-spain-extends-second-hand-initiative-to-70-stores/112422.article)). SidelineSwap (US) and Vinted's own sports category [unverified]. Many clubs run informal "kit swap" tables [unverified].
- **Clone risk (F3 = 1):** Vinted and skelbiu already let you sell gear. The difference is **club-internal, size-forecast matching and hand-over at training**. The team must say this in one sentence.

**The top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Parents can just use Vinted."* They can, but they mostly don't for €10 boots: photographing, haggling and shipping cost more than the item is worth. Our survey will show what really happens to outgrown gear. Inside a club, the buyer is already at the same training session, the size is predicted, and the hand-over is free.
2. *"Isn't used sport gear unsafe?"* For helmets and protective gear, yes, and the AI blocks or flags them. Boots, skates, skis, clothing and balls are fine to reuse, and they're the bulk.

### 🥉 3. Šimtadienio spinta: wear it twice (Sustainability, LT tradition) — 14/16

**Pitch.** Šimtadienis (the party 100 days before graduation) and išleistuvės (prom) are huge Lithuanian school traditions. Each grade-12 student buys an outfit worn once: girls a dress, boys a suit. **Šimtadienio spinta** is a closed, school-verified lending closet. Last year's graduates (and older siblings) upload their outfits. The AI tags style, colour, length and size from a mirror selfie, matches this year's students by size and taste, and makes sure the same dress never appears twice at the same event or in the same town.

**The 2-minute demo.** Upload a mirror photo of last year's dress. It gets tagged *"emerald satin, midi, S, worn at Šimtadienis 2026, Kaunas"*. A buyer describes *"kažką žalio, ne per ilgo, S dydis"* and gets the match plus a collision warning ("worn at your school last year: show it?"). The borrow/return calendar ends with the counter: *"18 outfits shared, €X saved, Y new garments not bought."*

**What the AI does.** Vision tagging of style attributes, natural-language taste search, size matching from measurements, and the "collision avoidance" feature (the social fear that actually stops reuse). It's useful but lighter than #1/#2, hence F5 = 1.

**Build (2 days).** The same PWA/Supabase/vision-API base as #1. Add a lending calendar and the school/graduation-year verification (a school email or a teacher-issued code).

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- Survey grades 11–12 and recent graduates: what did you spend, how often has it been worn since, would you lend or borrow?
- Collect 10–20 real outfits from last year's alumni via the class Instagram.
- **Timing advantage:** šimtadienis is ~Feb 2027, so this year's grade 12 is shopping in the next 2–3 months. The demand is live during the contest.

**The impact number.** **New garments not bought** and **€ saved per graduate**. One reference site quotes graduation dress rental at €70–120 vs €70–250 to buy, and a boys' suit at €125–300 [weakly verified: [finonamai.lt](https://finonamai.lt/informacija/isleistuviu-sukneles-nuoma/), an SEO-style site; replace with the team's own survey].

**The LT hook.** A uniquely Lithuanian tradition (šimtadienis); jurors will grin. Vinted is a Lithuanian company. A Vilnius city clothing-swap event exists, so the city already promotes swaps [verified: [vilnius-events.lt](https://www.vilnius-events.lt/event/clothing-swap-and-fair/?lang=en)]. That's a potential partner.

**Prior-art check.** The Lithuanian search ("šimtadienio išleistuvių suknelė vienam vakarui nuoma mainai abiturientės platforma") found commercial rental salons and classifieds only ([paslaugos.lt suknelių nuoma](https://paslaugos.lt/sukneliu-nuoma), [finonamai.lt šimtadienio nuoma](https://finonamai.lt/informacija/sukneliu-nuoma-simtadieniui/)). **No peer-to-peer school swap was found** [unverified absence]. Abroad: US "prom dress closet" charities and Rent the Runway-style rental [unverified, from memory].

**The top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Girls don't want to wear someone else's dress."* That's exactly why the collision check exists (never the same school or town in the same year), and why it's lending, not second-hand. The survey will test the willingness; if it's under ~30%, pivot to boys' suits and accessories.
2. *"It's once a year; is there a daily user?"* There isn't one, and that's the honest weakness (F7 = 1). The answer: the same closet serves every school event (Kalėdų balius, Rugsėjo 1-oji, concerts, competitions), and the engine is shared with #1. *Recommendation:* pitch it as a **feature of #1**, a seasonal "Šimtadienio spinta" module, not standalone.

### 4. Kas žaidžia?: the group-chat bot that makes pickup games actually happen (Sport) — 13/16

**Pitch.** Teens don't lack apps; they lack **enough people at the same court at the same time**. The pickup game dies in the group chat: "kas po pamokų?" gets 3 "maybe"s and nobody comes. **Kas žaidžia?** is a Discord/Messenger/Telegram bot that lives in the existing class chat. It reads the casual replies ("gal", "jei nelis", "po 16 galiu"), turns them into a real head-count, confirms the time when a threshold (e.g. 6 for 3×3) is met, checks the weather, pings the wider school server when the game is one player short, and splits fair teams from past results.

**The 2-minute demo.** Live in a Discord server on the projector: three jurors type casual Lithuanian replies. The bot answers: *"5/6 for 3×3 at 16:30, Žirmūnų court. Rain chance 10%. Need 1 more → pinged #11-klasės."* Someone joins, and the bot posts the teams: *"Komanda A: … (avg rating 1,520) vs B: … (1,510)."* The stats card: *"This month, 9 games happened out of 14 proposals (was 3/14 before the bot)."*

**What the AI does.** An LLM parses messy Lithuanian slang and emoji availability into structured yes/no/time (rules can't). An Elo-style rating balances the teams. Weather comes from an API. The AI part is real but modest (F5 = 1).

**Build (2 days).** Day 1: discord.js or python-telegram-bot, the LLM call for parsing, and SQLite. Day 2: team balancing, a weather API (Open-Meteo, free), the "1 short" broadcast, and stats logging. Messenger bots need Meta approval, so **use Discord or Telegram**.

**Traction by Nov 5.** Install it in 2–3 real class chats or a school sports Discord on Nov 3, and count games proposed vs played by Nov 5. Quick survey: "How many planned games fell through this month?" A PE teacher's quote.

**The impact number.** **Pickup games that actually happen per week** (proposal → played conversion), and the active minutes they add.

**The LT hook.** Basketball culture; courts are everywhere but empty in the evening [unverified]. The channel is school sports clubs, LMKL (school basketball league) and the municipal youth departments.

**Prior-art check.** Two Lithuanian searches ("programėlė rasti su kuo žaisti krepšinį futbolą aikštelėje Lietuvoje…", "lietuviška programėlė sporto partnerių paieška futbolo mačas trūksta žaidėjų startuolis") returned only sports news (lrt.lt, tv3.lt) and **no Lithuanian pickup-game app** [unverified absence; the search tool was weak here]. Abroad there are many: Playtomic (padel/tennis booking, present in LT [unverified]), Spond (team management), Footy Addicts, Meetup [unverified, from memory]. **Clone risk vs Kineto (2025 winner):** Kineto shows groups and facilities for *individuals*. This one lives inside the *existing* friend chat and fixes the head-count problem. The difference is defensible but needs saying (F3 = 1).

**The top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Isn't this just a Discord poll?"* A poll needs everyone to click; teens reply "gal po 5 jei nelis 🏀". The bot understands that, follows up with the "maybes", and broadcasts when the game is one short. The jury sees the demo with real messy messages.
2. *"Last year's winner (Kineto) already gets youth moving."* Kineto plans *your* calendar. We fix the moment a *group* game collapses, inside the chat teens already use, with no new app to install.

## Summary: top 4

1. **Radinių lentyna (16/16, Sustainability).** An AI-tagged, searchable feed of the school's lost-and-found pile; unclaimed items go to Vinted or donation for the class fund. It's the best fit: every student feels it, the demo uses a real hoodie, you can log your own school's pile by Nov 5, and the impact number is clear (% returned, kg of textiles saved). No Lithuanian school solution found [unverified absence]. Foreign schools do it manually, and there is venue SaaS abroad.
2. **Išaugau (15/16, Sport + Sustainability).** Club-level hand-me-down of outgrown sport gear, with AI size/condition reading and next-size forecasting. It hits both themes, and the November winter-sport timing helps. Its clone risk is Vinted/skelbiu, so it must stress club-internal matching. Decathlon's BuyBack isn't found in LT.
3. **Šimtadienio spinta (14/16, Sustainability).** A school-verified lending closet for šimtadienis/prom outfits, with collision avoidance. A great LT hook, but seasonal. Best as a module of #1 on the same engine.
