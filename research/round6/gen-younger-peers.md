# Round 6 generator: building for someone close (younger kids, siblings, left-out classmates)

**Lens.** "We're the older students. We saw this happen to our younger siblings and the kids in our school." The users are grades 1–8 in our own school, younger siblings, and classmates who get left out: kids with ADHD, dyslexia, autism, asthma, diabetes or excess weight, kids in the PE "preparatory/special group", new arrivals from Ukraine who don't speak Lithuanian yet, and kids bused in from villages. This is the LaKiKe shape (teens built for grades 1–3 and placed 2nd in 2024, see [`../past-winners.md`](../past-winners.md)).

**Constraint check for every idea:** buildable in 2 days, and testable with **one class of younger kids by Nov 5** (with the teacher's OK).

**Search budget used:** 8/8 WebSearch calls. Seven were Lithuanian-language prior-art and fact checks; results are in the top-4 sections and in "LT facts found" at the end.

Filters (0–2 each, /16): **F1** we feel it · **F2** juror gets it in 10 s · **F3** not a clone · **F4** 2-day prototype · **F5** AI useful · **F6** traction by Nov 5 · **F7** reach · **F8** measurable impact.

## Top 1: Visi žaidžia ("Nobody on the bench"): an AI adapter for inclusive PE (Sport & tech) — 16/16

**Problem.** In every PE lesson some kids are not doing the lesson: the special/preparatory-group kids, the asthmatic, the kid with a cast, the overweight kid hiding, the Ukrainian newcomer who doesn't understand the rules. Most of them sit on the bench.
- Lithuanian pupils are assigned to PE health groups by the family doctor after the annual check-up [verified: VU study of 308 special-PE-group pupils, grades 5–12, 34 LT schools, [epublications.vu.lt](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:1922052/1922052.pdf)].
- A Lithuanian university survey found students "often sat on a bench or did unrelated tasks" during school PE [verified: VGTU study, [vdu.lt CRIS](https://www.vdu.lt/cris/entities/publication/b8fc4c5f-d568-4a09-a886-dad589a4f335)].
- "Still cases where kids are left on the bench" (adaptive-sport coach) [verified: Sportas.lt, in `../local-data.md`].
- Since 2024, all schools must accept SEN pupils if the parents choose it [verified: [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/lietuvoje/2/1842238/itraukusis-ugdymas-nuo-2024-metu-suplanuotos-150-mln-euru-investicijos-kas-ir-kaip-keisis)]. That means more mixed-ability PE lessons with the same single teacher.

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. (0:00) A photo of a real bench in our gym with 4 kids on it: "this is every PE lesson in our school."
2. (0:20) On a tablet, the teacher picks today's lesson: "Dodgeball, grade 3, 24 kids." She taps 4 chips: *astma*, *special group: no running*, *arm in a cast*, *no Lithuanian (UA)*.
3. (0:40) In 5 seconds the AI returns a 4-row plan. The asthmatic kid gets "walk-in zone, swap every 3 min, inhaler reminder at warm-up". The kid with the cast is "referee with whistle + score keeper". The special-group kid is "target thrower from a fixed spot, no running". The UA kid gets the rules as a 4-picture strip with Ukrainian audio.
4. (1:10) The cards appear big on screen and print as A6 cards.
5. (1:30) A clip from our class test: the same 3rd-grade class, bench empty, plus the counter "benched minutes: 34 → 6" (whatever we actually measure).
6. (1:50) A quote from the PE teacher.

**What the AI actually does.**
- It takes a lesson or game (free text or a picked template) and each pupil's constraint tags, and produces an **adapted variant or a meaningful role per pupil**, constrained by a safety rule-set we write: no running for "special group", no contact for "cast".
- This is real reasoning across rules, constraints and group size, which a teacher has no time for with 24 kids.
- It generates the **picture strip and translated audio** (UA/RU/EN) for newcomers.
- It learns from the teacher's thumbs up/down on each suggestion (stored per school).
- **Privacy:** tags only, no names or diagnoses stored; the teacher's device keeps the mapping.

**Build in 2 days (stack).**
- Web app (Next.js or plain HTML + JS), installable as a PWA on a tablet or phone.
- An LLM API with a strict JSON schema and a system prompt containing the safety rules and ~30 seed games from the Lithuanian PE curriculum.
- Pictures: a pre-made icon set, or an image model for the 4-frame strip with cached results. Audio: browser TTS, or a cloud TTS for Ukrainian.
- Day 1: game library, tag chips, prompt, JSON → cards. Day 2: picture strip + TTS, print view, a "benched minutes" stopwatch screen for the test.

**Class test by Nov 5.**
- One PE lesson in grade 3–5 with our school's PE teacher.
- Lesson A (Nov 3 or 4) runs as usual: we observe and log the minutes each kid sat out (a stopwatch per bench kid).
- Lesson B (Nov 4 or 5) uses the cards for the same class.
- Plus a 1-minute emoji survey of the bench kids ("how much fun?"), a PE-teacher quote, and a 5-question survey of 3–5 PE teachers from neighbouring schools.

**Impact number.** **Benched minutes per lesson** (or the % of the class active), measured before and after. Scaled up: the number of pupils in special/preparatory groups, times PE lessons per week.

**LT hook.**
- The 2024 inclusive-education law change and the €150 M inclusion investment [verified: LRT].
- The ministry's own PE adaptation recommendations exist on paper [verified: [nsa.smm.lt](https://nsa.smm.lt/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/16_BP-pritaikymo-rekomendacijos.pdf)]. Our tool turns them into a 5-second card.
- LT teens are among Europe's least active [verified, `../local-data.md`].
- Abuse of PE exemptions is a known issue [verified, `../local-data.md`]. Giving those kids a real role gets them moving anyway.
- **Channel:** school PE methodological groups, municipal inclusion consultants (~100 trained [verified: LRT]), the NŠA, LSU teacher-training courses.

**Prior-art check.**
- Searched: "dirbtinis intelektas fizinio ugdymo mokytojams pamokų planai pritaikymas mokiniams su negalia programėlė Lietuvoje" and "parengiamoji fizinio ugdymo grupė … ant suolo".
- **No Lithuanian app or startup found.**
- What exists is teacher-training courses on adapting PE for SEN pupils, e.g. the [LSU qualification programme](https://www.lsu.lt/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Pedagogu-kvalifikacijos-tobulinimo-programa_pradiniam-ugdymui_2022.pdf). It also says ICT is "an underused resource" in PE, which supports our case.
- Abroad: generic AI lesson-plan generators for teachers exist [unverified specifics]. A per-pupil inclusion adapter for PE was not found, but only in a narrow search.
- Not a clone of any past Baltic winner (all were movement or learning apps for the general student).

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"Is it safe? AI telling a kid with asthma what to do?"* → The AI only picks from a teacher-approved game library and a hard rule-set (special group = no running, etc.). The teacher sees and approves every card. It suggests roles, never medical advice, and the doctor's PE-group assignment stays the source of truth.
2. *"Teachers can do this themselves."* → On paper, yes (the ministry recommendations exist). In practice, one teacher with 24 kids and 4 different needs has 30 seconds before the lesson. Our test shows the gap: benched minutes before vs after. The teacher's quote is the proof.

## Top 2: Pamestukas: AI lost-and-found for the school (Sustainability) — 15/16

**Problem.**
- Every school has a mountain of lost jackets, hats, sports shoes, PE kits and water bottles, mostly from grades 1–5.
- Parents never see it. Kids don't remember where they left things. The pile gets thrown out or donated at the end of the year, and families buy new.
- Every teen on the team has a younger sibling who "lost the third hat this winter" [team to confirm with own story].
- Since 2025 textile waste must be collected separately in LT [verified: AM, `../local-data.md`]. Clothes thrown out by schools are an avoidable textile stream.

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. (0:00) Photo of *our school's* actual lost-and-found pile; we counted N items on Nov 3.
2. (0:20) A student snaps a jacket with a phone. The AI fills in: "Striukė, mėlyna, ~128 cm, Reima, name tag 'J. K.', found: sporto salė". It also recognises "this looks like the same one as item #41, a duplicate photo".
3. (0:50) A parent's view: types "pilka kepurė su pomponu" ("grey hat with a pom-pom"). The AI semantic search shows 3 matches with photos, and she taps "mine, pick up tomorrow".
4. (1:20) Dashboard: "Week 1: 87 items catalogued, 19 returned, ~X kg kept from waste". End of term: unclaimed items are auto-sorted into "swap rack" and "textile container".
5. (1:50) Quote from the school administrator or a parent.

**What the AI actually does.**
- **Vision tagging**: type, colour, size estimate, brand, readable name tags (OCR).
- **Duplicate detection**: the same item photographed twice.
- **Natural-language and photo search**: a parent can describe the item in LT/RU/UA, or upload an old photo of the kid wearing it.
- **End-of-term sorting**: reuse vs textile container, plus weight estimates.
- Without AI, nobody would fill in 100 item forms. That is why paper lost-and-found lists don't exist.

**Build in 2 days (stack).**
- Phone web app for students on duty: photo → a vision LLM returns a JSON of tags → stored in Supabase or Firebase.
- Parent page with a text search: LLM query expansion or embeddings (CLIP or text embeddings over the tags).
- A QR poster on the pile and a link sent by the class teacher through Tamo/e-dienynas messages.
- Day 1: capture + tagging + DB. Day 2: parent search, the "claim" button, the stats page.

**Class test by Nov 5.**
- Nov 3: catalogue the real pile (an afternoon for 2 people).
- Nov 3–4: with the school's OK, one or two class teachers of grades 1–4 send the link to parents.
- By Nov 5: count claims and returns, plus a 6-question parent survey (aim 50+) asking "how many items has your child lost this school year, and how much did replacing them cost?"
- A quote from the deputy head or the caretaker.

**Impact number.** **Items returned to owners per week**, and **kg of clothing kept out of waste** (weigh items, or use average weights). Plus parents' self-reported € spent replacing lost items [survey, not invented].

**LT hook.**
- Mandatory separate textile collection since 2025-01-01, with insufficient container capacity [verified: AM/MadeinVilnius in `../local-data.md`].
- **Channel:** Tamo/e-dienynas parent messages (every school has them), school administrations, municipal education departments. Unclaimed items can go to a charity partner (e.g. Caritas, Raudonasis Kryžius clothing points [unverified partnership]).

**Prior-art check.**
- Searched Lithuanian: "mokyklos pamestų daiktų programėlė nuotraukos mokiniai sukūrė" and "'pamestų daiktų' mokykloje krūva striukės nesusigrąžina tėvai".
- **No Lithuanian school lost-and-found app found.** Both searches returned only foreign stories about the problem: US/UK school piles ([EdWeek](https://edweek.org/leadership/what-happens-to-the-lost-and-found-mound-at-the-end-of-the-year/2024/05), [CBS Sacramento](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/folsom-return-sweaters-lunchboxes-elementary-school/), [City Limits: NY teens donate unclaimed items](https://citylimits.org/?p=22458269)). That confirms the problem is universal.
- Commercial lost-and-found software for venues and airports exists abroad [unverified names; do a 10-minute English check before submitting]. A school-specific, kid-clothing, AI-tagged version in LT appears open.
- **Weakness:** the LT search was shallow, because Lithuanian schools may solve this in a Facebook group. Ask 3 parents: "is there a FB group at your school for lost things?"

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"Is this really sustainability, or just convenience?"* → Every returned jacket is a jacket not bought. Unclaimed items go to reuse instead of the bin. We report kg, and textiles are now a regulated waste stream in LT. It is also the gateway habit: kids learn things have value.
2. *"Privacy: photos of kids' things with names on them."* → No photos of children. Name tags are OCR'd and then blurred in the public view; only the class teacher sees the full name. Parents log in via the school's messaging link.

## Top 3: Kiemo kapitonai ("Recess captains"): teens lead younger kids' recess with AI game cards (Sport & tech) — 14/16

**Problem.**
- In grades 1–4, long breaks are either phones in the corridor or chaos.
- New arrivals (Ukrainian kids) and shy, disabled or overweight kids are left at the edge because the games are verbal and unspoken rules dominate.
- Lithuania had roughly 8,000 Ukrainian pupils in schools in mid-2023 [verified: [LRT mediateka](https://www.lrt.lt/mediateka/irasas/2000255691/lietuvos-mokyklose-per-8-tukst-ukrainieciu-mokosi-ir-lietuviu-kalbos-bet-mokytojai-skatina-neapleisti-gimtosios); the current number is not found, ask the Mokinių registras].
- Winter: the yard is often closed and dark (Nov–Mar).
- UK experience says older pupils as playground leaders "can prove effective in engaging reluctant children" [verified: [Energize STW active breaktimes](https://www.energizestw.org.uk/active-breaktimes)].

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. A team member, in a captain's vest, opens the app: "Corridor, 2nd floor, 14 kids, grade 2, 1 kid in a wheelchair, 3 UA speakers, 15 min, equipment: 1 soft ball."
2. The AI produces a game card: name, a 4-picture rule strip, LT + UA one-liners read aloud, a role for the wheelchair user ("the lighthouse": she calls the colours), and a calm-down variant.
3. A video from our test: 2nd graders playing it.
4. The stats: "kids playing at the start vs at the end of break" and "UA kids who joined".

**What the AI does.**
- Constraint-based game generation and adaptation from a seed library of ~40 traditional Lithuanian/Ukrainian yard games (space, weather, group size, abilities, languages).
- Picture-rule strips and translated audio.
- After each break the captain taps "worked / flopped", and the AI ranks games per class.
- It shares a core with Top 1. **Option: merge #1 and #3** as "the same engine, two users": the PE teacher in the lesson, teen captains at recess.

**Build in 2 days.** Same stack as Top 1: web app + LLM JSON + icon set + TTS. Add a captain "flop/hit" log and a printable deck of the 20 best cards.

**Class test by Nov 5.**
- 2–3 long breaks with one 2nd–4th-grade class, with the class teacher's OK.
- Count kids actively playing at minutes 0, 5 and 10. Use break 1 without captains as the baseline.
- Short emoji survey of the kids, and a quote from the class teacher.
- Teen volunteers recruited from our own grade (a sign-up count is traction too).

**Impact number.** **Kids actively playing during the long break** (headcount, %), plus newcomer kids participating.

**LT hook.**
- The Ukrainian pupil population [verified, 2023].
- Inclusive-education reform from 2024 [verified: LRT].
- LT teen inactivity [verified, `../local-data.md`].
- **Channel:** school student councils (mokinių taryba), volunteering hours for older students, and the Lithuanian "socialinė-pilietinė veikla" (mandatory civic hours) [unverified exact requirement; check]. Every school has it, which makes adoption free.

**Prior-art check.**
- Searched: "aktyvios pertraukos pradinukams vyresni mokiniai organizuoja žaidimus programėlė".
- **No Lithuanian app found.** Results were foreign active-recess programmes (UK, Singapore) and a Lithuanian kindergarten active-games paper ([lituanistika](https://www.lituanistika.lt/content/99011)).
- Active-break videos (GoNoodle-type [unverified]) and Wordwall "brain breaks" exist ([wordwall](https://wordwall.net/sl/resource/100968926/brain-breaks)). The teen-led + AI-adapted + bilingual angle looks open.
- **Clone risk:** medium-low vs the 2025 "movement apps". This is not a points app; it's a teen-led play tool.

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"Why do you need AI? Print a book of games."* → The book doesn't know that today it's 14 kids in a corridor with one ball, a wheelchair and three kids who don't speak Lithuanian. The AI adapts it, and it translates and illustrates in seconds. The seed library is a book; the AI makes it usable on the spot by a 15-year-old.
2. *"Will teens keep showing up?"* → It counts toward civic-activity hours [to verify]. Captains rotate weekly; our sign-up sheet by Nov 5 is the evidence.

## Top 4: Rūšiuoklis Rudis: a talking sorting bin for grades 1–4 (Sustainability) — 14/16

**Problem.** Classroom sorting bins in primary grades are contaminated: yoghurt cups with food, juice boxes in "paper", wrappers everywhere. Kids don't know the rules, and nobody gives feedback at the moment of throwing. LT already proved habits change with the right mechanism: deposit return ~90% [verified, `../local-data.md`].

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. A cardboard bin set with a phone or ESP32-cam above it, and a cartoon fox, "Rudis".
2. A 7-year-old (or a juror) holds up a juice box. Rudis: "Ooo, tetrapakas! Į geltoną, bet pirma išgerk iki galo!" ("A juice carton! Yellow bin, but drink it all first!")
3. The juror tries to trick it with a greasy pizza box, and Rudis explains why it goes in mixed waste.
4. The class dashboard: "Wrong items in our bins: Monday 11 of 40, Thursday 3 of 38." It also lists the top 3 confusing items for the teacher to explain.

**What the AI does.**
- Vision classification of the item: a fine-tuned small model on-device, or a vision LLM with LT-municipality sorting rules in the prompt.
- Kid-level explanations in the local rules, with a voice character.
- Mistake logging gives each class its "most-confused items", which feed a 5-minute lesson.

**Build in 2 days.**
- Phone or tablet in a holder + a web app: camera → vision LLM → TTS. Optionally an ESP32-CAM + speaker if the lab has them (IoT bonus).
- Cardboard bin set and fox mascot (art class).
- Day 1: classifier prompt + rules for our municipality + voice. Day 2: logging + dashboard + mascot.

**Class test by Nov 5.**
- Nov 2–3: audit one grade 1–4 classroom's bins (count wrong items per bin). This is the baseline, with gloves.
- Nov 3–5: Rudis in the classroom, then audit again.
- Teacher quote, and kids' drawings of Rudis (emotional pitch material).

**Impact number.** **Contamination rate of the class's sorting bins (%), before vs after.**

**LT hook.**
- Deposit-system success [verified].
- Textile and sorting obligations [verified, `../local-data.md`].
- **Channel:** the regional waste-management centres (RATC) run school education programmes [unverified, check], and municipalities.

**Prior-art check: NOT DONE (search budget spent).**
- This is the weakest of the four on F3: "AI photo → which bin" apps and smart bins exist internationally [unverified names].
- **Must run before choosing:** "išmanioji rūšiavimo šiukšliadėžė mokykla", "rūšiavimo programėlė dirbtinis intelektas Lietuva", "mokiniai sukūrė rūšiavimo".
- The differentiators would be the kid-facing voice character + class contamination measurement + local municipal rules, but the jury may still see it as a tech wrapper (the brief warns against "photo of X").

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"There are already apps that tell you which bin."* → For adults with phones. A 7-year-old has no phone. Rudis lives at the bin, talks, and the class gets a number. We measured the bins, not downloads.
2. *"Novelty wears off after a week."* → Rudis's lines rotate, and the class competes on the weekly contamination %. Honest answer: we only have 3 days of data. The plan is a 4-week pilot.
