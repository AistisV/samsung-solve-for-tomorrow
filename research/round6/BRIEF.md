# Round 6 brief: student-centric, simple, shippable by Nov 6

## The realisation
The last three Baltic Solve for Tomorrow winners (see `../past-winners.md`) were **apps solving everyday student-life problems**, not technical marvels:
- **2025, sport:** 1st **Kineto (LV)**: app that reads young people's calendars, finds free slots for movement, suggests training, shows bookable facilities and local groups. 3rd **Orientr (EE)**: AI-generated virtual orienteering routes, won on "a ready prototype and a well-thought-out development plan". A Vilnius Lyceum 11th-grade team won a special Samsung prize.
- **2024, AI in learning:** 1st **Vilnius Lyceum**: AI app that adapts to your learning style. 2nd **LaKiKe (EE)**: QR codes on real library books → AI animated teaser + points + quiz, for grades 1–3.
- **2023, bullying/well-being:** 1st **BeePeace (EE)**: app to anonymously report bullying. 2nd **Eduwave (Vilnius Lyceum)**: game-style learning website.
- **2022, sustainable living:** **Minova (Klaipėda)**: AI demand forecasting to cut restaurant food waste.

**Common pattern:** (1) a problem every juror instantly gets; (2) the users are the students themselves or kids, so the team owns the problem; (3) a working prototype, usually an app; (4) a clear path to users, usually through schools. Hardware and big-institution ideas (moose sensors, lake-ice stakes) are more ambitious than any recent Baltic winner, but further from students' lives.

## This year
Theme: **Sport & tech** (make sport accessible/educational/meaningful; get students active) **or Sustainability** (tech for a greener tomorrow / living sustainably). Bonus for AI/IoT/VR/AR. Official criteria: see `../official-criteria-scoring.md` (§38 stage 2: technology, creativity + research into prior efforts, realism, local relevance, completeness/iteration, wider impact/policy; §48 final).

## HARD reality: today is Nov 2 2026, the concept + visual presentation is due **Nov 6**
- The prototype must be buildable in **~2 days** by teens who code: a web/mobile app using an AI API or on-device model, or hardware only from parts a school lab already has.
- Traction must be gettable by **Nov 5**: a survey of classmates (50–200), a test in one class or club, a quote from a teacher or organisation.

## What the team rejected (don't re-propose)
Classroom CO₂ meters, CPR trainers, school kit lockers, gamified step/points apps, AR city quests, screen-time-for-steps, tripod AI form-checkers, "a map of X" without a daily user, tech wrappers (photo of a test strip). Also, already explored: moose detection (LAUGAS exists in LT), lake ice stakes, shower meters, hot-water building diagnosis, a blind-sport audio aid.

## Also avoid copying last season
The 2025 sport season was full of movement apps (Kineto, Orientr, city-quest, screen-time, mental-health + movement). A sport idea must be clearly different. A **sustainability** idea from students' own daily lives may stand out more.

## Filters for round 6 (0–2 each, /16)
1. **We feel it**: students (or their younger siblings, classmates, school) live this problem weekly.
2. **Juror gets it in 10 s.**
3. **Not a clone** of a past Baltic winner/finalist or a common app (check!).
4. **Prototype in 2 days** that actually works.
5. **AI is useful**, not decoration.
6. **Traction by Nov 5** (survey, class test, quote).
7. **Reach**: obvious channel (schools, e-dienynas/Tamo, NVŠ, a municipality, an existing org).
8. **Measurable impact**: one number that changes.
