## Top 4 profiles

### 1. Pamestinukai: the AI lost-and-found that empties itself into reuse (15/16, Sustainability)
**Shape:** LaKiKe (a physical pile of real objects → AI) × Minova (waste cut for one school).

**Pitch:** "Every school has a mountain of jackets, hats and sports shoes nobody claims, and in June it goes in the bin. We photograph it once; AI turns it into a searchable catalogue parents reach through Tamo. Whatever is still there after 30 days goes to our swap rail or the textile container, and we weigh every kilo."

**2-minute demo.**
1. The stage has a real box of 15 items from our school's lost-and-found.
2. A teammate photographs a hoodie. In 3 s the card appears: "Pilkas džemperis su gobtuvu, Nike, ~146 dydis, rastas sporto salėje, lapkričio 3."
3. A juror plays a parent and types "sūnus pametė pilką bliuzoną su kapišonu". The hoodie is the top match, even though no word is the same.
4. The dashboard shows: our school's shelf, N items catalogued, X returned this week, Y kg on the swap rail, Z kg to textile collection.

**What the AI actually does.**
- A vision-language model (e.g. Gemini/GPT-4o-mini/Claude via API) turns each photo into structured fields (type, colour, brand, size label if visible, distinguishing marks) and a Lithuanian description.
- Text embeddings match a parent's free-text description against the catalogue across synonyms (bliuzonas/džemperis/megztinis, kapišonas/gobtuvas).
- When a parent writes "lost", the request is stored, and every new item is checked against open requests, so a match fires automatically.
- Useful, not decoration: nobody catalogues 200 items by hand, and keyword search fails on Lithuanian synonyms and inflection.

**How to build it in 2 days (stack).**
- Next.js or plain HTML + Firebase/Supabase (images + table). One `/catalogue` page per school, opened by a link sent through Tamo, with no login.
- One serverless function calls the vision model and returns JSON. Embeddings are stored in Supabase pgvector, or a simple cosine search runs in JS for fewer than 500 items.
- Day 1: upload → AI card → list. Day 2: search plus "lost" requests, match notification by email, the 30-day status (rail/textile) and a kg field.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- Ask the deputy director or caretaker to let us photograph the *whole* current lost-and-found today. Item count and weight on day 1 are hard, self-generated data.
- A Google Forms survey of 100–200 students and parents: "Have you lost something at school this year? Did you get it back? How much did it cost?"
- The class teacher of one class sends the catalogue link to parents via Tamo, and we count claims by Nov 5.
- Get a quote from the caretaker or director.

**Impact number:** % of found items returned to their owner (baseline: our survey vs. pilot), plus **kg of clothing diverted from the bin per school per year**, measured by weighing.

**LT hook.**
- Separate textile collection has been mandatory since 2025-01-01, and the containers are often contaminated [prev-verified: AM, `local-data.md`]. Our end stream is *sorted, clean, labelled* textiles, or reuse first.
- Tamo/e-dienynas reaches every parent, so there is no app to install.
- The student council runs the swap rail.

**Prior-art check.**
- **Abroad, it exists:**
  - **FindIt**, built by a 15-year-old and deployed at Odle Middle School, Bellevue. Staff photograph items and students search by description [verified: [GeekWire 2025](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/teen-finds-a-way-to-simplify-lost-and-found-with-an-app-that-uses-ai-in-the-search-process/)].
  - **LostiFind**, which does AI recognition and parent matching; its App Store page reports a DNS outage [verified: [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/lostifind/id6745828727)].
  - Commercial **FoundFastApp**, used by K-12 schools and venues [verified: [Capterra](https://www.capterra.ae/software/1096302/FoundFastApp)].
  - Hackathon versions (MavFind) [verified: [Devpost](https://kelaenderkoe.pages.dev/linux-https-devpost.com/software/mavfind)].
- US schools donate unclaimed piles. One district reported more than 2,000 lb washed and donated [verified: [KXXV](https://www.kxxv.com/story/38376255/lost-and-found-items-washed-donated-to-communities-in-schools), [EdWeek](https://edweek.org/leadership/what-happens-to-the-lost-and-found-mound-at-the-end-of-the-year/2024/05)].
- **Lithuania:** 2 Lithuanian searches found **no school lost-and-found app or programme** [unverified absence; the search results were only uniform rules and unrelated pages].
- **Our difference:** the US tools stop at "return". We add the **sustainability loop** (a 30-day rule → reuse rail → textile container, kg logged), the **Tamo channel** and **Lithuanian-language matching**.

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"An American teen already built this."* Yes, and that proves the need. Theirs returns items. Ours measures and closes the waste loop, works in Lithuanian, and goes through Tamo, which every LT parent already has. We cite FindIt in our research section (criterion 38: research into prior efforts).
2. *"Is it really sustainability, or just convenience?"* Every returned jacket is a jacket not re-bought, and every unclaimed one goes to reuse instead of the general bin. We show the kg we weighed at our own school, not an estimate.

### 2. Gedimų medžiotojai ("Varva"): anonymous student spotters for school waste (15/16, Sustainability)
**Shape:** BeePeace (an anonymous channel for what adults don't see) moved to resource waste. Minova's € framing is added.

**Pitch:** "Teachers don't use the student toilets at break. Students do, and we all know which one has been running for a month. One anonymous photo, and AI tells the caretaker what it is, where it is and how many euros a year it's wasting."

**2-minute demo.**
1. Live, a juror photographs a running tap set up on stage (or a lit, empty room on a slide).
2. The app returns: "Category: water leak, continuous stream. Estimated loss: ~X L/day ≈ €Y/year (from a measured flow rate). Room: 2nd-floor girls' WC. 3 earlier reports merged."
3. Switch to the caretaker's view: a ranked list by €/year, and a "fixed" button with a before/after photo.
4. The public board ends the demo: "This month students found €___/year of waste, and ___ items were fixed."

**What the AI actually does.**
- A vision model classifies the photo: leak, running toilet, lights on in an empty room, window open with the heating on, broken radiator valve, PC lab left on, or "not waste / joke". It also filters abuse, including faces and people. A privacy filter refuses photos with people in them.
- Duplicates are merged by room plus category plus image similarity, so 20 reports of the same toilet become one ticket with weight 20.
- A loss estimator applies a small rule table (flow rate from a short video's stream thickness, or a "fills a 1 L bottle in N s" button, × the water tariff; lamp count × W × hours) and turns each item into €/year, so the caretaker fixes the expensive things first.
- Honest note: the AI here is "useful triage", not a forecast. The value is in turning noise into a ranked work queue.

**How to build it in 2 days.**
- A PWA (React/Vite) with a QR code on each floor's noticeboard that pre-fills the room, and no login, so it stays anonymous.
- Supabase stores the reports. One function calls the vision model and returns JSON. A tariff table holds the water price from the local supplier and the electricity price.
- Day 1: report, classification, list. Day 2: deduplication, the € estimate, the caretaker view, the public board.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- Put QR posters on 3 floors for 2–3 days and count reports.
- Do a "waste walk" with the caretaker to validate the AI's categories and to time fixes.
- Survey: "Do you know of something broken or wasteful at school that nobody fixes?" (n 100–200).
- Get a quote from the director or caretaker, and ideally from the school's Eco-Schools coordinator.

**Impact number:** **€/year of verified waste fixed per school**, measured as flow rate × tariff for every fixed leak. A secondary number is the median days from report to fix.

**LT hook.**
- Cities already use the same mechanism. **"Tvarkau miestą"** lets residents report city problems to the municipality [verified: [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/eismas/7/1781165/programele-tvarkau-miesta-glumina-pilietiskus-vilniecius-ar-ju-duomenys-gali-nukeliauti-pazeidejui), [Civic Tech Guide](https://directory.civictech.guide/listing/tvarkau-miesta)]. We bring it **inside the school**, where the city app doesn't reach.
- Anonymity: LT schools already accept anonymous student reporting through **"Patyčių dėžutė"** (Kurk Lietuvai), whose pilot had 1,000+ students and 89 reports [verified: [Kurk Lietuvai PDF](https://data.kurklt.lt/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Patyciu-dezutes-pristatymas-LRVK.pdf)].
- Channel: the Eco-Schools / Žalioji vėliava programme (energy reduction is one of its work areas) [verified: [paninfo.lt](https://paninfo.lt/panevezio-svietimo-istaigoms-zaliosios-veliavos/?pdf=60625)]. Also **"Tvari mokykla 2030"**, which lists 87 schools at stage II and 12 at stage III [verified: [vda.lrv.lt PDF snippet](https://vda.lrv.lt/public/canonical/1773819400/2273/Duomenai.pdf)].
- The municipality pays the utility bills [unverified per school; typical for municipal schools].

**Prior-art check.**
- Lithuanian searches found "Tvarkau miestą" (city level, municipal) and nothing school-level [unverified absence: 2 searches].
- Abroad: generic FixMyStreet-type tools [unverified] and facility-ticket software for staff [unverified]. We found no *student-anonymous, waste-€-ranked* school tool.
- **Our difference:** inside the school, run by students, anonymous, and every ticket priced in €.

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Students will spam jokes."* The vision filter rejects non-waste photos and photos with people. Deduplication turns mass reports into one weighted ticket. The pilot reports the spam rate honestly.
2. *"The caretaker can just walk around."* They do, but not into student toilets at break, and not at 16:30 when the PC lab is left on. Our pilot shows how many items students found that weren't already known. That count is the proof.

### 3. Kodėl ne KK? (why not PE?): the anonymous channel for why students skip PE (14/16, Sport & tech)
**Shape:** BeePeace (an anonymous channel) + Vilnius Lyceum (AI adapts to the individual). We deliberately avoid *bullying*: Patyčių dėžutė already covers that in LT [verified: [Kurk Lietuvai](https://data.kurklt.lt/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Patyciu-dezutes-pristatymas-LRVK.pdf)].

**Pitch:** "Half the class has a note to skip PE, and the teacher thinks it's laziness. It's the changing room, the shower nobody uses, the fear of running in front of boys, the grade. We built an anonymous channel that tells the school *why*, and gives every student a version of PE they'll actually do."

**2-minute demo.**
1. A student opens a QR link: "What stops you from enjoying PE?" They answer in free text or by voice, anonymously.
2. Live on screen, the AI clusters 150 real answers from our survey into themes with counts and quotes, for example "Persirengimas / not enough time: 38%", in our own data.
3. The AI drafts 3 concrete, cheap fixes for the PE department, for example "allow PE kit all day on PE days", "+5 min changing time" or "choice-based last 15 min", each linked to the cluster it addresses.
4. Personal side: a student in the preparatory group enters their limits (from the medical form category) and their likes. The AI generates a 4-week adapted plan, and the student logs a progress check against themselves (the SkillFit idea, as a supporting feature only).

**What the AI actually does.**
- An LLM does topic clustering and sentiment on free text in Lithuanian, and the counts come out by grade and gender.
- It extracts representative anonymised quotes and removes names and identifying details (PII scrub).
- It generates and ranks fixes from a curated list of interventions; the list is the team's research, the AI matches.
- It generates individual adapted plans within explicit limits (a rule list of exercises excluded per medical group).
- Useful: a PE department will never hand-code 300 open answers, and that is why nobody knows the real reasons.

**How to build it in 2 days.** A Google Form or a custom PWA collects the answers. A Python/Streamlit or Next.js dashboard sends batches of answers to an LLM with a fixed theme schema, re-runs, and shows the counts. The plan generator is a second prompt with a JSON exercise library. Day 1 is the survey and the clustering; day 2 is the dashboard, the fix ranking and the plan generator.

**Traction by Nov 5.** **This is the strongest of the four. The survey *is* the product.** Run it in 3–6 classes (n 100–200) on Nov 3–4. Show the AI clusters to the PE teacher and the deputy director, and get their quote plus one fix they agree to trial. Count the students currently exempted or in the preparatory group (anonymous aggregate from the school).

**Impact number:** the number of PE lessons skipped or "note" exemptions per class per month, before vs. after the first fix. That is slow, so for Nov 6, report **the % of students for whom the top barrier is fixable by the school at no cost**, a number from our own data.

**LT hook / evidence.**
- PE-exemption abuse is documented: doctors report kids asking for preparatory-group notes so they "don't have to run" [prev-verified: tv3.lt, ve.lt in `local-data.md`]. Only ~5% of LT teens get enough activity [prev-verified: HBSC via MadeinVilnius].
- A Lithuanian survey (n=150) found that **the first reason for negative attitudes to PE is not liking to change clothes** [verified: [VU epublications](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:1968295/index.html)]. In Ireland, 54.5% of 1,439 students named the uniform/clothing as a PE barrier [verified: [Univ. of Limerick](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/let-us-wear-our-own-goddamn-clothes-students-perceptions-of-schoo/)]. Danish and Swiss reports say young people avoid shared showers, including from fear of phone cameras [verified: [bluewin.ch](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/switzerland/showering-naked-better-not-young-people-are-increasingly-avoiding-sports-changing-rooms-2739905.html)].
- Channel: the PE department and the student council. It scales through municipal education departments and the NŠA.

**Prior-art check.** Lithuanian searches found Patyčių dėžutė (bullying) only, and no tool aimed at PE barriers [unverified absence: 2 searches]. SkillFit (DE) covers progress grading but not *why students avoid PE*. **Our difference:** it targets avoidance, the step before any form-checker or grading app matters.

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Isn't this just a survey with ChatGPT?"* Every year it's a survey nobody reads. Ours is continuous, stays anonymous, reads free text in Lithuanian at scale, and links each theme to a fix. It also gives the excluded preparatory-group student a personal plan. Our pilot shows the clusters the PE teacher hadn't expected.
2. *"Anonymous channels attract abuse, and teachers will feel attacked."* There are no teacher names: the PII scrub removes them, and the output is themes and counts, not complaints. The PE teacher co-designed the questions, and we show their quote.

### 4. Išaugau (I outgrew it): the school swap that knows next season's size (13/16, Sport + Sustainability bridge)
**Shape:** Minova (a forecast that cuts waste) + Liova (circular objects) + the student council as the channel.

**Pitch:** "Every winter parents buy new skates and ski boots that a kid will outgrow in one season, while last year's pair sits in a cupboard two classrooms away. Our school swap shelf uses AI to predict each child's size next season, so the right pair finds them *before* the shop does."

**2-minute demo.**
1. Photograph a pair of kids' skates: the AI reads the size from the label or the sole, sets the category and grades the condition.
2. A parent profile: child age 9, size 33 now, measured in March. The AI predicts "35 by January" and shows the 2 matching pairs on the shelf.
3. The counter: "pairs re-homed this season: N, new purchases avoided: N, € saved for families: sum of the new-price estimates".

**What the AI does.**
- A vision model extracts the size, brand and condition from photos.
- A growth-based size predictor, built on paediatric foot-growth curves [unverified: need a source, or the team's own data from the swap's past sizes], forecasts the size at the start of the season.
- Matching. Honest: **the AI is the weakest of the 4.** The predictor is the one non-trivial part.

**Build.** The same stack as #1 (they share 80% of the code: photo → card → match), so these two could merge into one "Mokyklos antras ratas" (the school's second round) platform.

**Traction.** One collection day before winter (skates, skis, helmets, boots) at our school or one NVŠ sport club. Count items and matches, and survey parents on what they spend on outgrown gear.

**Impact:** the number of pairs re-homed and € families saved (from each item's new price, which the team looks up).

**LT hook.** Winter sport kit is seasonal and expensive for families. The NVŠ voucher pays for the club, not for the kit [unverified: check whether NVŠ funds can cover equipment]. Natural partners are NVŠ sport clubs and the student council.

**Prior-art check.** The Lithuanian search found only classifieds on skelbiu.lt, for example used kids' ski boots at €5 and rental of kids' ski sets at €40 per 7–10 days [verified: [skelbiu.lt](https://www.skelbiu.lt/paieska/slides-vaikams/)]. **No school or club swap initiative was found** [unverified absence: 1 search]. Vinted and Facebook groups are the obvious competitors [unverified, but well known].

**Top 2 jury objections.**
1. *"Vinted exists."* Vinted is strangers, shipping and haggling. This is your own school, free, matched to size *before* the season, and run by the student council. Our count of re-homed pairs shows whether people prefer it.
2. *"Where is the AI?"* Fair. The size forecast is the core. Without it, the swap shelf is just a shelf. If the jury needs stronger AI, merge it with #1, where the vision catalogue plus Lithuanian semantic matching carries it.

---

## Summary: top 4
1. **Pamestinukai (15/16, Sus):** AI catalogues the school's lost-and-found from photos, parents search in plain Lithuanian via a Tamo link, and unclaimed items go to reuse or textile with kg weighed. Prior art exists in the US (teen-built FindIt, LostiFind, FoundFastApp) but stops at "return". None was found in LT. Our twist is the waste loop. Instantly relatable, buildable in 2 days, and the data (count and weigh the pile) can be collected tomorrow.
2. **Gedimų medžiotojai / Varva (15/16, Sus):** BeePeace-style anonymous student reports of building waste (running toilets, lights, open windows). AI triages them, merges duplicates and prices each in €/year for the caretaker. Its city-level analogue is "Tvarkau miestą". Nothing school-level was found. It can go through Eco-Schools and "Tvari mokykla 2030".
3. **Kodėl ne KK? (14/16, Sport):** an anonymous channel on why students fake PE exemptions. AI clusters the free text into themes plus no-cost fixes, and gives preparatory-group students an adapted plan. It is backed by an LT study where changing clothes was the first reason for negative PE attitudes. The best traction (the survey is the product) and clearly not another movement app. Avoid "bullying" framing, because Patyčių dėžutė exists.
4. **Išaugau (13/16, Sport + Sus):** a school/club swap of outgrown winter sports gear with AI next-season size prediction. Only classifieds were found in LT. Its AI is weakest, so the best use is **to merge it with #1** into one "school second-life" platform.

**Recommendation:** #1 and #2 are the safest sustainability picks, and #3 is the strongest sport pick with traction. Before committing to #1, also search Estonian/Latvian school news for "lost and found app" to make sure no Baltic finalist did it [not checked: the budget is spent].
