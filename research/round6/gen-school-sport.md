# Round 6 generator: SPORT & TECH from students' own lives (not another movement app)

Lens: what actually **stops** Lithuanian teens from doing sport, or makes it worse: PE lessons, exemptions, being picked last, club cost, quitting at 14–16, coaches with no time, injuries, school leagues, younger siblings. The rule was to stay clearly away from last season's "app that makes you move" family (Kineto calendar slots, Orientr routes, city quests, screen-time-for-steps, mental health + movement) and from round-4/5 ideas the team already saw.

Search budget: 8/8 WebSearch calls used, 7 of them in Lithuanian. Tags: **[verified: source]** means I saw it in a search result this session; **[unverified]** means my own knowledge or a guess. No statistics are invented. Where a number is needed, the pitch says "measure it in our school by Nov 5".

Filters (0–2 each): **F1** we feel it · **F2** juror gets it in 10 s · **F3** not a clone · **F4** 2-day prototype · **F5** AI is useful · **F6** traction by Nov 5 · **F7** reach channel · **F8** measurable impact.

## 2. Top 4 profiles

### ⭐ 1. Suolelis ("the bench"): nobody sits out PE

**The problem.** Every PE class has 2–5 kids who are not doing the lesson. Some have a medical exemption or a special-group assignment. Others have a sprained ankle, are back after the flu, are on their period, or "forgot their kit". They sit on the bench, scroll, or are sent to the library.
- Lithuanian students are assigned to PE health groups by the family doctor at the yearly health check. Children with a lasting or temporary condition go to the **special PE group (SFUG)**, mostly for vision, heart and circulation, musculoskeletal problems, asthma or diabetes. One study of 308 students in grades 5–12 at 34 schools found SFUG students are **1–3% of a school** [verified: VU thesis, epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:1796333]. That share excludes the much larger group of temporary and informal sit-outs. We count those ourselves.
- Exemption is a director's order based on a parents' request plus a doctor's certificate. Schools differ on whether the exempt student must stay in the lesson. Kaunas Suzuki progymnasium requires attendance unless it is the first or last lesson of the day [verified: suzuki.kaunas.lm.lt exemption procedure; VU gymnasium procedure].
- Doctors report kids asking for preparatory-group certificates so they "don't have to run or do push-ups" [verified earlier: tv3.lt / ve.lt, in `../local-data.md`].
- Research notes that PE teachers are often under-prepared to work with SEN students, and that standard activity forms don't always work for them [verified: VU theses in search results].

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. The teacher's screen shows today's plan: "Grade 9b, volleyball: warm-up, serving drill, 3v3 games."
2. Three students tap their status on a phone: "Asthma, cold air today", "Sprained right ankle, week 2", "Period, low energy".
3. In about 5 seconds each one gets a personal plan. The asthma student does a longer indoor warm-up, then serves from a closer line with a rest every 4 minutes. The ankle student gets seated serving and passing against the wall, then becomes 3v3 scorekeeper and referee with a rules card. The period student gets a lighter version of each block and keeps the option to switch.
4. The teacher's dashboard: "27/27 participating, 3 adapted." A weekly view shows each student's active minutes. Demo line: "Last month in our class: 41 bench-lessons. With Suolelis: 0."

**What the AI actually does.**
- Combines the teacher's lesson plan with each student's limitation and builds an adapted version from a curated, teacher-approved exercise library. It uses an LLM with a structured prompt and a safety rule list ("ankle: no jumping or cutting; asthma: warm-up ≥10 min, avoid cold-air sprints").
- Picks a meaningful **role** when moving is not possible (referee, scorer, video-feedback filming, warm-up leader), so the student stays part of the class.
- Learns over time which adapted tasks a student actually did, and offers more of those.
- The safety rule is that the AI never overrides a doctor's restriction. It only chooses inside the allowed list, and the teacher approves with one tap.

**How to build it in 2 days.**
- Stack: Next.js or plain React web app (PWA on phones), Supabase for auth and data, Claude or GPT API for plan generation with JSON output.
- Data: a static `exercises.json` (~60 exercises tagged by body load, impact, intensity and equipment) and `restrictions.json` (~15 common limitations mapped to forbidden tags). The AI picks and adapts from these and doesn't invent exercises, which also answers the safety question.
- Day 1: data files, prompt, teacher plan-entry screen. Day 2: student status screen, teacher dashboard, one real lesson test.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- **Bench count:** have one student in each of 5–10 classes tally how many classmates sat out each PE lesson this week. That gives the school's own number.
- **Survey (target 100+ students):** "Have you sat out PE this year? What did you do instead? Would you rather do an adapted task?"
- **Live test:** one PE lesson with our school's PE teacher, plus a quote from them and ideally from the school nurse (visuomenės sveikatos specialistas).

**Impact number:** bench minutes per class per week. Also the % of sit-out students who are active for ≥20 minutes of the lesson.

**LT hook:** Lithuanian youth are among the least active in Europe [verified earlier: `../local-data.md`, HBSC]. PE is the one place every child is reached. The SFUG / preparatory group system and per-school exemption procedures are Lithuanian specifics. Scale channel: the school PE teachers' methodical groups, NŠA, the municipality public-health bureau (which employs school health specialists), and later e-dienynas/Tamo integration.

**Prior-art check:**
- Lithuanian searches for an adapted-PE app for students with health conditions found **no app**, only theses and the national PE curriculum [searches: "pritaikytas fizinis ugdymas programėlė…", "atleisti nuo kūno kultūros…"].
- Abroad: adapted-PE lesson resources and teacher guides exist, and UK campaigns (Women in Sport) push for period-friendly PE [unverified details]. I did not find a student-facing AI that adapts the actual day's lesson, but the English-language search was not run, so the team should do one quick English check.
- Links: [VU thesis on SFUG](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:1796333/1796333.pdf), [Suzuki progymnasium exemption procedure](https://suzuki.kaunas.lm.lt/files/atleidimo%20nuo%20kuno%20kulturos,%20dailes,%20sokio%20ir%20kuno%20kulturos%20tvarkos%20aprasas.pdf), [VU gymnasium procedure](https://www2019.vu.lt/upload/MOKINI_ATLEIDIMO_NUO_MEN_FIZINIO_UGDYMO_IR_KIT_PRIVALOMJ_DALYK_PAMOK_TVARKOS_APRAAS.pdf), [Kaunas study on PE assessment](https://www.lituanistika.lt/content/83358).

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"Is an AI giving exercise advice to sick kids safe?"* The AI only chooses from a teacher-curated library inside the limits set by the doctor's group. The teacher approves each plan with one tap, and "no movement" maps to roles, not exercises. It is safer than today, where nobody adapts anything.
2. *"Kids will just use it to skip more."* It works the other way. Today "I have a note" means 45 minutes off. With Suolelis, a note means an adapted task, so fake notes lose their point. Teachers also see the weekly pattern.

### ⭐ 2. Jaunasis treneris ("young coach"): teens run sport for the little ones

**The problem.** Younger kids (our siblings, grades 1–4) have long recesses and after-school hours with no organised play, and schools don't have enough coaches. Many older students would happily run games but don't know how to plan a session for 15 eight-year-olds. Many also need volunteering hours [unverified: LT schools' social-civic activity requirement; check the school's ugdymo planas]. The first session goes badly and they stop.

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. A grade-10 leader enters: "18 kids, grade 2, small gym, 30 min, 4 balls + cones, they were wild last time".
2. AI returns a timed plan: a 5-minute tag warm-up, a 10-minute relay with 4 teams, a 10-minute "ball guardians" game, a 5-minute calm-down. Each game comes with a 2-line explanation and **what to say** to start it. There is a "too wild" fallback.
3. After the session the leader taps 3 buttons (fun 1–5, chaos 1–5, which game flopped). The next plan adapts: "drop the relay, add more chasing games, shorter explanations".
4. The coordinator dashboard shows 6 leaders, 4 sessions this week, 94 child-sessions, 31 volunteer hours logged.

**What the AI does.** It generates age-appropriate, space- and kit-aware session plans in Lithuanian from a game library, and adapts the next plan from feedback. It coaches the teen leader with short scripts for starting, splitting teams and handling a crying kid. Without the AI, a teen has to search YouTube and improvise.

**Build in 2 days.**
- React/PWA + Supabase + LLM API (JSON plan schema) + a `games.json` library of ~40 games (age, space, kit, intensity). Volunteer-hour log and a printable plan card.
- Day 1: plan generator. Day 2: feedback loop, dashboard, a real session.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- Run **2–3 real recess sessions** with grade 1–3 classes using generated plans. Photos and the primary teachers' consent are needed.
- Recruit leaders: "how many grade 9–12 students would lead one session a week?" (survey 100+).
- Quote from a primary teacher, the PE teacher and the deputy head responsible for volunteering.

**Impact number:** active child-minutes per week from teen-led sessions. Also the number of teen leaders still active after 4 weeks.

**LT hook:** the activity crisis starts young; only 13.6% of children are active ≥60 min after school [verified earlier: SAM, `../local-data.md`]. Schools want both volunteering and more PA. Channels: schools (one gymnasium + its progymnasium), municipal sports schools, the NVŠ system, Lithuanian School Sports Association-type bodies [unverified name].

**Prior-art check:**
- Lithuanian search for older students leading sport for little ones as social-civic activity found **no programme or app**, only general volunteering pages (Panevėžys Vyturio progymnasium is an EU-accredited volunteering school) [search: "vyresni mokiniai veda sporto užsiėmimus pradinukams…"].
- Abroad, the model is established: Scottish **Active Schools** and Sports Leaders UK train young leaders [verified that the Active Schools page exists: glowscotland; Sports Leaders UK unverified]. That supports the idea; we bring it to LT with an AI session planner.
- Links: [Glow Scotland Active Schools](https://blogs.glowscotland.org.uk/sa/monktonpsandeyc/pupils/active-schools/), [EU youth volunteering, Vyturio progimnazija](https://youth.europa.eu/volunteering/organisation/74966_lt).

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"Teenagers supervising small kids: who is responsible?"* Sessions run inside the school during recess or after-school time, with a teacher on duty in the room. Leaders get a 1-page safety checklist in the app. This is what the school's own supervision rules already require.
2. *"ChatGPT can already write a game plan."* A blank chat gives a generic plan. Ours uses the actual group, space and kit, remembers what flopped last time, and logs hours and participation for the school. The value is the loop and the school system around it, not one plan.

### ⭐ 3. Trenerio balsas ("coach's voice"): personal feedback from a coach with no time (+ quit-risk radar)

**The problem.** A youth coach has 20–30 kids, 90 minutes and no time for notes. Most kids hear personal feedback rarely; parents hear nothing until something goes wrong. At 14–16, kids, especially girls, quietly drift away and the coach notices too late. Research hits confirm 14–16-year-olds are among the least active and girls are less active than boys [verified: LSU / VU paper summaries in the search results]. Lithuanian research also stresses the coach–parent–teen relationship as key to staying in sport [verified: VDU theses]. I found **no Lithuanian data on the exact quitting age** [unverified], so measure it in our own club with a survey.

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. The coach walks off the court and records a 60-second memo in Lithuanian: "Good session. Ugnė's left layups finally clean. Matas slacking on defence again. Emilija not here, third time. Need to work on boxing out."
2. The app shows 3 personal kid notes in a warm tone (Ugnė: "your left-hand layups were the best today, keep it up"), a team summary for parents, attendance updated, and an alert: "Emilija: missed 3 of the last 4, and her check-in mood dropped. Suggested message: …".
3. Kids see their own feed of "what coach said about me" over the season.

**What the AI does.** Lithuanian speech-to-text, then an LLM extracts per-player observations and attendance. It rewrites them as encouraging, specific feedback (a coaching-style rule: name the behaviour, not the person). A simple quit-risk score combines attendance trend, a monthly 3-emoji check-in and the coach's mentions. Remove the AI and the coach would have to type 25 messages.

**Build in 2 days.**
- PWA + Supabase.
- Speech-to-text: Whisper API (supports Lithuanian) or the browser Web Speech API with `lt-LT` [unverified quality, test day 1].
- An LLM with a JSON schema {player, observation, tone, action}; the roster is matched by first names.
- Risk score: plain rules plus the LLM's explanation.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- 2–3 real coaches from the school's or town's club record memos for 3 sessions.
- Survey club kids (50+): "In the last month, how often did your coach tell you something personal about your play?" Re-ask after the test.
- Coach quote about time saved.

**Impact number:** personal feedback messages per kid per month (baseline from the survey vs after). Long-term: club retention of 14–16-year-olds.

**LT hook:** basketball and club culture are huge, and coaches are overloaded volunteers or low-paid. The NVŠ voucher funds clubs, so retention keeps the voucher money working [NVŠ verified earlier, `../local-data.md`]. Channels: sports schools, federations (LKF), NVŠ providers.

**Prior-art check (the weakest of the 4 on originality):**
- No Lithuanian app found in LT searches. Club-management platforms do cover attendance, schedules and parent messaging: **Sportlyzer** (Estonian, 21,000+ coaches [Estonian origin unverified]), **ProTrainUp** (has a Latvian case study), **KluboS** (QR attendance + parent PWA), **Anolla** (LT-language page) [verified: search results].
- None found that does **voice memo → per-kid feedback + quit-risk**. Still, a juror from Estonia may know Sportlyzer, so pitch it as "a feedback and retention layer", not club management.
- Links: [Sportlyzer](https://sportlyzer.com), [ProTrainUp](https://protrainup.com/en/sports/volleyball), [KluboS (GetApp)](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/klubos/), [Anolla LT](https://anolla.com/lt/komandinio-sporto-programine-iranga), [VDU: coach role in parent–teen interaction](https://www.vdu.lt/cris/entities/publication/5c2e4edf-87a3-4f7b-b38d-f3d6ed91a0b5).

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"Sportlyzer and others already exist."* They manage clubs: payments, schedules, attendance. We fix what they don't touch, which is the kid hearing personal feedback and the coach noticing a quitter. It could even plug into them.
2. *"Privacy: AI notes about minors, sent to parents."* Notes are only positive or actionable by design (the rewrite rule). The coach approves before anything is sent, data stays with the club, and parental consent is collected at signup. This is GDPR-compatible with a simple consent flow.

### ⭐ 4. Pavėžėk ("give a lift"): carpooling to training (Sport × Sustainability)

**The problem.** Every evening, 10 parents from the same team drive 10 cars to the same hall at the same time. The kids feel it too: siblings dragged along, "I can't go to training because mum works late", and kids from car-less families drop the sport. Kids being driven to school has risen +200% in 30 years [verified earlier: Vilnius study, `../local-data.md`]. Training trips are probably similar [unverified].

**The 2-minute demo.**
1. The team page shows 14 families as pins on a map.
2. The AI proposes 4 carpool groups and a 2-week rota that is fair (each driver drives about equally) and respects constraints ("Mon only", "can take 3 kids", "no car").
3. A parent taps "I can't drive Thursday"; the AI re-plans and pings a swap.
4. Counter: "This month: 312 car-km and 54 kg CO₂ saved; 3 kids without a car now get to every practice."

**What the AI does.** It solves the matching and rota as a constraint problem: cluster by route, balance fairness, re-plan on changes. A heuristic solver plus an LLM handles plain-language changes ("Thursday's off for us, but I can do Saturday"). Honest note: the core is optimisation, so the "AI" is solid but not flashy.

**Build in 2 days.**
- PWA + Supabase + Leaflet/OpenStreetMap + OSRM or Google Distance Matrix for route times.
- Clustering (k-means or greedy) and a rota solver (OR-Tools or a simple greedy), plus an LLM for change messages.
- Messenger/Viber share links for notifications.

**Traction by Nov 5.**
- Survey parents of 1–2 school or club teams (team members send the link to their parents' group): "How many km per week? Would you share? Do you know a kid who skips training for lack of a lift?"
- Run one real week's rota for one team.

**Impact number:** car-km and CO₂ saved per team per month, and the number of kids whose attendance rises because they now have a lift.

**LT hook:** car dependence for kids' activities. NVŠ activities often sit across town from home [unverified]. Channels: clubs, sports schools, NVŠ providers, municipality mobility plans (Vilnius already studies kids' mobility).

**Prior-art check:**
- Lithuanian search for parents sharing rides to training and activities found **nothing**. Abroad: a US nonprofit, **Parents in Motion** (Ohio), runs ride-sharing for kids' activities [verified: WMAR2 news]. Family carpool apps like GoKid exist in the US [unverified]. General LT rideshare (e.g. BlaBlaCar-style) isn't team- or child-focused [unverified].
- Link: [WMAR2: kids' ride-sharing program, Ohio](https://www.wmar2news.com/news/national/a-ride-sharing-program-for-children-in-ohio-helps-parents-who-are-constantly-shuttling-kids).

**Top jury objections.**
1. *"Isn't it just a WhatsApp group?"* Parents already try that and it collapses: unfair load, last-minute chaos, nobody counts. The value is the automatic fair rota, instant re-planning and the counted impact for the club and municipality.
2. *"Students aren't the users; parents are."* The kids are the ones who miss training without a lift, and our siblings are the passengers. Also, it is one of few ideas that hits both themes, sport access and greener transport.

## 3. Notes for the team
- **Suolelis is the strongest fit for the round-6 pattern:** every juror sat on a PE bench or watched someone do it, the users are students, it is fully school-channelled, and no LT prior art was found. It is also clearly not a "move more" app; it fixes the PE lesson itself. It merges well with #5 (progress-based grading) and #16 (condition card) if the team wants a bigger "inclusive PE" story.
- Before Nov 5, the team should do **one English prior-art search** for Suolelis ("adapted PE app AI injured students alternative lesson") and for Jaunasis treneris ("young sports leaders app session planner"). My budget ran out before English checks.
- Fact still missing: the national share of PE-exempt students. Only the 1–3% SFUG figure from one study is verified. Use our own bench tally as the headline number.
