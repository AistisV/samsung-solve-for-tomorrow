# Round 5 generator: sport and tech with real edge

**Lens:** things that teens who already play sport would want and use every training session. I looked at what athletes and coaches rig up themselves or pay for (shot tracking, pacing, bar speed, rebound walls, trick logs), then asked whether a cheap, public or shared version could exist for a Lithuanian courtyard, school gym or track.

**Excluded, as the brief rejects them:** tripod AI form checkers, step/points apps, AR quests, screen-time-for-steps, kit lockers, CPR trainers, heart-rate PE. I also left out ideas from earlier rounds: GrowthGuard, AnkleUp, FlashPE pods, ActiveLens, HoopStats, OpenHall, and lights-on-demand.

**Searches used: 4 of 4.** Everything tagged [unverified] comes from background knowledge and must be checked before the jury sees it.

**Scoring:** each filter is scored 0–2, for a total out of 14. The filters are **WANT · WOW · NOT-SEEN · BUILD** (6 weeks) **· AI** (does real work) **· BIG** (scales) **· LT** (hook).

## 2. Top 5 profiles

### ⭐ 1. Kiemo Karalius: every courtyard hoop becomes an arcade machine
**What it physically is:** a sealed, solar-powered box on the back of a street-court backboard.
- A **ToF distance sensor or IR break-beam** looks across the plane just under the ring. A ball passing through gives a ~24 cm occlusion that looks different from swinging chain nets.
- A **backboard IMU and a MEMS microphone** pick up rim, backboard and bank hits.
- An **ESP32-S3** runs a TinyML classifier. The box sends data over NB-IoT/LTE-M or LoRa.
- On the pole: a big arcade **button + NFC/QR tag**. Press it for "30 seconds, max makes", "10 free throws" or "Around the world".

The court then shows up on a city map as *active* or *empty*, with "412 shots in the last hour". No camera means no GDPR fight and nothing that's worth stealing.

**Demo:** A real rim (or a mini hoop) on stage. A jury member presses the button and gets 30 seconds, while the big screen counts makes and shows "rim-out" or "bank" live. The team then opens the **live map of 3 pilot courts in their town** with real weeks of data: "Court A: 4,000+ shots last week, peak Tuesday 19:00. Court B, rebuilt last year with public money: 60 shots." (The numbers here are placeholders to fill in from the pilot.) That second part is the insight wow: *nobody in Lithuania knows which public courts are actually used.*

**What the AI actually does:**
- Classifies make, miss, rim-out, bank-in or airball-ish by fusing ToF occlusion, IMU vibration and audio.
- Filters out chain-net swing, rain and a kid hanging on the rim.
- Segments sessions and estimates the number of players from shot cadence (a 1-player drill looks different from a 3x3 run).
- Detects challenge cheating (a ball held in the ring).
- Server side, forecasts court busyness ("will there be a run at 18:00?").

**Closest existing products:**
- **RIMNET** puts a camera at the hoop base that counts makes and misses, with leaderboards across 11 countries [verified: rimnet.ai].
- **HoopLink** is instrumenting outdoor courts with cameras and sensors in Prince George's County, US [verified: hooplink.ai].
- The **KošKo** IoT paper does CV score tracking with public leaderboards [verified: MDPI Computers 14/11/469].
- **Fullcourt** gives crowd-reported court occupancy [verified: App Store].
- **ShotTracker** (sensor in the ball and net) is for pro and college use [unverified].

**Our difference:** it's camera-free (privacy and vandalism), stays bolted on, runs on solar, and you need no phone or app to play (the button is enough). It costs about €100 per hoop, and it produces **municipal usage data**, which none of the above sells. Honest caveat: the "smart outdoor court" category exists in the US, so we pitch the camera-free, public-infrastructure angle, not "first ever".

**How it scales:** municipalities already maintain hundreds of courtyard courts, so this can be a line item in court renovations. Usage data can decide where the next court or lights go. Leagues can run "court of the month" challenges between towns. It can be packaged later for football goals too (crossbar vibration).

**LT hook:** basketball as religion, with a hoop in almost every Soviet-block courtyard [unverified count]. Lithuania took **men's 3x3 bronze at Paris 2024**, beating Latvia 21–18 [verified: LRT, olympics.com].

**BOM (rough, [unverified] prices):** ESP32-S3 €8, 2× VL53L1X ToF €12, IMU €3, MEMS mic €3, SIM7080G NB-IoT modem €20 + SIM, 10 W solar + 18650 pack + charger €25, IP65 box + stainless brackets €15, vandal button + NFC €10. **About €100 per hoop.**

**Biggest risk:** make/miss accuracy outdoors with chain nets, rain and dunks bending the mount, plus vandalism. Mitigation: start with the free-throw and challenge modes, where the shooter is known and the geometry is predictable. Publish a hand-checked accuracy figure (e.g. 200 filmed shots, filmed by the team only, then deleted).

### ⭐ 2. Siena: the €150 Footbonaut for the garage wall
**What it physically is:** a 3×3 grid of plywood or HDPE target panels, about 1.5 m square in total, screwed to a school or courtyard wall.
- Each panel has **piezo contact sensors** and a **WS2812 LED ring**.
- An ESP32 with a battery or mains supply runs the wall.
- A phone app or a single big button picks a drill.

The wall lights a zone, you pass, kick or throw at it, and it scores hit or miss, **reaction time**, and an estimated **ball speed**. Modes: football one-touch passing, weak-foot only, handball, basketball chest and bounce passes, volleyball setting and serving at a target. Two walls can play head-to-head.

**Demo:** the wall on stage with a soft ball. A jury member gets a 30-second "reaction passing" drill and the screen shows the heat map: "your left-bottom is 40% slower, so the wall will now target it". Then a clip of the pilot wall in the school yard with the leaderboard.

**What the AI actually does:**
- Localises the impact from multi-piezo amplitude and timing patterns (a learned model, since panels couple mechanically and a simple threshold misfires).
- Estimates ball speed from the impact signature, calibrated against phone slow-motion or a radar-gun app.
- A **multi-armed-bandit drill engine** picks the next target to train your weakest zone and foot while keeping the success rate around a "flow" level. Each player's profile follows them via NFC tag or QR.

**Closest existing products:**
- **Footbonaut** (Borussia Dortmund, Hoffenheim) is a cage that fires balls at players and lights a target gate. It is famous, but the price is reported to be in the €1M+ range [unverified].
- Commercial interactive football walls and "smart rebounders" exist (e.g. SKILLCOURT-type systems) [unverified names and prices].

**Our difference:** it's a ~€150 open design that goes on the wall kids already kick balls against, and it is multi-sport.

**How it scales:** as a kit for school yards and club halls. Municipalities could fit one on every courtyard court wall. Clubs can run weekly "wall leagues" between towns.

**LT hook (moderate):** the Soviet-block childhood of kicking the ball against the garage wall ("prie garažų"), rebooted. It also works for basketball passing, so it covers basketball as well as football.

**BOM ([unverified]):** 9× 40 cm panels €40, 18 piezo discs + conditioning €10, ESP32 €8, 9 LED rings €20, 12 V pack or PSU €25, fixings and foam backing €25, speaker for the countdown €5. **About €130–150.**

**Biggest risk:** mechanical durability (repeated shots from teen footballers) and crosstalk between panels. Answer: mount panels on rubber isolators and collect training data early. Fallback: a single panel with a 3×3 LED grid and learned localisation.

### ⭐ 3. Zuikis: chase the light (a Wavelight for every school stadium)
**What it physically is:** 20 portable "rabbit" cones, each a 3D-printed or repurposed cone holding an ESP32-C3, a bright WS2812 ring and an 18650 battery.
- The cones go on the lane markings every 20 m of a 400 m track (or every 10 m of a school loop).
- ESP-NOW keeps their clocks in sync, and a phone app sets the pace.
- A light wave travels around the track at your target pace. Up to 4 runners each get their own colour.
- **Ghost mode** replays your last run's real split profile, so you race yourself.

**Demo:** a 5 m strip, or 6 cones across the stage. "This light is moving at Kipchoge's marathon pace. Now watch the pace of a 16-year-old's 1 km." Then a video of the class running the pilot. Result slide: "even-split runs up from X% to Y%, 1 km times −Z s" (filled in from the pilot).

**What the AI actually does (honest: moderate):**
- From 2–3 logged runs, and optionally Galaxy Watch HR, it predicts a realistic target and builds an even or negative-split plan.
- It adjusts the next interval's pace from HR drift and RPE.
- It auto-calibrates cone positions using the runner's GPS/IMU crossing times, so a sloppy placement doesn't wreck the pace.
- The AI is a supporting part; the pacing hardware is what people will notice.

**Closest existing products:**
- **Wavelight** (Arnhem, NL): LED pacing lights on the inner curb, allowed in competition by World Athletics since 2020, with a mobile version and 10 programmes at once [verified: Wikipedia; wavelight-technologies.com].
- **LED Rabbit** (Indico, Italy): portable, up to 12 athletes [verified: indicotech.com].

Both are elite and club equipment [price unverified, likely thousands]. **Our difference:** a ~€200 open-source kit that a PE teacher can carry in one box, plus the personal ghost.

**How it scales:** a kit per school or per municipal stadium. Running clubs, parkrun-style events and fitness-test days can use it.

**LT hook (moderate):** winter darkness is actually *better* for chasing a light. It could tie into the national pupil fitness testing if it includes an endurance run [unverified which test].

**BOM ([unverified]):** 20 × (ESP32-C3 €4 + LED ring €3 + 18650 + holder €3 + cone/print €2) ≈ **€240**, or ~€120 for 10 cones on a 200 m loop.

**Biggest risk:** with sparse cones, the "moving light" is less magical than a continuous strip, and sunlight makes LEDs hard to see. Test the 10 m spacing early and use high-brightness rings with diffusers.

### ⭐ 4. Rezervas: "you have 2 reps left" (bar-speed coaching for teen gyms)
**What it physically is:** a matchbox-sized clip on the barbell sleeve (ESP32-C3, a 6-axis IMU, LiPo, magnet or velcro) that talks BLE to a phone. It can also be a school gym station: 10 clips plus a wall tablet.

**Demo:** a jury member (or a team member) does a set of squats or bench presses with a light bar. The screen shows each rep's speed as a bar, and at the rep where speed drops more than 25% it says "stop, set done". Next slide: "estimated 1RM 72 kg, no max attempt needed". It speaks straight to the ego-lifting crowd.

**What the AI actually does:**
- Segments reps and integrates IMU velocity with zero-velocity resets at the top and bottom of each rep.
- Fits a **personal load-velocity profile** to predict 1RM without max testing.
- Uses a velocity-loss threshold to end sets before failure. Velocity-based training (VBT) literature uses thresholds of roughly 20–30% [unverified; check Pareja-Blanco et al.].
- Flags form breakdown from bar-path tilt and wobble.
- Suggests next session's load from fatigue trends.

**Closest existing products:** GymAware (linear position transducer, pro), Vmaxpro and Enode (IMU clips, a few hundred €), RepOne, and the Metric VBT phone-camera app [all unverified names and prices].

**Our difference:** ~€20 hardware and a *shared school-gym station* with teen-safety defaults: no max tests, and auto-stop by velocity rather than failure. It is aimed at the kids who otherwise learn from TikTok.

**How it scales:** school gyms, municipal gyms, clubs' S&C rooms. Coaches get a dashboard across a team.

**LT hook (moderate):** strong gym culture among LT teens [unverified], and **Žydrūnas Savickas**, one of the most famous strongmen ever [background knowledge].

**BOM ([unverified]):** ESP32-C3 €4, BMI270 IMU €4, LiPo + charger €5, case and magnet €4. **About €17 per clip.**

**Biggest risk:** IMU velocity drift makes it less accurate than a string transducer. Validate against phone slow-motion (240 fps) and report the error. A generalist jury may see it as "a fitness gadget, not a solve-for-tomorrow"; frame it as injury prevention and safe strength training for youth.

### ⭐ 5. Triukas: the board that knows your tricks
**What it physically is:** a rugged 3D-printed pod between the truck and the deck (or in the tail) holding a high-range IMU (±16 g, ±2000 °/s) and an ESP32-C3 with BLE. A phone app logs every attempt.

**Demo:** a team skater does an ollie, a kickflip and a pop-shove-it on a mat on stage, or a stage-safe clip. The screen names each trick live with pop height and rotation, and marks it landed or bailed. Then **S.K.A.T.E. referee mode:** two boards, and the app judges whether the second skater matched the trick.

**What the AI actually does:** a 1D-CNN or GRU on the IMU windows classifies 6–10 trick classes plus landed or bailed. It also estimates pop height, flip and shove rotation, and logs progression ("kickflip land rate 18% → 41% in 3 weeks"). Collecting a labelled dataset (filmed attempts) is a real ML project the team can be proud of.

**Closest existing products:** academic IMU trick classification (e.g. work from FAU Erlangen) [unverified]. Past action-sports trackers (e.g. Trace) [unverified status]. I know of no mainstream product for this [unverified].

**Our difference:** it's cheap and made *by skaters, for skaters*, with a referee mode.

**How it scales:** skateparks, skate schools and online "trick-of-the-week" leagues. The same pipeline can work for snowboards and BMX.

**LT hook: weak.** Skateparks in Vilnius and Kaunas; skateboarding has been an Olympic sport since Tokyo 2020 [background knowledge]. This is its main weakness for the jury.

**BOM ([unverified]):** IMU €5, ESP32-C3 €4, LiPo €4, printed TPU/PC case + bolts €5. **About €18.**

**Biggest risk:** impact damage, and needing a real skater on the team to collect a few hundred labelled attempts. Weak LT and BIG scores.

## 3. Summary
- **Strongest:** **Kiemo Karalius** (13/14). It has the best LT hook (courtyard basketball; 3x3 bronze at Paris 2024 [verified]), a real public-infrastructure scale story (municipal court-usage data), a fun arcade demo, and AI doing real sensor fusion. Weakness: **smart outdoor courts already exist in the US** (RIMNET, HoopLink [verified]). The pitch must be the *camera-free, solar, no-phone, municipal-data* version, not "first ever". Court Pulse (#4), Kiemo Kamera (#3) and Elo balancing (#7) should be folded in as layers, not separate ideas.
- **Most "not seen":** **Siena** (13/14; a Footbonaut-style wall for about €150, multi-sport, used daily) and **Zuikis** (12/14; a Wavelight kit for about €200, where AI plays a supporting part).
- **Most WANT for gym teens:** **Rezervas** (12/14). It is a gadget-ish product with existing competitors, but has a real youth-safety angle.
- **Passion pick:** **Triukas** (11). It only works if someone on the team skates.
- **Reserve:** the auto-referee outdoor ping-pong table (#9, 11/14), which has a great everyday-school angle.
- **Verify next:**
  - What LT municipalities spend on courtyard courts, and how many courts there are.
  - Footbonaut and Wavelight/LED Rabbit prices.
  - VBT velocity-loss thresholds.
  - Whether any LT or Baltic court sensor exists.
