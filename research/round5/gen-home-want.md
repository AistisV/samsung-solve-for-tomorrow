## TL;DR

- **ShowerCoach 2.0 = "Gyvatuko detektyvas" (the Snake Detective).** Every Soviet-block flat pays a "gyvatukas" fee to keep hot water circulating, so that hot water comes out *instantly*. Many residents still run a bucket of cold water first, and that water is **billed as hot water** [verified: kurjeris.lt]. A €10 clip-on sensor on the bathroom towel-rail coil (the "gyvatukas" itself), plus the shower meter, turns every flat into a probe. Put 8–15 flats together and you get a **heat map of the building's hot-water circulation**: which riser and floor gets 50 °C water, and at what time. That is the legal test NVSC sets: ≥ 50 °C at the tap after 1 minute of running [verified: nvsc.lrv.lt]. The administrator gets a fix list.
- **Why this answers "not an SfT project?":** the shower gadget becomes the family's reason to install it. The *project* is a crowd-sourced diagnosis of a collective, invisible waste that ~38,000 old blocks pay for [verified in round 4]. That problem is Lithuanian (and post-Soviet), measurable in October, and nobody measures it from the residents' side.
- **Verdict:** a genuine contender, **conditional on a week-1 reality check.** If the team's own blocks all get hot water in under 15 s, there is no villain, and it falls back to a nice gadget. Filter score ≈ **13/14**.
- **Part B top 4:** **StoveCoach** (a wood-stove burn coach, 13/14), **Freezer Doctor** (the berry-and-mushroom chest freezer, 12), **Meter Eye** (a camera that reads the water meter, 12) and **Electricity Stethoscope** (reading the smart-meter LED, 12). Honourable mentions: a winter bird-feeder counter and a lake-bloom buoy (both 12, but they can't be piloted by Nov 6).

# PART A: ShowerCoach 2.0, "Gyvatuko detektyvas" (the Snake Detective)

## A1. The facts that make it big

| Fact | Source |

**Reading.** The shower is the **only point in the building where residents measure hot water every day**. That's why amphiro/Oras turned it into a building sensor, but only for professional buildings in Western Europe. In a Lithuanian block, nobody on the residents' side measures whether the circulation they pay for works. Round 3 killed a "gyvatukas optimiser" as "niche, little public evidence". That was a top-down utility tool. Seen from the residents' side, there is public evidence: the NVSC FAQ, the complaint articles and the fee headlines.

**Important nuance.** In many blocks the bathroom **towel-rail coil (the "gyvatukas", literally "little snake") sits on the hot-water circulation riser**. It heats the bathroom all year, and it is where the fee's name comes from (Kauno energija: "vonios šildytuvas (gyvatukas)"). So **the coil's temperature is a direct, no-plumbing reading of whether circulation reaches your flat.** In some buildings circulation stops in the basement (the 10 kWh norm). There, waiting is by design, and the device would reveal that too.

## A3. Honest technical feasibility

**Sensors**
- **Flow** (layer 1): hall-effect turbine sensors (YF-S201 / YF-B series, G½"). Uncalibrated accuracy is only ±10% or so. Calibrate against a bucket and stopwatch at 3 flow rates to get about ±3–5% [unverified]. Some YF-B variants have a built-in NTC in the brass body [unverified]. That saves one sealed probe.
- **Temperature at the shower:** the unit sits **after the mixer**, so it sees *mixed* water (e.g. 40 °C). **It cannot run the 50 °C legal test.** That is the most important technical catch. Fixes:
  - (a) Layer 2's second NTC on the **hot supply pipe** to the mixer. Pipes in Soviet bathrooms are often exposed [unverified; check in each flat].
  - (b) A **"1-minute test" mode**: open hot fully, then the unit logs the curve for 60 s, exactly like the NVSC test.
- **"Waiting litres"** = volume flowed from the start of the draw until the mixed temperature first reaches the household's usual shower temperature (learned). It is a clean, defensible number.
- **Coil clip** (layer 2): the coil's surface temperature lags the water but is steady. For "is circulation alive" we care about hours, not seconds, so the lag doesn't matter. Clamp the NTC with thermal paste and a hose clip, and insulate over it with a strip of pipe foam. Validate each install by holding a kitchen thermometer in a cup of tap water once.
- **Time-to-hot at the tap without a shower unit:** a pipe clip alone can't see when the draw *starts*, because the stagnant cold slug looks like "no change". So the draw start must come from the flow sensor, from Meter Eye (Part B #4), or from a cheap accelerometer on the pipe (flow noise) [unverified that this is reliable]. v1 uses the shower unit for events and the coil clip for continuous health.

**Power**
- **Coil clip:** an ESP32-C3 in deep sleep, waking every 10 s for an ADC read, stores to RTC memory and sends every ~15 min. Rough average: ~0.3–0.6 mA, so **several months on 2×AA** [calc: 30 mA × 0.1 s awake per 5–10 s, plus ~5 µA sleep; real numbers must be measured]. No water, no mains, so it's safe.
- **Shower unit, v1:** a sealed 18650 cell woken by the flow sensor's pulses. Weakness: hall sensors draw mA while powered. Power the sensor only when a micropower wake source trips, or accept weekly charging. **Weekly charging kills month-2 use** (round-4 judge B).
- **Shower unit, v2 (the "self-powered" slide):** a G½" micro hydro generator (sold cheaply online as 5 V/12 V units [unverified specs]) charges a 1–5 F supercap. **The generator's AC frequency is the flow reading**, so one part does power and sensing. There's no standby drain because the device only lives while water flows (the amphiro a1 principle [verified]). Timestamps come from a Wi-Fi/NTP sync at the end of each shower, or from the phone over BLE.
- **Go/no-go for v2:** measure shower flow on the **top floor** with and without the turbine. If the flow drop is > 10% it annoys users, and top-floor pressure in old blocks can be weak [unverified]. Then keep v1 and show v2 as a bench prototype.

**Waterproofing and safety**
- Everything is low voltage (≤ 5 V), with no mains in the bathroom.
- Electronics are potted (silicone or epoxy) in an IP67 box, with the display behind a clear lid. The shower unit is mounted at the **hose inlet**, not in the spray.
- Plumbing uses standard G½" fittings with PTFE tape. Old mixers vary, so bring 2–3 adapters [unverified].
- **Legal and ethical line:** we measure temperature, *not* bacteria. Say "below the temperature NVSC sets to prevent Legionella", never "your water has Legionella".

**Accuracy summary (targets to verify in week 1)**
- Litres: ±5%
- Temperature: ±0.5 °C in water, ±2–3 °C at the coil surface
- Time-to-hot: ±3 s
- € per shower: ± the tariff month

## A4. Where the AI does real work (not a wrapper)

1. **Building diagnosis model (the core).**
   - **Inputs:** coil temperature series from N flats with known floor and riser; waiting-litre events from the shower units; time of day; outdoor temperature; optionally the substation's hourly heat data from the utility.
   - **Output:** a ranked list of causes, each with a confidence and the evidence behind it:
     - "Riser B, floors 7–9 fall below 45 °C between 23:00 and 06:00. Pattern matches: **circulation pump on a timer / off at night**."
     - "Riser B is cold at all hours while riser A is fine. Pattern matches: **unbalanced or closed circulation valve on riser B**."
     - "Whole building 44–47 °C. Pattern matches: **substation hot-water setpoint too low**."
   - **Method:** anomaly detection on daily profiles, clustering flats by riser, plus a small physics model. It is Newton cooling of the stagnant water column: the "waiting litres" should grow with the time since the last draw, and the fitted dead-volume per flat tells you whether hot water reaches the riser near the flat or only the basement.
   - It is honest ML at teen scale, and it makes a decision a threshold can't: *which* fix, *where*.
2. **Household pattern learning (the family layer).**
   - After ~45 s it predicts the final litres and € of *this* shower from the household's own distribution: "on track for €0.41".
   - A monthly hot-water bill forecast.
   - Anomaly flags: "a 38-minute shower", "hot water drawn at 03:00, possible dripping mixer".
   - **No per-person identification** (round-4 veto). Anyone who wants a personal target presses a button.
3. **Plain-language reports.** An LLM writes the Lithuanian monthly letter for the administrator from the computed findings (a generation layer only; the numbers come from the model).

## A5. Stage demo (2 minutes)

1. **The joke (10 s):** "My dad yells about hot water. So I measured. The problem isn't me, it's the snake." A photo of a towel-rail coil appears; every Lithuanian juror has one.
2. **The live gadget (30 s):**
   - The shower unit is on a small closed rig: a 12 V pump, a 5 L insulated tank at ~42 °C, and a long thin coil of clear tube as "the riser".
   - Pump **off**: water comes out cold, and the ring counts "waiting: 1.8 L, 1.4 ct".
   - Pump **on**: instant warm water, waiting 0.1 L.
   - Scaled: the thresholds are set in demo mode (e.g. 38 °C) for safety.
3. **The reveal (60 s):** our own block as a **floor × riser heat map** from the October pilot, then a 24-hour time-lapse of coil temperatures. Say the top floor of riser B goes blue every night. Then: "our block paid €X for the snake in October; the 9th floor drained Y litres of 'hot' water that was cold." (X and Y come from the pilot, never invented.)
4. **The fix (20 s):** the administrator's letter or reply, and before/after if they rebalance a valve in time. The map of the city is the ask: "every block could have this map".

The WOW comes from **the data reveal**, not the pipe. Round-4 judge A's "water through a pipe isn't memorable" is answered by the heat map.

**Illustrative scale** [calc, not a source]:
- A DN20–25 riser holds ~0.35–0.5 L per metre, so a cold riser up to the 9th floor (~24 m) is **~8–12 L** to drain. That matches "a bucket".
- 10 L × €7.92/m³ ≈ **8 ct per event**. Twice a day that is ≈ **€4.8 per flat per month**, *on top of* the circulation fee.
- In a healthy block it should be 0.5–2 L. The pilot decides which story is true.

## A8. Scaling path

1. **Family:** ShowerCoach as a school-library loan, or bought as a kit (€25–35).
2. **Building (the unit that matters):** the bendrija buys 10–15 coil clips for ~€150 for a 1-month audit. That's a one-off, but the clips can stay.
3. **Administrator/maintainer:** a dashboard across their portfolio of blocks, so they fix the worst risers first and avoid complaints and NVSC trouble. Round 3's red team found that maintainers' incentive is **zero complaints and legal compliance** rather than savings, and this sells exactly that.
4. **Heat utility:** the utility bills the gyvatukas and fields the complaints (Panevėžio energija already writes about managing gyvatukas heat). It can hand clips out, or combine the data with its substation meters.
5. **City:** an open **"Karšto vandens žemėlapis" (hot-water map)**, NVSC's temperature norm made visible block by block.
6. **Abroad:** the same Soviet-era systems exist across LV, EE, PL and UA [unverified detail], which fits the "transferable" criterion.

## Top-4 profiles

### B1. StoveCoach (Malkų treneris): "the stove that tells grandpa when to add a log" (13/14)

**Pitch.** A €20 clip-on for wood stoves, fireplaces and masonry stoves. It reads the flue temperature and tells you, on a small display or phone, **"add 2 logs now"**, **"open the air, you're smouldering"** or **"this wood is wet"**. For the traditional masonry stove it says when the embers are really done so the damper (*sklendė*) can close without CO risk.

**Why a family wants it**
- Grandpa's pride is his stove, and it's a real daily chore.
- Less wood bought (money), a warmer house for the same wood, and less smoke in the neighbourhood.
- The damper question in old houses is real safety knowledge that used to be passed down by feel.

**LT hook**
- The coal/peat ban in the big cities and resort towns started on **1 May 2026** [verified in round 4: am.lrv.lt]. **This is the first heating season under it**, so households shift to wood.
- In the cold season, all LT air-quality stations record PM exceedances [verified in round 3: kaipkada.lt]; Vilnius names private-house wood burning as the winter PM source [verified in round 4: vilniusinstitute.lt].

**Hardware**
- K-type thermocouple + MAX31855 on the flue pipe (or a probe in the flue), ESP32, OLED/LED, battery or USB.
- Optional: an ESP32-CAM in the yard aimed at the chimney top in daylight; a model scores **smoke opacity** from dark to clear.
- **Safety:** a *certified* CO alarm next to it, never a DIY MQ-7 as the life-safety device.

**AI**
- Burn-phase classification from the flue-temperature curve: ignition, flaming, char/embers, smouldering.
- Refuel-moment prediction learned per stove.
- Wet-wood signature (slow rise, low plateau).
- Chimney-cam smoke score as ground truth that trains and checks the temperature model.
- That is a real sensor-fusion story, not a wrapper.

**Demo.** A heat gun on the thermocouple plays through a burn cycle live while the coach changes its advice. Beside it, a side-by-side time-lapse from the pilot: our grandparents' chimney, smoky vs clear, with the AI smoke score.

**Pilot by Nov 6.** 5–8 houses (grandparents, sodybos, families with fireplaces).
- Week 1 baseline: logging only.
- Then 2 weeks coached.
- Metrics: logs/kg of wood per day vs outdoor temperature, % of burn time smouldering, chimney smoke score.

**Prior art**
- Analog magnetic stove-pipe thermometers with a "burn zone" are common and cheap [unverified].
- Some premium stoves have automatic air control [unverified].
- The UK "Ready to Burn" wet-wood rules [unverified detail].
- The claim that correct operation greatly cuts particle emissions needs one solid source before use [unverified].

**Scale.** Chimney sweeps, municipal clean-air campaigns, the APVA boiler-replacement programme's "until you replace it, burn right" message.

**Risks**
- It's a private-house idea, and the team may live in blocks (the "at grandma's" angle solves this).
- CO liability: never advise closing the damper on temperature alone; show "check embers".
- The camera only works in daylight.

### B2. Freezer Doctor (Šaldiklio daktaras): "don't lose the chanterelles" (12/14)

**Pitch.** A wireless temperature tag lives inside the old chest freezer in the garage, basement or sodyba. A metering smart plug (a SmartThings plug works) watches the compressor.
- It alarms before a power cut or failure thaws the summer's berries and mushrooms.
- It learns the freezer's normal duty cycle, so it notices a failing door seal or ice build-up weeks early.
- It says what the old freezer costs per year and when a new one pays back.

**The insight that makes people lean forward**
- Many freezers are rated only for certain room temperatures ("climate class").
- In an **unheated garage or sodyba in a Lithuanian winter** some models can misbehave or stop cooling properly [unverified: check climate class SN/N behaviour].
- The device catches that.

**Why a family wants it.** Every family with a chest freezer of berries, mushrooms, meat or dumplings fears the thaw, and it's the stock from the summer foraging. It's a repeated, background, "saved us once" device.

**LT hook.** Foraging and freezing culture (blueberries, chanterelles, cranberries), freezers at sodybos.

**AI.** A duty-cycle model against ambient temperature, with anomaly detection (compressor running longer for the same ambient means seal/ice/refrigerant trouble), and a thaw-time forecast after a power cut ("you have ~14 h").

**Demo.** A mini cool box with a Peltier "compressor" and a finger on the lid seal. The AI flags the abnormal cycle live. A pilot curve from a real family freezer.

**Pilot.** 8–10 family freezers now (the harvest just went in): energy per day, a duty-cycle baseline, and any anomalies found.

**Sustainability.** Food waste avoided (kg), plus kWh of old appliances. Honest note: the energy story needs sourced numbers on old vs new freezers [unverified].

**Risks.** It could feel like "just a thermometer alarm", which is a commodity. The duty-cycle diagnosis and the winter-garage insight must carry it. Radio from inside a metal freezer is weak; use a thin flat-cable probe through the seal.

### B3. Meter Eye (Skaitiklio akis): "the meter that reads itself and hears the toilet" (12/14)

**Pitch.**
- An ESP32-CAM clamped over each water meter (hot and cold) reads the dials with an on-device digit/dial model.
- It submits the monthly reading automatically (no more crouching behind the toilet with a torch).
- It spots **night flow**, meaning a running toilet or a dripping tap.
- It turns the family's water into a daily curve.

**Why it matters for this lens**
- It is the **no-plumbing** way to get hot-water events for Part A: meter dials start moving, which marks the draw start.
- It can be sold as its own idea or as Part A's whole-flat layer.

**Prior art.** The open-source "AI-on-the-edge-device" project does meter reading on ESP32-CAM [unverified, not re-checked]. Utilities roll out remote-read meters in places [unverified for LT].

**LT hook.** Residents in many LT blocks declare readings monthly [unverified; confirm with the team's own utility]. It's the same old-block bathroom.

**AI.** A CNN for digits and the analog pointer (real on-device inference), leak detection from the night-flow baseline, and per-flat usage profiles.

**Demo.** Turn a tap, and the digits on screen tick in real time. Then "night mode" shows a leaking cistern trace from the pilot.

**Pilot.** 10 flats for 3 weeks: leaks found, reading accuracy against a human, litres per person per day against the vanduo.lt Klaipėda average of ~1.0 m³ hot water per person per month [verified in round 4].

**Risks**
- It could look like a smart-home hobby project, and WOW is only moderate.
- Meters behind hatches have bad lighting (an LED ring fixes it).
- It needs power (USB from a hall socket, or a battery with hourly photos).

### B4. Electricity Stethoscope (Elektros stetoskopas): "tape it on the meter, it tells you what's on" (12/14)

**Pitch.**
- A photodiode taped over the smart electricity meter's pulse LED (imp/kWh) counts every watt-hour, with no electrical work at all.
- The AI disaggregates big loads: kettle, oven, electric boiler, heater, dryer, sauna.
- It combines them with the hourly Nord Pool price: "your boiler heats at 18:00 at peak price; move it to 03:00 and save €X/month."

**Why a family wants it.** Parents want the "why is the bill so high?" answer. The teen demo ("turn on the kettle; the screen says *kettle, 2.1 kW, 0.6 ct a cup*") is great for showing off.

**Honest limits**
- Pulse resolution is poor at low loads. At 1,000 imp/kWh, a 100 W load blinks once every 36 s [calc], so it only works for big appliances.
- Whether ESO's meters expose a pulse LED or a customer data port needs checking [unverified].
- Commercial analogues exist (Tibber Pulse, Sense, Loop) [unverified details], but not in school fairs.

**LT hook.** Dynamic/hourly price plans and the post-2025 Baltic grid (synchronised with continental Europe in Feb 2025 [unverified date; confirm]); cross-link to round 3's GridPulse.

**AI.** Event detection + clustering of power steps (NILM), appliance labelling with one-tap confirmation, and price-aware scheduling advice.

**Demo.** A live kettle and hair dryer on stage, fed through a plug-in energy meter with a pulse LED (the same principle), and the screen names the appliances.

**Pilot.** 8 flats/houses for 3 weeks: kWh identified by appliance, € shiftable.

**Risks.** Heat, not electricity, is the big LT home cost for block flats (heating and hot water are up to 80% [verified in round 3]). So it's strongest for private houses with electric boilers, heat pumps or saunas.

### Honourable mentions
- **Winter feeder counter (#5):** the highest WANT + WOW for families. Weaker on sustainability (nature/biodiversity, not energy) and NOT-SEEN (Bird Buddy-style feeders exist).
- **Lake bloom buoy (#6):** a strong LT hook and WOW, but no blooms in Oct–Nov, so there's no pilot before Nov 6.

## Summary

- **Part A.** ShowerCoach becomes **"Gyvatuko detektyvas"**: the family shower meter plus a €10 clip on the bathroom towel-rail coil in every volunteer flat.
  - Together they give a floor × riser heat map of the block's hot-water circulation. Residents pay for it every month; in the heating season it's billed by a fixed norm (e.g. 160 kWh/flat) whether it works or not.
  - The legal test (≥ 50 °C after 1 min, NVSC) and "a bucket of cold water billed as hot" (Tauragė) are verified.
  - Feasibility is good: the coil clip is trivial and months on AA; the shower unit is medium; the self-powered turbine is a stretch.
  - **Key catch:** a shower unit after the mixer can't measure the 50 °C supply, so the hot-pipe/coil clip carries the building story.
  - The AI is a real, small cause-ranking model.
  - The pilot fits the heating-season start. **Verdict: ≈ 13/14 and a genuine contender, if a week-1 check finds a block where the snake is failing.**
- **Part B.** 28 ideas; the top 4 are **StoveCoach (13)**, **Freezer Doctor (12)**, **Meter Eye (12)** and **Electricity Stethoscope (12)**. Meter Eye can double as Part A's no-plumbing layer. StoveCoach is the strongest standalone alternative, and it's pilot-able right now at grandparents' houses.
