# Past Solve for Tomorrow (Baltics) — what judges picked

> Note: the official site (solvefortomorrowbaltics.com) is blocked from this research environment, so this is assembled from news coverage via web search. Gaps are marked.

## Program history (Baltics)
| Season | Theme | Known winners / finalists |
|---|---|---|
| 2022/23 | Sustainable lifestyle | **Winner (Baltic): "Minova" — Klaipėda** (Deimantė Šileikaitė, Rapolas Žygus). AI app that helps *restaurants* forecast demand and cut food waste. Later represented LT at the first pan-European final in Milan (Oct 2025) — "talks of investment started". |
| 2023/24 | ? (likely education/AI) | *No data found.* |

LV finalist: app linking physical activity to screen-time control; app for visiting places in Latvia. 1st/2nd place: *not found.* |
| **2026/27 (ours)** | **Sport & tech OR sustainability** | 6th season. Prize fund last year €16–18k: winner €5k cash + €5k Samsung devices. |

## Patterns — what this tells us

1. **Winners are narrow, concrete use-cases with a clear user:** "restaurants' food waste", "grades 1–3 reading via physical library books". Not "everyone".
2. **AI is in nearly every winner.** And criterion 48.7 formalises it. Expect ~every entry to say "AI" — so AI alone is not differentiation, it's table stakes.
3. **A working prototype visibly moved the jury** (Orientr "stood out precisely because they had a ready prototype and a well-thought development plan" — program director Eglė Tamelytė).
4. **Physical-world hook wins attention**: QR codes on real books, real orienteering, real restaurants. Pure screen apps blur together.
5. **Last year's sport season was saturated with "gamified movement app with points / map / quests"** — at least 6 of ~15 known finalists are variations. The same judges will see *more* of these this year. → **If we pick sport, "gamified app that rewards moving" is a trap.**
6. **Sustainability has had a winner already (Minova, food waste)**, so food-waste-with-AI is taken in judges' memory. Needs a different angle.
