## 3. Detailed profiles of the top 4 (plus 1 runner-up)

### 3.1 ArtiMetras: "How close did that car pass me?" ★ STRONGEST PICK

**One-line pitch:** A €40 box on a teenager's bike measures, for every car that overtakes, the exact passing distance against Lithuania's new 1 m / 1.5 m law. Pooled rides from school cyclists give the city the first-ever map of *where* drivers break it, so the next protected bike lane goes where kids are actually being brushed.

**Problem (LT evidence):**
- The legal minimum passing distance (1 m at ≤50 km/h, 1.5 m above) has applied since Dec 2024. In Sept 2026 an exception is being debated ([Reidas](https://reidasofficial.lt/patarimai-ir-rekomendacijos/keliu-eismo-taisykles-naujienos/soninis-atstumas-lenkiant-dviratininka-1-metras-ket/), [Dzūkų žinios](https://dzukuzinios.lt/2026/09/lenkiant-dviratininka-1-ir-15-metro-taisykle-galioja-svarstoma-isimtis/)). **No compliance data exists in LT** (**unverified**: I found none).
- Cyclist injuries are up in 2026: 103 injured and 3 dead in Jan–May, against 94 and 1 in 2025 ([15min](https://www.15min.lt/verslas/naujiena/saugukelyje-lt/per-penkis-menesius-suzeisti-103-dviratininkai-nauja-kampanija-vairuotojus-ragina-matyti-ne-kliuti-o-zmogu-1662-2696846)).
- Vilnius wants cycling to grow from 4.6% to 7.5–10% of trips by 2030 ([MadeinVilnius](https://madeinvilnius.lt/transportas/vilniaus-gatves/vilnius-siemet-nuties-19-km-nauju-dviraciu-taku-tikslas-250-km-tinklas-iki-2030-m/)). Perceived danger is the classic barrier. Parents already don't let kids travel alone: only 16% feel confident ([jp.lt](https://jp.lt/kas-ketvirtas-pradinukas-gatveje-nesijaucia-saugiai-kaip-suprasti-ar-vaikas-pasirenges-savarankiskai-keliauti-i-mokykla/)).

**What the tech does and why the idea collapses without it:**
- A side-facing ToF/ultrasonic distance sensor on the handlebar or rack samples at ~20–50 Hz. An ESP32 detects the overtaking event: the distance signal drops and recovers, which is how OpenBikeSensor does it. GPS and speed are logged with each event.
- A phone camera on the handlebar runs on-device YOLO to confirm that the object is a vehicle and classify it (car, bus, truck, van). It also cuts the false positives that pure ultrasonic sensors get from parked cars and poles. Faces and plates are blurred on the phone.
- Uploads go to a map that aggregates per street segment: number of passes, % under 1 m, median distance, share by trucks and buses.
- **Collapse test:** a cyclist cannot estimate 0.8 m vs 1.2 m while being passed at speed, and has no way to count thousands of events per street. Without the sensor you have anecdotes. The municipality gets no evidence, and the debate on the law has no data.

**Adoption loop in one breath:** School cycling teams and commuting teens ride with the box (they want to prove their route is dangerous and get it fixed) → the data is pooled on a public map → the municipality's mobility unit (Vilnius JUDU / Kaunas) and the Transport Competence Agency (TKA) use it to rank streets for protected lanes and for school-route safety, and the Ministry of Transport uses it in the passing-rule debate → the city or TKA funds a lending pool of ~30 boxes per city through schools (≈€1–2k). Cycling NGOs spread it.
- First partner to approach: TKA (it already ran the "see a person" campaign and the school-environment study), Vilnius JUDU, or the Lithuanian Cyclists' Community (Lietuvos dviratininkų bendrija, **unverified contact**).

**Where the core tech is proven:**
- **OpenBikeSensor** (Germany/Austria, open hardware, ultrasonic + GPS). An Austrian paper analysed **11,399 overtakes**: compliance was ~40% in cities (≥1.5 m) and ~19% in rural areas (≥2 m) ([Sensors 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26010087), [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12787887/)). Cologne/Bonn measured >4,000 events, and the city of Konstanz shared a dataset ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2022/02/05/openbikesensor-measures-close-calls/), [GitHub](https://github.com/openbikesensor/OpenBikeSensorFirmware)).
- **SenseBike**: a low-cost sensor that measures passing distance plus air quality ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12656652/)).
- A peer-reviewed before/after study used overtaking-distance measurement to evaluate advisory cycle lanes ([MDPI 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/18/18/9324)).
- On-device YOLO vehicle detection on Android is mature.

**Prior art and how ours differs:**
- OpenBikeSensor exists, but: (a) it is not used in the Baltics, as far as I found (**unverified**); (b) it is ultrasonic-only, whereas we add camera AI confirmation and vehicle classification; (c) our framing is **school routes and teen cyclists**, which ties it to TKA's school-safety work and to a live legal debate; (d) the data goes into LT municipal decisions.
- Garmin Varia is a rear radar that warns *the rider* and does not measure lateral distance for the city.
- Honest note: the novelty is the *application and deployment model*, not the sensor. The brief says this is what we want.

**Measurable impact and pilot plan (by Nov 6):**
- Metrics: % of overtakes <1 m per street segment; the number of overtakes <1 m per 100 km ridden; the top-10 most dangerous segments on school routes.
- Longer term: the before/after change on a street that gets a lane or a 30 km/h zone, and the change in the cycling mode share of pupils at the pilot school.
- Pilot: 3–5 boxes, 5–10 student riders, 2–3 weeks of commutes in October. Expect several hundred overtakes (**estimate**). Publish "X% of passes on street Y broke the law", which is a real LT first. Validate against a tape-marked test lane (a known 1.0 m / 1.5 m) to show sensor accuracy.

**Demo:** A bike on a trainer on stage, with the sensor facing a cardboard "car" on a trolley pushed past at different distances. The phone beeps red at <1 m and green at ≥1 m, and the live map fills with the team's real October data. The "wow" is the heatmap of their own city with real violation percentages.

**Top 3 judge risks:**
1. *"This is just OpenBikeSensor."* Answer: yes, the proven block is intentional. What's new is the camera AI, the school-route targeting, and plugging it into LT's new law and municipal decisions. Nobody in LT has this data.
2. *"Ultrasonic is noisy, and parked cars cause false triggers."* Answer: the camera AI cross-check, plus validation against a measured test lane. We report accuracy honestly.
3. *"Will the city act on it?"* Answer: Vilnius is building 90+ km of new lanes by 2030 and must choose where. TKA runs school-safety studies. The ministry is debating the rule now. We would ask for a letter of intent from one of them.
- Also expect privacy questions (GDPR): video stays on the phone, only numbers are uploaded, and faces and plates are blurred.

**Self-score (1–10):** Collapse 9 · Adoption 7 · Proven 9 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 8 · Demo 9 · Local evidence 8. AI/IoT bonus: both. Themes: sport (active mobility) + sustainability (mode shift).

### 3.2 Druskos radaras: precision winter salting to save city trees

**One-line pitch:** A cheap infrared road-surface thermometer and humidity sensor on vehicles that already drive the city at night (sidewalk tractors, buses, garbage trucks), plus an ML ice model. Together they tell the salting crew *which streets actually need salt tonight and how much*, instead of salting everything "just in case".

**Problem (LT evidence):**
- Vilnius used 85,000 t of salt in 2023–24, then 38,300 t and 23,500 t (scope and years to be confirmed on [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/lietuvoje/2/3004294/ziema-vilniuje-druskos-ir-vel-pribarstyta-tiek-daug-kad-ji-neistirpo-net-per-vasara)).
- Salt residue was still visible in summer 2026. An arborist measured 0.5–8 g/L. Old trees are dying, and a public protest happened ([LRT protest](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/lietuvoje/2/2487686/savivaldybe-apie-protesta-del-vilniaus-medziu-bukles-druskos-barsteme-dvigubai-maziau)).
- The city washes trees with ≥70 L each in spring ([Alfa](https://www.alfa.lt/aktualijos/aplinkosauga/savivaldybe-prabilo-po-dvieju-savaiciu-tylos-atskleide-druskos-normas-ir-prisipazino-pavasariais-plaunanti-medzius/409904/)).
- Vilnius is already experimenting ("salt-free" squares, more gravel, a Žygimantų street experiment) ([Vilnius](https://vilnius.lt/naujienos/vilnius-ruosiasi-ziemos-sezonui-dar-daugiau-skaldeles-skverai-be-druskos-ir-eksperimentas-zygimantu-gatveje)).

**What the tech does and why the idea collapses without it:**
- The sensors log pavement temperature, dew point and a surface wet/dry estimate every few metres along a route. That builds a **thermal fingerprint** of the city (bridges, shaded courtyards and underpasses freeze first).
- An ML model (gradient boosting on the thermal fingerprint plus the LHMT forecast) predicts which segments will drop below freezing with moisture tonight. It outputs a per-street "salt / gravel / nothing / reduced dose" plan.
- **Collapse test:** pavement temperature and black ice are invisible. Crews currently decide per district from the air forecast. The whole point is sub-street resolution.

**Adoption loop:** The city's street-maintenance contractor or municipal company gets a nightly salting plan on a tablet → salts less where it isn't needed → the municipality (which pays for salt and for tree replacement) mandates it in the maintenance contract, because the salt-and-trees issue is politically hot → sensors ride on vehicles that already exist.
- First partners: the Vilnius municipality environment and "green" unit, the tree activists (free publicity), and a regional contractor.
- Watch out: if contractors are paid per tonne spread, their incentive is reversed. The city must own the metric (**unverified** how LT contracts are structured).

**Where the core tech is proven:**
- Minnesota's statewide "Salt Solutions" programme used pavement temperature sensors to cut salt and sand, and accidents fell 6% in the first year ([MnDOT report](https://mdl.mndot.gov/_flysystem/fedora/2023-06/199820.pdf)).
- Research on sensor-based spreader automation ([arXiv 2010.12983](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.12983)) and on ML precision salt dosing ([ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2095756425000820)).
- Guidance on vehicle-mounted pavement temperature measurement ([UVM Sea Grant](https://www.uvm.edu/seagrant/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Measure_Pavement-Temperature_2025_10_22.pdf)).
- Commercial products exist: Vaisala road sensors and vehicle-mounted temperature sensors.

**Prior art and how ours differs:** Road-weather stations and pro mobile sensors cost €thousands per unit and cover highways (Via Lietuva). Our angle is dense, low-cost coverage of **city sidewalks and streets**, optimised for **tree survival**. We close the loop with Vilnius's existing 500 soil conductivity sensors, which gives measured salt in the soil near trees after each salting decision.

**Measurable impact and pilot:**
- Metrics: tonnes of salt per km per season; soil conductivity at tree pits; slip and fall incidents (as a safety guard).
- Pilot by Nov 6: night drives in October/early November along 3–4 routes with the IR sensor on the teacher's car. Show that surface temperature differs by up to X °C between segments of the same street (the value is to be measured; it's the key "wow" data point). Also sample soil salinity at 20 street trees with a €20 EC meter to show where last winter's salt remains.
- The real salt-reduction number needs winter, so the pitch is "pilot ready for winter 2026–27".

**Demo:** A tabletop "street" of tiles, one chilled with an ice pack and one at room temperature. A sensor on a toy truck drives over them, the map colours the frozen tile red, and the "salt only here" prescription appears. Then show the real thermal map of our town.

**Top 3 judge risks:**
1. *"Safety: what if you under-salt and someone falls?"* Answer: the model only reduces salt where the surface is provably above freezing or dry, and the default stays "salt". Minnesota's accidents fell.
2. *"No winter before Nov 6."* Answer: the thermal fingerprint can be measured on autumn nights, and soil salt from last winter is measurable now.
3. *"Contractors won't bother."* Answer: the city is the payer and is under public pressure. The plan goes into the contract.

**Self-score:** Collapse 9 · Adoption 7 · Proven 8 · Measurable 8 · Not saturated 9 · Demo 7 · Local evidence 9. IoT and AI both apply. Theme: sustainability (strong).

### 3.3 Geltonas algoritmas: school-bus route optimiser for consolidating districts

**One-line pitch:** When a district closes or merges schools, our optimiser redraws the yellow-bus and special routes in minutes, with fewer km, fewer buses and no child riding over 30 minutes. It also shows which seats are free for rural residents.

**Problem:**
- Buses cover several hundred km a day, and some rides approach an hour against a 30-minute target ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/eismas/7/1567524/liudesys-regionuose-autobusiuku-vaikams-pavezeti-truksta-o-sie-dar-ir-genda-nes-rieda-varganais-keliais)).
- Consolidation raises distances: minimum class sizes rise from 2026, and districts such as Anykščiai have 2026–2030 network plans ([Anykščiai](https://infoerdve.lt/anyksciu-rajono-savivaldybe-kviecia-vertinti-mokyklu-tinklo-plana/)).
- Budgets are real: Klaipėda district spent €382k on pupil transport in 2019 ([SMM report](https://www.svis.smm.lt/file/manual/Savaivaldybiu_pazanga/2019%20m/Klaipedos_r%202019%20pa%C5%BEangos%20ataskaita.pdf)).
- Yellow buses can't carry residents, which undermines rural lines ([Mano ūkis](https://www.manoukis.lt/mano-ukis-zurnalas/2015/09/sesk-pavesiu-viesasis-transportas-ir-kaimo-pensininkai/)). Lazdijai's 2026 reform that merges pupils into regular routes is the model to scale ([Lazdijai](https://infoerdve.lt/lazdiju-rajono-viesojo-transporto-sistema-pavyzdys-visai-lietuvai-2026-09-21/)).

**Tech and collapse test:** Capacitated vehicle routing with time windows is NP-hard, and planners in LT do it by hand or from historical routes (**unverified**). We build OR-Tools/OSRM on OpenStreetMap, with pupil *stops* (not home addresses) as input. Proven: Boston/MIT cut routes 20%, removed 50 buses, and saved $5M and 1M miles in year one ([MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/creating-better-bus-routes-algorithms), [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/digital-government/2019/08/boston-school-bus-routes/159113/)). There is also open-source BusPlan ([GitHub](https://github.com/azavea/bus-plan)) and rural SBRP research ([arXiv 2507.19538](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19538)).

**Adoption loop:** A district's education department (which pays for fuel, drivers and buses) → uploads its stops and schools → gets routes and a savings report → uses it when approving the new school network. It reaches them through the municipal association (LSA) and the ministry's yellow-bus programme.

**Metric and pilot:** km/day, number of buses, maximum and mean ride time, tonnes CO₂. Pilot: rebuild one district's published routes (some councils publish route tables, e.g. [Pasvalys](http://www.pasvaliosm.lt/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/T1-173.pdf), [Kaunas district](https://www.vrk.lt/documents/10180/566883/16+Kauno+rajono+savivaldyb%C4%97.pdf/8f6a4666-a093-4f9d-92e7-8ea858ea38e9)) and report the % saving. Then simulate "school X closes".

**Demo:** A live map where you click "close this school" and routes reflow in seconds, with € and CO₂ counters.

**Risks:** Data access and GDPR (use stops, not addresses). Local knowledge (bad gravel roads in winter) is handled with road-type penalties. "Commercial software exists" (Transfinder etc.), but it is US-focused, expensive, and not used in LT (**unverified**).

**Self-score:** Collapse 9 · Adoption 7 · Proven 9 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 8 · Demo 8 · Local 8. AI bonus weak (optimisation, not ML). Theme: sustainability. The teen "wow" is lower.

### 3.4 Smoky-car screener: roadside low-cost plume sensing to find tampered DPFs

**One-line pitch:** A shoebox at the roadside sniffs each passing car's exhaust plume (black carbon/PM spike relative to the CO₂ spike) and flags the few high emitters, likely cars with a removed DPF, that produce most of the soot.

**Problem:** LT has an old fleet (~16–18 years; **verify with ACEA**). Garages openly advertise DPF deletion. Inspection only checks opacity until EU PN testing arrives "in coming years" ([Reidas](https://reidasofficial.lt/naujienos/dpf-filtras-technine-apziura-daleliu-skaitikliai-lietuva-2026-2/)). The LEZ programme is stalled outside Kaunas ([LRT EN](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/2564609/do-lithuanian-towns-need-low-emission-zones-government-rethinking-earlier-decision)).

**Proven:** The EU H2020 **CARES** project showed that low-cost point-sampling and plume-chasing sensors measure individual vehicles' BC/PN and detected a tampered-DPF Golf ([ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048969724018527), [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/814966/reporting/de)). Plume chasing has also been used in Czechia ([ScienceDirect 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0048969726000483)).

**Adoption loop:** The Environmental Protection Department / TKA runs screening campaigns → flags cars for targeted inspection → removes the worst emitters. Municipalities use the data to justify an LEZ.

**Why it isn't top:** Student-grade sensors (PM2.5 optical plus NDIR CO₂) are probably too slow and insensitive for single-car plumes, since CARES used faster BC instruments (**risk: the science may not work at student cost**). Plate reading raises legal and GDPR issues. A demo is possible with a tailpipe on stage, but the brief's "no hopefully the science works" rule bites.

**Self-score:** Collapse 10 · Adoption 6 · Proven 7 · Measurable 8 · Not saturated 9 · Demo 5 · Local 7.

### 3.5 Runner-up: VR "first walk to school" trainer

A VR simulation of the child's *actual* route (built from street imagery/3D). Grade 1–4 pupils practise crossings and are scored on gap selection, and parents get a readiness report. The context: only 16% of parents feel confident letting a child go alone, 24% of pupils feel unsafe, and pedestrians are about a third of LT road deaths. VR pedestrian training has published trial results (Schwebel et al., University of Alabama; **not re-verified here because the search budget was exhausted**). It gets the VR bonus, but it partly fails the collapse test (a real accompanied walk does much of the same), and impact (mode shift) is hard to measure by Nov 6. Scores: C 6 · A 6 · P 7 · M 5 · S 7 · D 9 · L 8.

## 4. Recommendation

**Strongest pick: ArtiMetras (3.1).**
- It is the only idea here that scores ≥8 on almost every hard criterion *and* lets the team generate real, headline-grabbing Lithuanian data before Nov 6 ("X% of cars on street Y broke the 1 m law").
- It hits both themes: active mobility/sport and green mode shift.
- It plugs into a live policy debate (Sept 2026 talk of an exception to the passing rule) and into rising 2026 cyclist casualties.
- It uses proven open hardware plus AI and IoT, and the stage demo is easy and physical.
- The judge's obvious objection ("it's OpenBikeSensor") is answerable: the brief explicitly wants proven blocks in a new application.

**Best backup: Druskos radaras (3.2).** It has an emotionally strong local story (dying Vilnius trees, a protest, salt still visible in summer 2026) and a clear payer. Its weakness is that the salt-saving number needs winter.
