# Round 3 brief: shared by all research agents

## Context
Samsung **Solve for Tomorrow 2026, Lithuania/Baltics**. Teams of 3–5 students (grades 9–12) + a teacher. One idea, one theme:
1. **Sport & tech**: make sport more accessible/educational/meaningful; get students more active.
2. **Sustainability**: how can tech help build a greener tomorrow / help us live more sustainably.

Timeline: stage 1 idea form **Oct 16 2026**; developed concept + visual presentation **Nov 6**; local jury picks TOP 5 per country; mentoring; **Baltic final in December, pitched in English**.

**Official judging criteria:**
- Stage 2: Technology usable? · Creativity/novelty + *research into prior efforts* · Quality/realism (clear model of how it reaches its goals) · Local relevance (fits local realities, constraints, opportunities) · Completeness · Broader impact (inspires others, community benefit, **could influence local policy**).
- Final: best use of tech · detail/clarity of plan · link to global problems, transferable to other communities · feasible given resources and laws · sustainable, widely applicable impact · **ability to reach the target audience** · **bonus for AI, IoT, VR or AR**.

The team can code and build hardware. Hardware is welcome if it makes the demo cooler.

## What already failed, and why (don't repeat these mistakes)
| Idea | Why the team rejected it |
|---|---|
| WellWatch: phone photographs nitrate test strips for private wells | **Tech is a wrapper.** Remove the phone and it still works (people read strips by eye just as well). |
| WellWatch 2.0: DIY UV nitrate sensor | **"We build a sensor and… then what?"** No clear user, distribution or business loop. |
| BeetleNose: e-nose boxes on spruce trees detect bark beetles | **Who straps a €40 box to 1,000 trees?** Deployment is absurd at scale. And **unproven tech is suspicious**, not a plus. |
| SoundCourt: spatial-audio AI so blind kids play ball games with classmates | **Can't imagine it actually being used or scaling.** Cool on paper, no real adoption. |
| KneeGuard: phone AI checks exercise form | **Overused/obvious.** |
| Gamified step/points/quest apps, AR orienteering, screen-time-for-movement | **Saturated**: last year's (2025, sport-only) Baltic finalists were full of these. |
| Food-waste AI for restaurants | Already won in 2022 (Minova). |

## HARD criteria: an idea must pass ALL of these
1. **Collapse test**: remove the tech and the idea collapses. The tech does something humans can't do by eye, by hand or with a spreadsheet.
2. **Adoption loop is obvious**: you can name *who* uses it, *why they'd bother*, *who pays or who deploys it*, and *how it reaches them* in one breath. The deployment must be realistic (no "a sensor on every tree").
3. **Proven building blocks, new application**: the core tech must be proven *somewhere* (research with real results, or a product in another country/industry). The novelty is applying it to a Lithuanian problem in a new way. No "hopefully the science works" ideas.
4. **Measurable impact**: you can state a number that changes (kg, €, tonnes CO₂, hectares, people, incidents), and how you'd measure it in a pilot.
5. **Not saturated**: a judge hasn't seen it ten times. Check prior art honestly.
6. **Demo-able by students in ~6 weeks**: something that works on stage (hardware or software), even if simplified.
7. **Real local problem with evidence**: Lithuanian data, institutions, laws, news.

## Nice to have
- AI / IoT / VR / AR at the core (the final bonus).
- A "wow" moment in a live demo.
- Policy hook (a law, EU directive, municipal programme or deadline it plugs into).
- A natural first customer/partner (municipality, company, state agency, school network) that might sign a letter.
- The team can generate **real pilot data** before Nov 6.
