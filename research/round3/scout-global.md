### A2. Patterns: why judges pick something

1. **A named, narrow user with a physical problem.** Diabetics' feet, the visually impaired, babaçu breakers, villages on small streams, one named river. Nobody won with "everyone should move more". Europe's sport-tech winners were all about **health or inclusion** (CuraStep, VisionNex, SkillFit, Singapore's cooling vest, Türkiye's pool sensors), **not gamification**.
2. **A physical artefact on the table.** Shoe, glasses, power bank, solar dryer, wound scanner, sensor under a bridge, turtle robot, generator bike. Of the 5 Milan winners, 3 were hardware. The pure apps that won had a very sharp health-education gap (My Hormone) or a clear AI mechanism (SkillFit).
3. **Self-generated numbers.** Fionn: >1,000 tests, 87%. SJWP 2024: PFAS within 10%, −93%. WarnMe: sensor prices and a live network. Bogotá: −24%. Science juries in particular reward *measured* results over projected ones.
4. **Cheap, made from reuse, open.** The €50–85 sensor (WarnMe), recycled parts (Liova, BabaçuTech), local wool (Oilsorb), a chitosan bio-electrode. "Costs ten times less than the pro version" is a winning sentence.
5. **Deployment has already started.** WarnMe is "being scaled across their region with partners". Minova was in investor talks. SkillFit and the German winners go into a **6-month pilot "impact phase"**. India turns winners into incubatees. Samsung increasingly judges these as *startups-in-embryo*, so a partner letter plus a pilot plan matters.
6. **A clear sensing/AI mechanism.** The winning tech does something humans can't: see for the blind, predict PFAS, detect N₂O, hear a flood coming. That matches the brief's collapse test.
7. **Demo style.** A live object (shoe, glasses on a judge, a sensor reacting on stage), a short map or dashboard fed with *their own pilot data*, and a one-number outcome.

## Top profiles

### ⭐ 1. SchoolGate: AI counter for the school drop-off (sustainability; sport as the secondary angle)

**Pitch.** *"Every morning, 400 cars squeeze into 100 metres outside our school, and nobody knows the number, so nobody can change it. Our €60 AI box counts every car, walker and cyclist, and it proved that a School Street works."*

**LT problem (evidence).** Children driven to school **+200% over 30 years**; independent travel down from 7/10 to 5/10. **182 children were injured within 100 m of schools in 2022–25** ([Vilnius](https://vilnius.lt/naujienos/tyrimas-apie-vaiku-sauguma-kelyje-susimasto-tik-2-tevai-is-10), [15min](https://www.15min.lt/verslas/naujiena/saugukelyje-lt/stebejo-vaikus-ir-ju-tevus-tyrimas-atskleide-kaip-vaikai-is-tiesu-elgiasi-kelyje-1662-2754846)). Only ~5% of teens get enough activity; active commuting is the cheapest daily movement there is. *(To research: whether Vilnius/Kaunas already run "mokyklų gatvės" pilots, and how they measure them.)*

**What the tech does.** An old Galaxy phone or a Raspberry Pi 5 in a classroom window facing the gate runs an on-device YOLO model plus a tracker. Per 5-minute bin it counts cars, pedestrians, cyclists, scooters and buses; **drop-off stops** (a car stationary for 5+ seconds in the no-stopping zone); **idling time** (converted to g CO₂/NOx with emission factors); and **conflicts** (a child track crossing within X m of a moving car track). It never stores images. Only counts leave the device, as Telraam does. A dashboard compares a normal week, an intervention week and the week after. The collapse test holds: a human can tally one morning, but not hundreds of mornings, dwell times, idling and near-conflicts every day across several schools. That continuous before/after comparison is what makes a policy trial credible.

**Adoption loop.** *Users:* the school administration and the parents' council, who want proof to ask for a school street / "kiss-and-go" zone. *Why bother:* safety pressure after incidents, plus the School Streets idea. *Who pays or deploys:* the municipal transport/mobility department (in Vilnius, JUDU / Susisiekimo paslaugos, unverified) or the school from its own budget, at ~€60–150 per gate. *How it reaches them:* a school network (e.g. a municipality's schools) and an open-source kit that other student teams install at their own schools, so the adoption channel is *students themselves*.

**Where proven.** Telraam (Leuven, BE), with the EU H2020 **WeCount** project in several European cities: citizen-installed window counters producing open traffic data used by municipalities 🟡. London and others run hundreds of School Streets with published evaluations 🟡.

**Prior art and our difference.** Telraam counts general traffic. We add **school-specific metrics** (drop-off dwell, idling, child–car conflicts), a **before/after trial protocol**, and deployment at *one Lithuanian school with real data*. LT prior art: manual observation studies (the 15min/"Saugu kelyje" research) exist, but no continuous counter that we know of (unverified).

**Impact metric and pilot by Nov 6.** Two weeks of baseline counts (Oct 12–23). Then a one-week "walk to school" / nudge intervention, or a temporary closure if the municipality agrees; if not, a parents' messaging campaign. Measure the Δ in cars/morning, drop-offs in the no-stopping zone, % active arrivals, idling minutes → kg CO₂/week. Validate the counter against a 30-minute manual count (accuracy %).

**Demo.** A toy street on stage (or a live camera over the audience entrance). The box counts in real time, and a 4-week pilot graph from our own school shows the step change.

**Judge risks and answers.**
1. *"Privacy: cameras on kids."* All processing is on-device. No frames are stored or sent; only integers leave. GDPR-by-design, like Telraam; we show the code.
2. *"Humans can count cars."* For one hour, yes. Not every day, with dwell times and conflicts, across a before/after trial. That is exactly why cities adopted Telraam.
3. *"Will anything change?"* The data is the lever for the School Street decision. We bring a letter from the school/parents' council, and ideally the municipality, for a trial week.

**Self-score (1–10).** Collapse 7 · Adoption 8 · Proven 9 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 8 · Demo 8 · Local evidence 9 · AI/IoT bonus 9.

### 2. OvertakeSense (*Pralenk saugiai*): how close do cars pass teen cyclists? (sport & tech / active mobility)

**Pitch.** *"Drivers think they pass cyclists at 1.5 m. Our bike box measured 1,000 overtakes in Vilnius, and X% were closer than one metre. Here's the map of the 10 streets where teens shouldn't have to ride."*

**LT problem.** Teens are among the least active in Europe; cycling to school is an easy daily activity that parents forbid because "roads are dangerous". *Missing evidence to collect:* LT cyclist injury and fatality stats (Lietuvos kelių policija / LAKD / Regitra), the KET rule on lateral passing distance (I believe KET sets a minimum lateral gap for overtaking cyclists, **unverified**), and Vilnius cycling plans.

**What the tech does.** An ESP32 box on the handlebar or rack. A side-facing ultrasonic or time-of-flight (ToF)/lidar sensor measures the lateral distance of every passing vehicle at 20–50 Hz. GPS logs the location. Optionally a rear camera + on-device AI classifies vehicle type (car/bus/truck) and estimates speed. Data syncs to a map that aggregates **% of overtakes under 1.0/1.5 m per street segment**. Humans can't judge a gap of 0.8 m versus 1.3 m from a bicycle. This is pure sensing.

**Adoption loop.** *Users:* school cycling clubs, teen and adult commuters, cycling NGOs, who lend the boxes. *Why:* to prove danger and win bike lanes. *Who pays or deploys:* the municipal mobility department uses the heatmap to prioritise infrastructure (and the police for targeted enforcement); boxes cost ~€40–70. *Reach:* a lending pool in schools.

**Where proven.** **OpenBikeSensor** (DE): an open-source kit with a data portal, used by cycling groups and municipalities in Germany 🟡. **SimRa** (TU Berlin) crowdsources near-misses 🟡. German law since 2020 sets 1.5 m in town / 2 m outside town, and these sensors were used to show widespread non-compliance 🟡.

**Our difference.** The first LT deployment, a *teen-centred* route network (home → school), an AI vehicle-type layer, and results translated into a municipal "top 10 dangerous segments" list.

**Pilot by Nov 6.** Build 3 boxes by Oct 10. Team plus volunteers ride their commutes for 3 weeks. Target ~500–1,000 overtakes. Validate against a tape-measured static test.

**Demo.** A bike on stage; a judge walks or rolls a trolley past; the LED goes red and the screen reads "0.74 m". Then the city map.

**Risks.** (1) *"Tiny user base; teens don't bike in November."* The pilot runs in October, and the data serves the whole city. (2) *"Just a transfer of OpenBikeSensor."* Yes, deliberately proven tech; the novelty is the LT data and the teen-route policy loop. (3) *"Safety of kids riding to collect data."* Riders use existing routes only, with no instruction to ride dangerous streets.

**Self-score.** Collapse 9 · Adoption 7 · Proven 8 · Measurable 8 · Not saturated 9 · Demo 9 · Local evidence 5 (needs research) · AI/IoT 8.

### 3. 50/50 Watts: the school keeps half of the heat it saves (sustainability)

**Pitch.** *"Our school heats empty classrooms on weekends and heats the street through open windows. Our sensors find the waste room by room, and a 50/50 deal means the school gets half of the money saved."*

**LT problem.** Municipalities pay school heating (mostly district heat). The EU Energy Efficiency Directive (recast 2023) requires public bodies to cut final energy use by **≥1.9%/year** 🟡 (verify the article/number). Classroom CO₂ and overheating are recurring hygiene topics (the HN 21:2017 school norm, **unverified**). *Missing:* LT school heat cost per m², municipal energy-management programmes.

**What the tech does.** 10–20 €8–15 sensor nodes (temperature, humidity, CO₂; window reed switch), SmartThings/ESP32 → cloud. AI/rules flag: heating while windows are open; rooms above 22 °C; weekend/holiday heating; CO₂ above 1,500 ppm (poor ventilation); forecasts of the next day's savings. A weekly "energy detective" report goes to the class and the caretaker. It turns meter bills (monthly, whole building) into room/hour diagnoses.

**Adoption loop.** *Who pays:* the municipality, which saves money. *Who uses:* students and the caretaker. *Why bother:* 50% of savings goes back to the school (as in Hamburg's fifty/fifty and Euronet 50/50 🟡). *First partner:* our school plus the municipal education/property department.

**Pilot by Nov 6.** LT heating season usually starts in October. Put sensors in 10 rooms for 3 weeks and quantify the hours of "heating + open window" and weekend overheating → estimated kWh/€.

**Demo.** Open a window next to the node on stage → alert "Room 204: €0.40/hour escaping".

**Risks.** (1) *Collapse:* "bills already show savings." Bills can't tell you *which room/hour*; the tech makes the behaviour change targeted. It's the weakest point (score 6). (2) *"Smart-building IoT is common."* In companies, yes; a student-run savings-sharing model in LT schools is not known (unverified). (3) *District heating has no room-level valves in old schools.* True for some buildings, so pick a school with thermostatic valves or a renovated building.

**Self-score.** Collapse 6 · Adoption 9 · Proven 8 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 6 · Demo 8 · Local evidence 6 · AI/IoT 8.

### 4. StreamWarn LT: a SJWP-winning flood warning for small Lithuanian rivers (sustainability / climate adaptation)

**Pitch.** *"Official gauges watch the big rivers. Our €60 sensor under the village bridge watches the small ones, and texts the elder before the water reaches the road."*

**LT problem.** Spring ice jams and floods (Nemunas delta / Rusnė area and smaller rivers) and flash floods after summer downpours. *Evidence still to collect:* LHMT gauge coverage vs number of rivers, PAGD flood call-outs, municipal flood events.

**What the tech does.** A replica of WarnMe: an ultrasonic/IR distance sensor under the bridge, solar-powered, LoRa/NB-IoT. The server computes the rise rate and threshold crossing plus an ML forecast combining LHMT precipitation data. Alerts go by app or SMS to the seniūnija (local district) elder and residents. A camera variant could detect ice jams (CV on ice cover).

**Adoption loop.** Users are seniūnijos and residents; the municipal civil-protection department deploys; one sensor per risky bridge (tens per municipality, not thousands). This is realistic.

**Proven.** WarnMe, SJWP 2025, already scaling in its region ✅ ([Xylem](https://www.xylem.com/en-us/making-waves/water-utilities-news/stockholm-junior-water-prize-winners-bring-faster-flood-warnings-to-small-communities/)).

**Pilot by Nov 6.** Install 1–2 sensors on a local stream bridge (with the municipality's permission) and show autumn rain responses against the nearest LHMT gauge.

**Risks.** (1) *Impact is hard to prove in 6 weeks* (no flood); show accuracy against LHMT instead. (2) *A copy of a 2025 winner.* The judges of this Baltic contest likely don't know SJWP; still, credit it openly as "proven". (3) *Is flooding a big LT issue?* Needs evidence, which is the weakest part.

**Self-score.** Collapse 9 · Adoption 7 · Proven 10 · Measurable 5 · Not saturated 8 · Demo 8 · Local evidence 4 (unverified) · AI/IoT 8.
