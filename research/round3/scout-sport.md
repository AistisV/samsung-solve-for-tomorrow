## 0. Honest bottom line first

The sport theme has a problem that sustainability mostly doesn't: **the Lithuanian sport problem is behavioural** (only about 5% of teens are active enough, and more than half of inactive kids "don't see the point"). Technology that *changes behaviour* is almost always a wrapper (apps, points, quests), and those are the saturated category. Technology that **passes the collapse test** in sport usually *measures* something humans can't see: heart rate, movement intensity, forces, ball events. So the best sport ideas are **measurement ideas**, and a judge will always ask: *"measuring doesn't make kids move, so what?"*

Only one idea below survives that question well enough for me to rate it at the level of a strong sustainability idea: **ActiveLens**, a computer-vision tool that audits how much kids *actually* move in PE and at break time. It works because the "so what" is already in place: Lithuania has a legal PE framework, school public-health specialists who monitor and advise schools, an "Active School" network and a SAM guideline to aim for ≥50% moderate-to-vigorous activity, yet **nobody measures it**. In my judgement it's roughly **on par with a good sustainability idea, not clearly better**. Sport remains the harder theme to win on the collapse test and on novelty.

## 3. Detailed profiles

### ⭐ 1. ActiveLens: "the first honest measurement of how much Lithuanian kids move at school"

**Pitch:** A phone or cheap camera in the gym corner, with on-device pose AI, measures *every* child's movement intensity through a whole PE lesson or break. No wearables and no stored video. The teacher gets a class report ("47% of lesson in MVPA; 6 kids mostly standing during the drill; small-sided games doubled activity vs. queueing drills"), and the school health specialist gets a school-level indicator.

**Problem (LT evidence):**
- Only ~5% of grade 9–13 students meet activity guidelines ([HBSC via MadeinVilnius](https://madeinvilnius.lt/en/life/health/young-people-in-Lithuania-are-among-the-least-physically-active-in-Europe/)). PE lessons are the one place where *all* kids move, including the 95%.
- LT's own measurement: in an LSU accelerometer study, **average MVPA in PE didn't reach 50%** ([vb.lsu.lt](https://vb.lsu.lt/object/elaba:1983066/1983066.pdf)). Internationally, 50% MVPA of PE time is the standard target (US CDC/Healthy People; *background knowledge*). SAM's school activity recommendations ([SAM PDF](https://sam.lrv.lt/uploads/sam/documents/files/Veiklos_sritys/visuomenes-sveikatos-prieziura/mityba-ir-fizinis-aktyvumas/fizinis-aktyvumas/rekomendacijos/mokiniu_fizinis_aktyvumas_rekomendacijos.pdf)) exist, but no routine tool checks them. *(Whether the SAM document states "50%" explicitly is **unverified**.)*
- The system already collects **fitness outcomes** (annual national test, risk-zone consultations with parents) ([Diena](https://m.diena.lt/naujienos/lietuva/salies-pulsas/iveda-naujove-del-kuno-kulturos-vertins-mokiniu-fizini-pajeguma-935238)) but measures **zero process**: nobody knows whether a given school's PE actually gets kids moving. It's like measuring exam results without knowing whether lessons happen.

**What the tech does / collapse test:**
- Pose estimation (MediaPipe/YOLO-pose, on-device) tracks each child's skeleton, computes per-child movement intensity from keypoint velocities, and classifies sedentary, light or MVPA per 5-second window (calibrated against HR straps or accelerometers in our own pilot).
- The human gold standard, **SOFIT/SOPLAY** (McKenzie et al., *background knowledge*), needs a trained observer who codes **one child every 20 s** or scans the area periodically. It's too labour-intensive to do routinely, which is exactly why nobody in LT does it. **A teacher physically cannot track 25 children's activity second by second while teaching.** Remove the AI and you're back to "we have no idea", so it passes the collapse test.
- Why not wearables? They cost per child, need charging and consent for *health* data, and take time to hand out. Camera + skeleton only means one device per gym, and no per-child identity is needed for class-level reports.

**Adoption loop (one breath):** *The PE teacher sets a phone on a tripod → gets a 1-page report after the lesson and sees which drills leave kids standing → changes the lesson format. The school's public-health specialist (employed by the municipal Visuomenės sveikatos biuras, VSB) deploys it across the municipality's schools and reports an "MVPA-in-PE" indicator. The municipality/VSB pays (a phone plus software licence, ~€0 extra hardware in schools that have tablets). Entry route: the Higienos institutas "Aktyvi mokykla" network and the VSBs that already run the fitness testing.*
- First partner: the team's own school, then its municipal VSB (every municipality has one; they already run the fitness tests and write school health profiles). LSU as validation partner (they built the fitness-assessment system and ran accelerometer PE studies).

**Proven building blocks:**
- Real-time multi-person pose estimation on phones: MediaPipe / YOLOv8-pose (*background knowledge*; mature, open source).
- Observational PA systems SOFIT/SOPLAY: validated for decades (*background knowledge*).
- Video-based activity-intensity estimation: a research area with results (several papers automate SOPLAY-style counts with CV; **specific citations unverified in this session, which the team must find**).
- Feedback to PE teachers increases lesson MVPA: HR-monitor programmes (Polar GoFit) and SPARK-style curricula show this ([Polar education](https://www.polar.com/en/business/education), [St. Louis MVPA study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4362389/)). *(The size of the effect is **unverified**.)*

**Prior art and how we differ:** Polar GoFit/MYZONE need a wearable per child and target US grading. Lü/interactive gyms are games, not measurement. Research CV-SOPLAY exists only as prototypes. **Novel:** a no-wearable, privacy-first *audit tool* plugged into an existing national public-health workflow (fitness test → risk-zone consult → now also "is the PE actually active?").

**Measurable impact:** % of PE time in MVPA per class (baseline vs. after feedback). Secondary: minutes of MVPA per pupil per week at school; % of break time active, by zone (yard redesign).
**Pilot by Nov 6:** (1) Weeks 1–2: build on-device pose pipeline. (2) Validate: 3–4 volunteers wear HR straps/phones (accelerometers) during 3 lessons, then correlate the video intensity score with the HR/accelerometer measure. (3) Baseline: film ~6 PE lessons (with consent; process on-device; store only numbers). (4) Show the teacher the report; film 4 more lessons after the teacher changes one drill. Target result: "*measured X% MVPA, teacher changed Y, rose to Z%*". That's real LT pilot data.

**Demo:** A camera on stage; the judges (or the team) do a 60-second "PE drill". The screen shows skeletons coloured green, yellow or red live and a class MVPA gauge filling up. Then "queue drill vs. small-sided game" side by side. That's the wow moment, and it's clearly AI.

**Top 3 judge risks:**
1. *"Filming children? GDPR!"* → Processing is on the device; **no video is stored or transmitted**, only per-window anonymous numbers. Class-level reports by default. Consent is via the school's existing consent process. (A DPIA would still be needed for real deployment. Say so honestly.)
2. *"Measuring doesn't make kids move."* → Measurement is the missing link in an existing chain: the state already tests fitness outcomes and advises parents, but can't see why a school's PE fails. Teacher feedback is the lever (evidence from HR-feedback programmes). The pilot shows the change in one teacher.
3. *"Is video-based MVPA accurate?"* → We validate it ourselves against HR/accelerometers and publish the correlation. We claim class-level accuracy, not medical-grade individual accuracy. Occlusion in a full gym is a known limitation, so we use two phones or a high corner mount.

**Self-score (1–10):** Collapse 8 · Adoption loop 7 · Proven blocks 7 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 8 · Demo-able 8 · Local evidence 7 → **~54/70**

### 2. PulseClass: heart-rate-zone PE (keep as ActiveLens's wearable mode)

**Pitch:** 25 cheap BLE HR straps (or Galaxy Watches) plus a wall display. Each pupil sees their own zone, the grade is based on *effort time in zone, not sprint speed*, and preparatory-group pupils get a doctor-set HR ceiling alarm, so they take part instead of sitting on the bench.
- **Problem:** as above; plus PE exemption abuse ([tv3](https://www.tv3.lt/naujiena/lietuva/vaikui-susirgus-pakanka-tevu-rastelio-o-kas-atleis-nuo-kuno-kulturos-n1011470)) and 1.7% preparatory / 0.7% special group ([HI](https://vssis.hi.lt/Naujienos/Naujiena?NewsId=1405)).
- **Collapse:** yes, because a teacher can't see 25 heart rates.
- **Adoption:** teacher → school buys the set (~€25–40/strap, *unverified price*) → effort-based assessment.
- **Proven:** Polar GoFit, MYZONE, US schools ([Polar](https://www.polar.com/en/business/education), [PhysednHealth](https://blog.physednhealth.com/pe-heart-rate-grading-science-equity/), [Sport Journal](https://thesportjournal.org/article/grit-predictability-of-effort-and-achievement-in-physical-education/)).
- **Why not #1:** it's a **product that already exists**, so a judge who searches "heart rate PE" finds Polar in 5 seconds. Novelty is "import to LT" only. Health-data consent for minors is heavier than for anonymous skeletons.
- **Self-score:** C 8 · A 7 · P 10 · M 8 · S 4 · D 9 · L 6 → **52/70**

### 3. HoopStats Youth: AI stat sheet for every school-league game

**Pitch:** A phone behind the baseline auto-detects made/missed shots and players (jersey OCR), producing a box score and per-player season profile for school (MKL) and regional youth games, which have no statisticians today. Regional kids become visible to coaches and scouts outside Vilnius and Kaunas.
- **Problem:** regional youth basketball lacks officials ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/sportas/10/2349860/krepsinio-teiseju-veiklos-vystymas-augantis-uzmokestis-ir-jaunu-teiseju-skaicius)); talent ID concentrates in big cities *(no hard LT number found, **unverified**)*.
- **Collapse:** yes. No human is there to log stats; the video exists but nobody watches it.
- **Adoption:** team manager films → players and parents get stats → the league (MKL/LKF) deploys. Freemium, parents pay.
- **Proven:** open-source YOLO shot detectors; SportsVisio, Pixellot, HomeCourt (*background knowledge*).
- **Prior art problem:** a crowded commercial field; judges may say "Pixellot does this". Differentiator: free/school-league, LT-league integration, jersey-number OCR. Serves already-active kids, so it's weak on the "5% active" problem.
- **Demo:** mini hoop on stage, live shot counter. Fun.
- **Self-score:** C 7 · A 6 · P 8 · M 6 · S 4 · D 8 · L 5 → **44/70**

### 4. LeagueMesh: schedule optimiser for regional youth leagues

**Pitch:** A constraint solver builds a season schedule for a regional youth league that fits hall availability, the few available referees and minimum travel. It outputs "X fewer km, Y fewer games without a referee, Z t CO₂".
- **Problem:** "teams played when a referee could" ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/sportas/10/2349860/krepsinio-teiseju-veiklos-vystymas-augantis-uzmokestis-ir-jaunu-teiseju-skaicius)); rural travel distances.
- **Collapse:** yes. Hundreds of teams × halls × referees is combinatorial, so humans use spreadsheets and get poor solutions.
- **Proven:** sports scheduling with OR/constraint programming is used by pro leagues (*background knowledge*); Google OR-Tools.
- **Pilot:** re-optimise a *published* past league schedule and report the km saved. That's real data with no permissions needed.
- **Weakness:** not "wow", no AI/IoT bonus unless framed as AI optimisation, and indirect for getting inactive kids moving. It's also a sustainability crossover.
- **Self-score:** C 8 · A 6 · P 9 · M 8 · S 7 · D 6 · L 6 → **50/70**

### 5. OpenHall: school halls open in the evening without a caretaker

**Pitch:** Keyless access tied to an existing booking identity (Kaunas/Vilnius systems), with an occupancy camera that counts heads and detects damage or incidents, plus auto lights and ventilation. The municipality opens idle halls at almost zero staffing cost.
- **Problem:** booking exists but halls stay closed because staffing is expensive (Alytus: ~€66k/yr for 14 halls, *search summary; page not opened*); residents ask "are the stadiums only for the chosen ones?" ([jp.lt](https://jp.lt/panevezieciai-piktinasi-ar-mokyklu-stadionai-tik-isrinktiesiems/)).
- **Proven:** Sportbox app-unlocked equipment boxes in DE/NL ([sportbox.de](https://sportbox.de/en/b2b/), [W&H](https://whsports.nl/en/product-category/outdoor-fitness/sportbox/)); smart access control is a mature product category.
- **Why not higher:** the tech *replaces a person* rather than doing something humans can't (a borderline collapse test); school directors carry the liability for incidents; and it's an IT-procurement feel rather than a student-innovation feel.
- **Self-score:** C 5 · A 7 · P 9 · M 8 · S 7 · D 7 · L 7 → **50/70**

## 4. Strongest pick and verdict on the theme

**Strongest pick: ActiveLens.** It's the only sport idea I found that is at once:
- **AI at the core with a true collapse test**: continuous multi-child activity coding is impossible by eye, which is exactly why the gold-standard method is sampled by hand.
- **Aimed at the 95%**: it's about PE and breaks, not athletes.
- **Plugged into an existing Lithuanian workflow**: annual fitness testing, VSB school health specialists, the Aktyvi mokykla network, SAM recommendations. That gives an obvious deployer and a policy hook: *"add MVPA-in-PE to the national school health indicators"*.
- **Pilotable with real data before Nov 6** in the team's own gym.
- **A live wow demo.**

**Main open items the team must verify** (the search budget ran out):
1. Find 2–3 papers on computer-vision automation of SOFIT/SOPLAY or video-based MVPA estimation, to prove the idea is not "hopefully the science works".
2. Confirm the SAM/HI wording on PE-lesson activity targets and the Aktyvi mokykla network size.
3. Check that no LT or Baltic product already does this.
4. Get the school's consent process sorted early.

**Is sport weaker than sustainability?** Mostly yes. Sport's headline problem (motivation and meaning) resists non-wrapper tech, the "movement app" space is saturated in these judges' memory, and the strongest sport tech (wearables, stats cameras) is heavily commercialised. ActiveLens is the exception: it's **competitive with**, not clearly better than, a strong sustainability idea. If a sustainability scout has a candidate with an equally clean collapse test *and* a hard € or tonnes number, I'd lean sustainability. ActiveLens's impact number ("% of PE time active") is solid but one step removed from health outcomes.
