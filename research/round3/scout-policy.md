## 5. Top profiles

### P1. "Fix or Rewet": satellite AI for decaying drainage systems (**strongest pick**)

**Pitch.** Lithuania is covered in Soviet-era drainage pipes that are silently failing. Farmers' fields flood, and municipalities get a small repair budget and no map of where it hurts. We use free Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 images plus AI to find waterlogging anomalies over mapped drainage systems. Then we add a twist: when a failing drain sits on **drained peat**, we flag it as a candidate for **rewetting** instead of repair. That saves the repair money and helps LT meet the Nature Restoration Law peatland target.

**Problem + deadline + evidence.**
- Radviliškis district municipality **published this exact problem as a GovTech challenge** (long-term monitoring of soil waterlogging and of land-reclamation systems using Sentinel imagery, ground measurements and AI) [V]. Its winner is not visible [?].
- Municipalities receive state money for drainage works via ŽŪA programmes [V].
- The Nature Restoration Regulation requires drained agricultural peat to be rewetted (30% by 2030), and the national plan was due in Sep 2026 [K].
- The share of LT land that is drained (often quoted at ~3 M ha) needs checking [?].

**What the tech does / why it collapses without it.** It compares each drained parcel's radar backscatter (a soil-moisture proxy) against its neighbours and against its own multi-year history, then flags persistent anomalies after rain. No inspector can walk thousands of km of drains, and a single photo doesn't show a *persistent anomaly*. It needs time-series analysis of radar data, which humans can't do by eye.

**Adoption loop.** Municipal melioration specialist → uses the ranked map to decide which repairs to fund this year (ŽŪA budget) → the farmer's complaint is resolved faster. Rewet candidates → Environment Ministry / APVA or LIFE peatland projects [?]. Why they'd bother: they asked for it publicly (the GovTech challenge). Delivery: a free web map/QGIS plugin, starting with Radviliškis.

**Where the tech is proven.** Sentinel-1 soil-moisture and flood mapping is operational (Copernicus EMS, ESA Soil Moisture products) [K]. KTU already built a real-time satellite mosaic of LT for GovTech [V]. Peat maps exist in LT soil and geology layers [?].

**Prior art.** The Radviliškis challenge suggests no off-the-shelf LT tool exists. Drainage-failure remote sensing is published research abroad (e.g. Nordic and Baltic tile-drain studies) [?]. The "repair vs rewet" decision layer appears new [?].

**Impact metric + pilot by Nov 6.**
- Metrics: hectares of waterlogged drained land detected; € of repair spending prioritised; ha of peat flagged for rewetting × an emissions factor (tCO₂e/ha/yr from IPCC wetlands guidance [K]).
- Pilot: run the model on one municipality (Radviliškis or the team's home district), pick 15–20 flagged and 10 control parcels, and visit them by bike/car with photos plus a simple soil-moisture probe. Report precision.
- Ask the municipality's melioration specialist to review the list and sign a letter.

**Demo.** A live map: drag a time slider through a wet spring and watch the red failing-drain parcels light up. Click one to see the radar time series, the "peat: yes" flag, and the recommendation "rewet: saves €X repair, Y tCO₂/yr". Then show a photo from the field visit of the actual flooded spot.

**Top 3 judge risks.**
1. *"Isn't this just Copernicus data?"* The data is free; the value is the parcel-level anomaly model, the drainage overlay and the fix-or-rewet decision. A municipality asked for it, and the challenge has no visible winner.
2. *"Do farmers want fields rewetted?"* Rewetting is voluntary and paid. We flag *candidates*, where repair is costly and the land is low-yield peat. The farmer decides.
3. *"Can high-schoolers process radar?"* Google Earth Engine / Copernicus Browser plus Python notebooks make it tractable. Keep it simple: a z-score of backscatter against neighbours, then a small classifier trained on field-visit labels.

**Self-scores (1–10).** Collapse 9 · Adoption 8 · Proven blocks 8 · Measurable 8 · Not saturated 9 · Demo-able 7 · Local evidence 8 (would be 9 once the drained-area stats are verified). **AI: yes; IoT: optional soil probe.** Theme: sustainability. Weakness: the "wow" is a map, not a gadget.

### P2. "HeatLeak": AI finds wasted heat in schools

**Pitch.** Schools in LT heat classrooms with the windows open and heat empty buildings over holidays. Cheap sensors (or recycled Galaxy phones) in every classroom plus an AI model spot the "radiator on, window open" signature and rank the worst rooms for the caretaker. This helps municipalities meet the EU rule to cut public-building energy use by 1.9% a year.

**Problem + deadline + evidence.** EED Art. 5 (public bodies −1.9%/yr) and Art. 6 (3% renovation) [K]. **Šiauliai municipality took exactly this problem (energy monitoring in educational institutions) to GovTech4All in 2024** [V]. EPBD building-automation duties [K].

**Tech / collapse.** A temperature + CO₂ + humidity sensor per room (ESP32, ~€15) plus the building heat-meter or ESO smart-meter feed. The model detects events: a sudden temperature/CO₂ drop while supply heat stays high means a window is open with heating on; occupancy (from CO₂) against heating on means an empty room being heated. A caretaker can't watch 40 rooms every 15 minutes; the patterns are invisible without the data.

**Adoption loop.** The school caretaker and director get a weekly "top 5 leaks" message. The municipal education/property department pays (it pays the heat bills and owns the 1.9% obligation). Budget: energy savings, plus KKP/APVA municipal energy measures [K]. Reach: one municipality's school network, starting with Šiauliai, which asked.

**Proven.** Open-window detection from temperature/CO₂ is used in commercial smart-TRV products (e.g. tado°, Netatmo "open window detection") [K]. School energy-feedback programmes (e.g. Euronet 50/50) show 5–10% savings from behaviour alone [K/?].

**Prior art.** Commercial building-management systems and energy dashboards. The differentiator is classroom-level *event* detection plus behaviour feedback in cheap retrofit hardware for old buildings without a building-management system. **Saturation risk: medium** (energy dashboards are common in student contests).

**Impact + pilot.** Pilot in the team's own school for 3–4 weeks (October is heating season): count open-window-while-heating events per room, run a feedback intervention in week 3 and compare. Convert to kWh and € using the building's heat meter. Real data by Nov 6.

**Demo.** A sensor box on stage. Open a "window" (a fan blowing in cold air) and the dashboard flags "Room 204: heating while window open, wasting ~X €/hour" live.

**Risks.** (1) "Dashboards exist": ours is a €15/room retrofit plus event AI, and it links to the EED 1.9% duty. (2) "Savings are small": heating is the largest energy cost in LT schools [?]; even 5% across a municipality is €10k+ [?, compute from real bills]. (3) "Who maintains it?": the municipality energy manager; recycled phones cut hardware cost.

**Self-scores.** Collapse 7 · Adoption 8 · Proven 9 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 5 · Demo 9 · Local evidence 7. **IoT + AI.**

### P3. "CanopyZero": tree-canopy baseline and felling watch for the Nature Restoration Law

**Pitch.** By 2030 every EU city must show *no net loss* of tree canopy compared with 2024. Lithuanian municipalities don't have a baseline. Our AI maps every tree crown from open orthophotos and LiDAR, sets the 2024 baseline, and each year flags where canopy disappeared. It cross-checks those losses against published felling permits, so unpermitted cuts surface automatically.

**Problem + deadline.** NRL Art. 8 urban-ecosystem obligation [K]. Tree-felling controversies in Vilnius and Kaunas are recurrent news [? — no specific article collected here]. Orthophotos and LiDAR on geoportal.lt [?].

**Tech / collapse.** Semantic segmentation of tree crowns (e.g. U-Net, or the Meta/WRI canopy-height models) plus change detection across years. Manual counting of tens of thousands of trees per city is impossible.

**Adoption.** Municipal urban-greenery departments must report canopy trends to the Environment Ministry for the NRL; citizen groups use the felling alerts. Budget: the NRL implementation, which must be funded somehow [K].

**Proven.** Copernicus Urban Atlas Street Tree Layer (2012/2018) [K]; the Meta/WRI 1 m global canopy height map (2024) [K]; i-Tree [K].

**Risks.** Copernicus may produce the official baseline, which makes this redundant (mitigation: higher resolution, annual, plus permit matching). Validating the model before Nov 6 is doable on one neighbourhood.

**Self-scores.** Collapse 9 · Adoption 7 · Proven 9 · Measurable 8 · Not saturated 7 · Demo 8 · Local evidence 6 (needs local news and data verified).

### P4. "BinEye": contamination camera on refuse trucks for bio-waste and packaging

**Pitch.** LT is at risk of missing the EU's 2025 55% recycling target [V]. Contaminated bio-waste and packaging bins ruin whole truckloads. A camera on the truck hopper classifies each bin as it is tipped, links it to the address via GPS, and triggers friendly feedback to the worst offenders. It's one device per truck, not per bin.

**Proven.** Prairie Robotics (Canada) and similar systems run truck-mounted contamination cameras [K]. Greyparrot does AI waste recognition at sorting plants [K].

**Adoption.** Regional waste-management centres (RATC) / municipal waste operators, which are paid on quality and face the targets. **Pilot problem:** students can't easily mount cameras on trucks by Nov 6. A proxy pilot is a camera over the school or apartment-block bio-waste container lid, measuring the contamination % before and after feedback.

**Risks.** Privacy (address-level feedback), a GDPR-sensitive area. Saturation among "AI sorts waste" student apps is high (judges may lump it in). The truck partnership is uncertain.

**Self-scores.** Collapse 8 · Adoption 7 · Proven 8 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 5 · Demo 7 · Local evidence 8.

### P5. "SchoolGate Counter": evidence engine for safe, active school trips (sport-theme option)

**Pitch.** Kids driven to school rose by 200% over 30 years, and 182 children were injured within 100 m of schools in 2022–25 [V]. An edge-AI camera at the gate counts drop-off cars, walkers and cyclists and near-miss conflicts. It gives the municipality the numbers it needs to justify a "school street", and gives the school a measurable active-travel KPI.

**Proven.** Telraam (a low-cost citizen traffic counter, Belgium; used across the EU) [K]; Vivacity (UK) for near-miss analytics [K].

**Risks.** Prior art exists for counting (the novelty is only the near-miss + active-travel framing); privacy of filming children (use on-device processing, keep no video); the tech is partly a counter.

**Self-scores.** Collapse 7 · Adoption 7 · Proven 9 · Measurable 9 · Not saturated 6 · Demo 9 · Local evidence 8.
