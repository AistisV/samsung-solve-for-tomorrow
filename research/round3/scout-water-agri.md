## 2. Top profiles

### #1: DrainScope (working name): "Satellites find the broken drainage that 52 municipal specialists can't walk"

**One-line pitch:** An AI that watches Lithuania's 2.6 million drained hectares from space and tells each municipality's melioration specialist which fields have failing drains or beaver floods this month, and which failing peat fields are cheaper to **rewet** than to rebuild.

**Problem (LT evidence)**
- Drainage built in the 1960s–80s: ≥75% worn. ŽŪM says 74.4% of state structures are depreciated, and 2 M of 2.5 M ha is in very poor condition. The official Mel_DR10LT dataset flags only 171k ha as "poor". **The state's own map is 10× more optimistic than its own ministry.** (Sources in §0.)
- 51,000 km of state ditches are maintained by municipalities, which have one melioration specialist or a small team each. Inspections are legally required at least **every 3 years**. Beavers dam up to 1/5 of ditches in some municipalities.
- Money is tiny next to a €1 B need. For example, LRT reported municipalities competing for a **€1 M** reconstruction call ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/verslas/4/2799258/savivaldybes-kvieciamos-pretenduoti-i-1-mln-euru-melioracijos-statiniu-rekonstravimui)). So **prioritising** where to spend is the whole problem.
- The green angle: failing drainage on **peat soils** is where rewetting (Nature Restoration Regulation, ≥30% of drained agricultural peat by 2030, LT plan due 2026) beats reconstruction. Waterlogged mineral fields also lose nitrogen through surface runoff (LT agriculture = 56% of the N load). Every euro spent rebuilding drainage on land that should be rewetted is wasted twice.

**What the tech does**
1. Pulls free Copernicus **Sentinel-1 SAR** (sees through cloud, every ~6 days) and **Sentinel-2** optical data over the drained-land polygons from Mel_DR10LT (open data).
2. For each drained field it models how quickly surface wetness clears after rain, compared with neighbouring fields on the same soil after the same rain event. **Persistent anomalies** (pooling longer than 7 days: the same threshold the law uses) are flagged as probable drain or outlet failure.
3. A second detector looks along state ditches for **beaver ponds** (sudden new open water or flooding upstream of a point, as in the EEAGER and IJGI 2025 deep-learning work).
4. It overlays soil type (peat vs. mineral) and gives a **triage label**: *repair outlet / clear ditch / reconstruct (grant-eligible) / candidate for rewetting (restoration plan)*.
5. Output: a phone map for the specialist, with a ranked list and a route. The specialist taps "confirmed / false alarm" in the field, and that feedback retrains the model (active learning).

**Collapse test:** Remove the satellites and AI and you are back to one person, 2.6 M ha and a 3-year cycle, which is exactly today's failure. Humans can't compare wetness decay across thousands of fields after every rain. This is not a wrapper.

**Adoption loop:** *Municipal melioration specialist* (user) → gets a monthly ranked list instead of walking blind → inspects the top 20 flagged sites, documents them, and uses the evidence for **state co-funded (up to 90%) maintenance** and for the farmer's **CAP reconstruction grant (up to €300k at 65%)**. **Who pays:** the municipality from its melioration budget, or ŽŪM / ŽŪDC as a national layer, because it would fix Mel_DR10LT. **Reach:** municipalities already share one national dataset (ŽŪDC), so one pilot municipality leads to a ŽŪDC data layer. **First partner:** **Kauno rajono savivaldybė** (its official publicly raised the 73% figure and it has a dedicated melioration fund, per LRT) or Marijampolė (earmarks part of its taxes for melioration, [infoerdve](https://infoerdve.lt/marijampoles-savivaldybe-kuria-tvaru-sprendima-dalis-mokesciu-gris-melioracijai/)). A letter from the municipal melioration specialist is realistic.

**Proven building blocks**
- Waterlogging detection from Sentinel-2 indices + clustering: [Water 2023, 15(7):1340](https://doi.org/10.3390/w15071340). Systematic review of remote sensing for subsurface drainage: [Agric. Water Mgmt 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378377424002270). Drainage-condition assessment by remote sensing: [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283620400_Use_of_Remote_Sensing_for_Identification_and_Description_of_Subsurface_Drainage_System_Condition). Deep learning for drain pipes: [arXiv 2210.02071](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02071).
- Beaver detection: EEAGER CNN, trained on 13,344 dams ([JGR Biogeosciences 2023](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2022JG007196)). Beaver-induced floodplain inundation via deep learning ([IJGI 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijgi14100383)). Ontario beaver ponds at 72.5% accuracy (from a search snippet).
- Sentinel-1 flood / water mapping is operational worldwide (Copernicus EMS).

**Prior art and how we differ:** US and Canadian research maps *where* tile drains are, or maps beavers for *restoration*. LT's Mel_DR10LT is updated manually from orthophotos and plans ([ŽŪDC](https://zudc.lt/meldr_10lt/)). I found **no** operational tool that (a) scores drainage *failure* over time and (b) triages it against rewetting obligations. This combination is the novelty. **[Check]:** ask ŽŪDC whether it runs any satellite wetness analysis.

**Impact metric and pilot (doable by Nov 6)**
- Pick one municipality (or one eldership). Run the model on autumn 2026 imagery (the Sept–Oct rains make this the ideal season).
- Field-verify the top 30 flagged sites and 15 random unflagged "control" sites with a phone and a small drone. Metric: **precision of flags** (target ≥60%) and **hit rate vs. random** (for example, 3–5× more failures found per inspection hour).
- Headline numbers: hectares of failing drainage found that Mel_DR10LT does not list; beaver-flooded ditch km; hectares of failing peat drainage proposed for rewetting, with t CO₂e/yr via IPCC emission factors (cite the IPCC Wetlands Supplement default for drained cropland on organic soil; **the exact factor still needs checking**).
- Scale metric: inspector-hours per detected failure, before vs. after.

**Demo:** A live map of the pilot municipality. A slider over rain events shows fields that dry out and fields that stay wet (SAR time-lapse). Click a red field: before/after imagery, the drone photo the team took of the actual pond or broken outlet, and the triage label. Wow moment: show a field the official map calls "good" next to our drone video of it under water. Hardware add-on: a €30 water-level logger (ultrasonic + LoRa) at one ditch culvert as ground truth.

**Top 3 judge risks**
1. *"Cloud cover in Lithuania."* Answer: Sentinel-1 SAR is cloud-independent. Sentinel-2 is used only when clear.
2. *"10 m pixels are too coarse for a drain line."* Answer: we don't map pipes. We detect the **symptom** (a waterlogged patch of ≥0.1 ha that persists), which is exactly what the law says to report.
3. *"Isn't this just helping intensive farming?"* Answer: the triage explicitly recommends **rewetting** failing peat systems (Nature Restoration Regulation), and it stops public money being spent rebuilding drains where nature restoration is cheaper and better for the climate.

**Self-score:** Collapse 9 · Adoption loop 8 · Proven blocks 8 · Measurable 8 · Not saturated 9 · Demo 8 · Local evidence 9. **Bonus: AI (core), IoT (optional logger).**

### #2: ManureWatch: "Sentinel-2 spots slurry on the fields, so inspectors raid the right farm"

**Pitch:** A satellite model that flags fields where manure or slurry was just spread during the ban period (Nov 15 – Mar 20), on waterlogged or frozen ground, or next to rivers. It gives the AAD a daily target list instead of random raids.

**Problem:** LT agriculture = 56% of the N load and 42% of the P load. The whole country is a Nitrates-Directive vulnerable zone. The AAD checks only about 170 operators per campaign and finds violations in ~26% of them, so enforcement is blind sampling (sources in §0).

**Tech:** Sentinel-2 bare-soil spectral indices (EOMI-type, SWIR/NIR shifts after organic amendment) plus a before/after change classifier per LPIS field parcel, masked by the ban calendar, water-body buffers and weather (frozen or snow days from LHMT).

**Collapse test:** Inspectors can't see 1.8 M ha of arable land every 5 days. Without satellites there is no targeting. Passes.

**Adoption loop:** AAD regional inspectors (user) → get a morning list of "fresh manure signatures on forbidden days" → a targeted raid → higher hit rate and a deterrent. Deployed by AAD / Aplinkos ministerija, with Copernicus data free. Reach: a single national agency, so one yes means national coverage.

**Proven:** Emilia-Romagna study co-authored with regional agency ARPAE, which developed new indices for manure on bare soil ([Sensors 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24144687)). France Versailles plain: EOMI indices ([Remote Sens. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13091616)). A method with F1 ≈ 0.9 on freshly manured plots ([Ecol. Informatics 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1574954123000353)). Slurry on emerging wheat ([Geocarto 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10106049.2023.2245371)). Pasture dataset ([PMC9730142](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730142)).

**Prior art / difference:** research only, with no operational national system found in LT, and in Italy only research-stage **[unverified whether ARPAE now runs it operationally]**.

**Pilot by Nov 6:** Legal spreading is still happening in Sept–Oct. Detect fresh-manure signatures in one district, then drive out within 1–3 days to verify (visual and smell, photo) → precision/recall on 40–60 fields. Also run a retrospective scan of the Mar 1–20 2026 ban window, reported as "suspected events" only (not accusations).

**Demo:** Live map; click a field to see its spectral curve jump after spreading, next to the team's ground photo.

**Risks:** (1) Winter clouds and snow cover most of the ban period. The real value is the shoulder weeks (early Nov, mid-Mar) and the "waterlogged ground" rule; SAR can't see manure. (2) Slurry injection is invisible; this is honest scope. (3) The farmer-policing image and data protection: position it as risk-based targeting, where the human inspector decides.

**Self-score:** C 9 · A 7 · P 8 · M 7 · S 9 · D 7 · L 8. AI core.

### #3: FishPass Counter: "An AI that counts every salmon climbing Lithuania's fish ladders"

**Pitch:** An underwater camera plus an edge-AI box (Samsung phone or Raspberry Pi + YOLO) that counts and identifies migrating fish in fish passes 24/7. It replaces people watching hours of video and gives the first real efficiency numbers for LT's fish passes.

**Problem:** Dams are a named reason LT rivers fail WFD good status, and they block salmon and smelt spawning ([BNS](https://sc.bns.lt/view/item/uztvankos-lietuvoje-trukdo-ne-tik-lasisoms-dalies-nerstavieciu-nepasiekia-ir-ziobriai-458561)). There are 25 fish passes. The legal efficiency test is >90% passing = efficient, <75% = not ([VU thesis](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:193291559/193291559.pdf)). The official method is video that humans count ([e-seimas](https://e-seimas.lrs.lt/rs/legalact/TAD/TAIS.262038/)). There is a fish-pass evaluation report from Gamtos tyrimų centras for AAA ([PDF](https://aaa.lrv.lt/uploads/aaa/documents/files/Zuvitakio+vertinimo+galutine+ataskaita.pdf)).

**Collapse test:** Counting thousands of hours of murky video by species is what humans do badly and rarely, which is why monitoring is sparse. Passes, but it is closer to "automation" than to "impossible".

**Adoption loop:** AAA / Žuvininkystės tarnyba / hydro operators (legally required to keep passes working) → install one box per pass → continuous counts → evidence for fixing or removing barriers (EU Nature Restoration Law free-flowing rivers target). Who pays: AAA monitoring budget and the hydro operators.

**Proven:** Commercial Riverwatcher / Vaki counters (Norway/Iceland). Many YOLO fish-detection papers. **[Links still to add: search budget was exhausted.]**

**Pilot:** Salmon and sea trout migrate upstream mainly Oct–Nov, **which matches the timeline**. It needs permission for one fish pass (for example on the Šventoji or Neris tributaries). Fallback: train on existing footage from AAA or the Riverwatcher public galleries.

**Risks:** Access and permission, murky water, and existing commercial products (so the novelty is a cheap DIY + open model + LT deployment). Is LT *currently* using video at all 25 passes? **[unverified]**.

**Self-score:** C 7 · A 6 · P 9 · M 7 · S 7 · D 8 · L 7.

## 3. Recommendation

**Strongest pick: #1 DrainScope (drainage-failure and beaver-flood triage with a rewet option).** It is the only idea here that hits every hard criterion hard:
- It solves a problem **the state admits it can't see** (171k ha "poor" officially vs. 2 M ha per ŽŪM).
- It has a **named legal user and legal trigger**: municipal specialists, 3-yearly inspection, the ">7 days pooling" reporting rule, new rules in 2026.
- It uses **free, proven data and methods**.
- It gives the team **real pilot data in October**, the rainy season, and a drone is enough for ground truth.
- It links to two big policy files, the **CAP reconstruction grants** and the **EU Nature Restoration Regulation (LT plan due 2026)**, so it could plausibly "influence local policy".
- The global story transfers to every post-Soviet and US Midwest drained landscape.
