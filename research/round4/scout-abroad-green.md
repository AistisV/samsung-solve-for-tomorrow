## 2. Full profiles

### #1 DušoSargas (ShowerCoach): "your shower, in euros, live" (TOP PICK)

**Pitch:** A €15 device that clips onto any shower hose and shows, as you shower, how many litres and euro cents you are using. In Switzerland the same idea cut shower energy by 22%, and we built the Lithuanian version with AI coaching for the whole family.

**Relatable problem + LT evidence**
- Every teenager has been yelled at for a too-long shower. It is a 10-second problem for a jury.
- Hot water in Vilnius cost **€7.92/m³** in 2025, and the price changes monthly with the heat price [verified: [housecare.lt](https://www.housecare.lt/2025/10/24/karsto-vandens-kaina-vilniuje-2025/)].
- A shower uses **10–12 L/min**, and 8–10 minutes is enough [verified: [ve.lt](https://ve.lt/gyvenimas/laisvalaikis/atskleide-kiek-laiko-vertetu-praustis-duse-daugelis-to-nezino), [jp.lt](https://jp.lt/6-paslaptys-kaip-taisyklingai-praustis-duse/)].
- Klaipėda's actual average is **1.00 m³ of hot water per resident per month**. The billing norm is 1.5 m³ for the first person in a flat [verified: [vanduo.lt](https://www.vanduo.lt/vidutinis-vandens-suvartojimas/) snippet, [Ukmergės šiluma norms](https://www.ukmergessiluma.lt/vartotojams-normatyvai/)].
- Some councils' studies say real use is about double the norms [unverified: search summary, source unclear].
- 40% of Lithuanians already say they save water by "a short shower instead of a bath" [verified: [15min survey](https://www.15min.lt/verslas/naujiena/energetika/tyrimas-atskleide-kaip-ir-kodel-gyventojai-namuose-taupo-vandeni-664-2658886)]. They believe they're saving, but nobody measures.
- Teens: in a UK school survey of 356 pupils, the average shower was **20.5 min**, and **83%** showered longer than the UK average of 7.5 min [verified: [ech2o shower survey report](https://www.ech2o.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/Shower-Survey-Report.pdf) snippet].
- Heating and hot water are the biggest items in LT household energy spending, up to 80% [verified in round 3: `round3/scout-energy.md`, Klaipėdos energija].

**Proven model abroad**
- **amphiro** (ETH Zurich spin-off, Switzerland). Tiefenbeck et al. (2018): real-time shower feedback cut shower water and energy use by **22%** in a large Swiss field experiment [verified: [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336447549_Real-time_feedback_reduces_energy_consumption_among_the_broader_public_without_financial_incentives)].
- The savings **did not decay** over time in a longitudinal follow-up [verified: same search, [Cambridge BPP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/two-steps-forward-one-step-back-negative-spillovers-in-water-conservation/F72BF7C266515FC01DD5E19C23700263)].
- A hotel RCT (6 hotels, 19,596 showers) found **−11.4%** energy [verified: [Tandfonline 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10548408.2025.2497959)].
- A Swiss district study with social comparison [verified: [Energy Efficiency 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12053-024-10199-9)].
- One paper's headline is "cuts shower time by 20%" [verified: [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328841629_Shower_Feedback_in_Switzerland_water_conservation_device_provides_real-time_feedback_cuts_shower_time_by_20)].

**Prototype / live demo (2 minutes on stage)**
- An inline unit sits between the shower hose and the handle: a hall-effect flow sensor (YF-B-series), a DS18B20 temperature probe, an ESP32 and a small waterproof display (or LED ring).
- Stage demo: a real shower hose fed from a bucket and a small pump, or a tap. As water runs, the display counts litres, **euro cents** at the LT hot-water price, and a polar-bear/ice-floe animation that shrinks. That's amphiro's own trick; ours can use a Baltic stork or a melting icicle.
- At 5 minutes the LED turns orange and at 8 minutes red.
- A phone app (Galaxy) shows the family dashboard and this week's AI report.

**How AI/IoT is used, and why it's useful rather than bolted on**
- **IoT is the core.** Live feedback at the exact moment of use is *the mechanism* proven in Switzerland. A bill a month later doesn't work.
- **AI, part 1: who is showering?** A small classifier on flow and temperature patterns (flow rate, preferred temperature, time of day, duration) recognises family members, so each person gets personal feedback without logging in. The shower-time challenge between siblings is the engagement loop.
- **AI, part 2: one weekly report per person.** An LLM turns the data into one short tip in Lithuanian, e.g. "Your showers went from 14 to 9 min, which saved €3.10 this month. Try turning the water off while you shampoo".
- **Energy estimate.** kWh are computed from the temperature rise (flow × ΔT × 4.186), so the device works with any heating: district heating, gas or electric boiler.
- Be honest in the pitch: the AI is modest. IoT feedback is the part that works, and the contest bonus explicitly includes IoT.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6 (6 weeks)**
- Build 10–15 units (about €15–20 each).
- **Week 1–2 baseline:** the display is covered, so the device only logs.
- **Week 3–4 feedback:** the display is on.
- Report the % change in litres per shower, minutes and € per household. This copies the Swiss design (a baseline phase, then an intervention phase).
- Also run a school survey of 200+ students: "how long do you shower? how long do you *think* you shower?" Measured vs. guessed makes a strong slide.

**Audience and reach**
- Families with teenagers, reached through the school: a "borrow it for a month" loan from the school library or eco-club.
- Scale partners: municipal heat utilities (Vilniaus šilumos tinklai, Kauno energija, which already run energy-saving campaigns), water utilities and the ILTE/APVA renovation programmes. Apartment-block administrators can hand units out.

**One impact number:** litres (and €) per shower per household, before vs. after. The target to beat is the Swiss −22%.
Illustration only [calculation, not a source]: a family of 4 at ~1 m³ of hot water per person a month × €7.92 ≈ €32 a month in hot water. If showers are about half of that (assumption), −22% of showers saves about €3.5 a month per family.

**Top 3 jury objections + answers**
1. *"Amphiro already exists, so what's new?"*: "Exactly. It's proven, and it's not sold or used in Lithuania [unverified: not searched]. We built it for LT prices and our heating system, with a per-person AI coach. It's 3–5× cheaper than the imported device (amphiro retail about €60–90 [unverified])." Research into prior efforts is an official criterion, so turn it into a strength.
2. *"The savings are small."*: "Per family yes, but it scales. Multiply by the number of flats a utility serves." Also show your own pilot %. The Swiss result was bigger than typical bill-feedback programmes.
3. *"People will stop looking at it."*: "The Swiss longitudinal study found no decay [verified]. The per-person game and the weekly report keep it alive."

**Exists in LT?** No Lithuanian shower-feedback product or programme turned up (only generic "shower shorter" tip articles) [unverified absence: no dedicated search].

### #3 SortRight / "Rūšiuok iš karto": AI that lights up the right bin

**Pitch:** A camera over the school's existing bins recognises what you're holding and lights up the correct bin in a second. Poland's Bin-e does this commercially at 92% accuracy, research shows instant feedback makes people sort better, and we made a €60 version for every Lithuanian school.

**Relatable problem + LT evidence**
- Everyone hesitates at the bin ("is a greasy pizza box paper?").
- In the Vilnius region, **3,900 t of recyclables** were pulled out of *mixed* waste in one year (1,929 t glass, 279 t plastic, 1,084 t other metal, 22 t aluminium) [verified: [vilnijosnaujienos.lt](https://vilnijosnaujienos.lt/gyventojai-misrios-atliekos-stiklas-metalas-plastikas-kartonas/)].
- Later data from the region's plant: 3,765 t glass, 2,962 t paper/cardboard and 2,348 t plastic packaging sorted from mixed waste [verified: same search summary; the source article is unclear].
- Packaging and other recyclables made up about **one third** of LT mixed municipal waste in 2016–2021, and 240.2k t of recyclables sat in mixed waste in 2021 [verified: search summary; likely from the [LRT / State Audit Office article](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/lietuvoje/2/3041092/vk-rusiuojanciu-gyventoju-skaicius-isaugo-bet-didele-dalis-siuksliu-nera-perdirbama). Confirm before use].
- Food is about **15%** of LT mixed waste [verified: [atliekukultura.lt](https://egzaminas.atliekukultura.lt/lietuvoje-maisto-kiekis-atlieku-misriame-komunaliniu-atlieku-sraute-sudaro-apie-15-proc-kiek-vidutiniskai-maisto-atlieku-per-metus-ismeta-vienas-lietuvos-gyventojas/)].
- Proof that LT habits *can* change: the deposit system returns about 90% of containers (`local-data.md`).

**Proven model abroad**
- **Bin-e** (Poland): an AI smart bin that identifies waste and sorts it itself at **~92%** accuracy (some sources say >95%). It is marketed to offices, airports and schools [verified: [Recycling International](https://recyclinginternational.com/technology/technology-news/artificial-intelligence-sorting/2082/), [Waste360](https://www.waste360.com/waste-recycling/bin-e-creates-contactless-smart-bin)].
- **UBC study:** immediate feedback raised sorting accuracy from **69% to 84%**, and the effect held a week later [verified: [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/12/181211073255.htm), [PubMed 30502613](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30502613)].
- Green/red light feedback is reported to reduce contamination [verified: same search summary].
- A 2026 systematic review of bin design [verified: [Wiley EPR](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/epr2.70002)].

**Prototype / live demo**
- An ESP32-CAM or a Galaxy phone on a bracket above 3–4 standard coloured bins, with LED strips on each lid.
- On stage a jury member holds up an item (yoghurt cup, pizza box, battery, apple core). Within 1 second the correct bin glows, and a speaker says "Plastikas – bet išskalauk!" ("Plastic, but rinse it first!").
- A dashboard shows today's "wrong-bin catches".

**AI/IoT, and why it's useful**
- The AI is the whole product: it recognises the object in real time. Options are a fine-tuned small image model (YOLO / MobileNet on-device) trained on *Lithuanian* packaging, or a cloud vision model with an LT rules prompt.
- It follows LT municipal sorting rules, which differ by region (e.g. whether glass is collected separately).
- Unlike Bin-e (€ thousands, replaces the bins), ours retrofits the bins schools already have.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6**
- **Baseline week:** audit the contamination of 2 school bin stations (weigh or count wrong items).
- **2–3 weeks with the station** at one location vs. a control location, then re-audit. Reuse UBC's 69→84% design.
- Also log 1,000+ recognitions for an accuracy figure.

**Audience and reach**
- Schools first: canteens and corridors, with the eco-club running it.
- Then the regional waste centres (VAATC, and others that run the "Atliekų kultūra" education programme), municipalities and shopping centres.

**One impact number:** % of items in the wrong bin at the school station, before vs. after (e.g. "from 1 in 3 to 1 in 8").

**Top 3 jury objections**
1. *"Every science fair has a smart bin."*: this is the **biggest risk**. Round 3 killed the phone-photo version as saturated. Answer: "Ours is not an app you have to open. It's zero-effort, retrofits existing bins, is trained on LT packaging, and comes with measured contamination data from our school."
2. *"Why not just put up better signs?"*: "Signs are static. The UBC study shows *immediate* feedback is what teaches, and we measured the difference."
3. *"Cost and maintenance?"*: about €60 of hardware per station, and the eco-club maintains it.

**Exists in LT?** A few "which bin" apps and sorting quizzes exist (e.g. the Atliekų kultūra education site). No AI bin-feedback stations in LT schools were found [unverified].

### #2 Energijos detektyvai 50/50: school energy savings the students keep half of

**Pitch:** In 500+ European schools, students cut energy use by at least 8% and the school kept half the money. We brought the 50/50 model to Lithuania with €30 sensors and AI that catches waste nobody sees: the heating running all weekend, or lights on at midnight.

**Relatable problem + LT evidence**
- Students feel it daily: classrooms at 26°C with windows open in January, lights on in empty rooms, projectors left on overnight.
- CO₂ reaches 3,000–5,152 ppm in LT classrooms (round 3, `scout-energy.md`), which is the same airing-vs-heating problem.
- School energy bills are paid by the municipality, so students have no incentive. 50/50 fixes exactly that.
- No evidence was found that Euronet 50/50 ever ran in Lithuania [unverified: the Euronet search returned no LT partner].
- *Weak spot:* a strong LT number on school energy spending was not found. It needs a request to your own municipality (ask the school's administrator for 12 months of bills).

**Proven model abroad**
- **Euronet 50/50 / 50/50 MAX** (EU Intelligent Energy Europe): 500+ schools and about 50 other public buildings in 13 countries, with **minimum savings of 8% per building**, almost without investment. 50% of the saved money goes back to the school [verified: [Interreg Europe good practice](https://www.interregeurope.eu/good-practices/euronet-5050-max), [euronet50-50max.eu](http://www.euronet50-50max.eu/en/about-euronet-50-50-max/the-50-50-concept-saving-energy-and-money)].
- **Hamburg fifty/fifty** since the 1990s [unverified details].
- **Energy Sparks** (UK, open source): pupil dashboards and a school scoreboard. Damers First School cut electricity by **12%**. In one year 73% of the Aspire Academy Trust's schools reduced use, saving 15 t CO₂ and £20k [verified: [energysparks.uk case studies](https://energysparks.uk/case-studies), [Aspire](https://www.aspireacademytrust.org/225/news-2/post/1007/aspire-crowned-energy-sparks-mat-champion)]. There is also a UK government evaluation case study [verified: [gov.uk PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5fa9283dd3bf7f03aef812b9/18-007974-01-EnergySparks-evaluation-case-study.pdf)].

**Prototype / live demo**
- An ESP32 with a non-invasive current clamp (SCT-013) on a distribution board, or a smart-meter reading, plus room nodes (temperature, CO₂, light, window-open reed switch).
- A web dashboard shows the school's live power.
- On stage: plug a "forgotten" lamp or heater into the demo board. The dashboard spikes, the AI flags "Anomaly: 1.2 kW at 22:40 in room 204 (usual 0.1 kW)", and a Telegram/Galaxy notification goes to the eco-team.

**AI/IoT, and why it's useful**
- The AI learns the building's normal load profile (timetable-aware: lessons, weekends, holidays).
- It flags **out-of-hours baseload and heating that runs when nobody is there**. That is where the Euronet and Energy Sparks savings come from, and humans can't watch it 24/7.
- An LLM writes the weekly "detective report" for the class and the principal.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6**
- Install in your own school (or one wing) for 2 weeks of baseline, then 2–3 weeks of an eco-team campaign plus alerts.
- Report the % change in kWh, especially night and weekend baseload.
- Get a **letter of intent from the municipality's education or property department** agreeing to a 50/50 trial in 2027. That letter is the "clear plan" slide.

**Audience and reach:** municipalities (they own school buildings and pay the bills), the ŠMSM eco-schools network ("Žaliosios mokyklos") and later all 1,000+ LT schools [unverified count]. Most of the model is paperwork plus a dashboard, so it's easy to scale.

**One impact number:** % kWh saved in our school over 3 weeks, and € returned to the school under 50/50.

**Top 3 jury objections**
1. *"Is the problem relatable?"*: it's weaker than a shower. Lead with the student experience (hot rooms with open windows) and the "half the money goes to our school trip or equipment" hook.
2. *"Behaviour change fades."*: Euronet reported a minimum of 8% per building across a multi-year project. The alerts are automatic, so they don't depend on motivation.
3. *"Will the municipality really pay?"*: it's a proven EU scheme with ready methodology. Only the LOI is needed for the pilot.

**Exists in LT?** Energy-efficiency renovation projects for schools exist (EU-funded modernisation). No 50/50 incentive scheme or pupil energy dashboard was found [unverified].

### #4 Šilumos detektyvas: borrow a thermal camera and AI shows you where your heat escapes

**Pitch:** In the UK, libraries lend thermal cameras so families can find drafts [unverified]. We built a school-library kit: a phone thermal camera plus AI that marks every leak on the photo and tells you which €5 fix saves the most.

**Relatable problem + LT evidence**
- The heating bill is the big winter shock in LT homes: heating and hot water are up to 80% of household energy spending.
- About 38,000 old apartment blocks exist, and renovation runs at about 359 blocks a year (round 3, `scout-energy.md`, [verified there]).
- So most families will live in unrenovated flats for years, and **cheap fixes** (sealing windows, radiator reflector foil, door draught strips) are what they can actually do.

**Proven model abroad**
- UK councils and libraries run thermal imaging camera loan schemes, e.g. via the Centre for Sustainable Energy in Bristol and many local climate groups [unverified: not searched; verify before use].
- **Weakest "proof numbers" of the four:** no evaluated % saving was found. Use it as a backup, or merge it into a kit with #1 (idea #23).

**Prototype / live demo:** a Galaxy phone with a clip-on thermal camera (e.g. a FLIR One or InfiRay P2 class device, about €200–300 [unverified price]). Photograph a window frame on stage (or a prop with a cold-air fan). The AI outlines the leak zones, estimates the ΔT and gives a ranked fix list with € a season.

**AI/IoT:** segmentation of cold spots and matching them to building elements (window seal, balcony door, radiator niche), then a € estimate from ΔT, area and the local heat price. It's useful because a raw thermal picture confuses laypeople. The AI turns it into an action.

**Mini-pilot:** audit 20 classmates' flats, do the fixes and compare a before/after thermal ΔT plus room temperature over 2 weeks (bill data takes too long).

**Audience and reach:** public libraries (a large municipal network), block administrators, and the heat utilities' energy-advice programmes.

**One impact number:** °C improvement at the leak points and estimated € a season per flat.

**Top objections:** (1) "A thermal camera already does this." The answer is the AI fix ranking in € for LT prices. (2) "No proven % saving." Admit it and show your own pilot data. (3) "Camera cost." One kit per school library serves 30+ families a year.

**Exists in LT?** Energy auditors do professional thermal imaging. No library or school loan scheme was found [unverified].

## 4. Recommendation
1. **ShowerCoach (#1)** is the most "winning-formula" idea in this scout:
   - an instantly relatable teen/family problem;
   - a peer-reviewed −22% result abroad to adapt;
   - a cheap device that works live on stage with water running;
   - a real before/after family pilot that fits in 4 weeks;
   - an LT € price hook.

   Weak spot: the AI is modest (IoT feedback is the core). Make the per-person recognition and the weekly coach solid.
2. **SortRight (#3):** strongest AI and demo, but high saturation risk. Only choose it if the team can show measured contamination data.
3. **Energy Detectives 50/50 (#2):** best deployment and policy story (municipal 50/50 deal), and the AI is genuinely useful. It's less emotional, so pair it with student-experience framing.
4. Backup: **Heat-leak kit (#4)**, or merge #1 + #4 into a "Home Energy Detective kit" lent through school libraries (#23), if the jury would favour breadth. For a 2-minute pitch, one sharp device is better.
