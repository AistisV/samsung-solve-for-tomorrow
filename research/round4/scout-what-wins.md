**Meta-observations**
1. **The Baltic jury differs from Milan.** Baltic winners were *school-life apps* (BeePeace, Vilnius Lyceum, LaKiKe, Eduwave), shaped by that year's theme. Milan and the US reward *hardware + a named user + AI*. The Baltic winner can go on to the European stage (Minova did) [prev-verified]. So **the best entry is school-relatable for Riga/Vilnius and has a device for Milan**.
2. **Vilnius Lyceum placed twice (2023 2nd, 2024 1st)** [verified]. Repeat schools learn the format: a polished pitch, a clear plan.
3. **Samsung itself pushes prototypes.** The UK added a physical-prototyping session this year, and in the US 80% of projects were AI/IoT/drones/3D printing [verified].
4. **Health and inclusion beat gamification** in every jury found. No gamified step app appears among the winners in 2023–2026.
5. **"Our town's specific problem" wins in the US** (Huff's named creek water, Reno wildfire). That matches the local-relevance criterion here.

## 2. Top profiles

Selection: #1, #2, #3 and #4 (sustainability, which the team prefers; all can be piloted in the heating season, now) and #5 (the strongest *demo* and the Milan-style "named user + wearable" pattern; sport). #6 is left to round 3, and #9 is vetoed.

### 2.1 Klasės oras: an AI airing coach for classrooms (16/18)
- **Pitch:** "Every student knows the 7th-lesson brain fog. We measured our classrooms and built a €15 device plus AI that says *when* to open the window and for *how many minutes*, so the air is fresh without heating the street."
- **Relatable problem + LT evidence:** stuffy classrooms in winter; windows either stay shut (high CO₂, sleepy) or stay open (heat lost). CO₂ above roughly 1,000–1,400 ppm is linked to worse concentration [unverified: e.g. Harvard COGfx, Allen et al. 2016]. Many renovated LT schools are airtight but lack mechanical ventilation [unverified]. Check the Lithuanian hygiene norm HN 21 for school air requirements [unverified]. **The strongest local evidence will be the team's own measurements** (that is the pilot).
- **Proven model abroad:** in 2021 the UK DfE gave CO₂ monitors to state schools (~300k+) [unverified]; Germany's UBA airing guidance ("Stoßlüften", e.g. 20-5-20) [unverified]; **Aranet4, a CO₂ monitor made by Latvia's SAF Tehnika**, is widely used [unverified]. That is a Baltic hook. Pattern W2/W6: AsthmaSense (air data → warning) and Treeva (AI → energy saved) [verified].
- **Prototype / 2-minute demo:** an ESP32 + SCD40 CO₂ sensor + LED ring in a 3D-printed case. On stage: a presenter breathes into it or holds it near the jury. The ring turns amber, then red, and the phone app says "Open the window for 4 min (outside +2 °C)". Then show a slide of *our* school's week: CO₂ curves per room.
- **AI/IoT:** IoT nodes stream data to a dashboard. A small model learns each room's CO₂ rise per lesson (occupancy and timetable) and its cool-down per minute of airing at a given outside temperature. It then recommends the *shortest* airing that brings CO₂ below the threshold, and predicts which lessons will go over. It's useful because a teacher can't do this physics in their head.
- **Mini-pilot by Nov 6:** 4–6 classrooms × 3 weeks. Week 1 is a baseline with no advice; weeks 2–3 add advice. Measure the minutes above 1,000 ppm, a room-temperature proxy for heat loss, and a 5-question alertness survey (target n ≥ 150 students). Get a letter from the director or the municipal education department.
- **Audience and reach:** school directors and caretakers, run through the municipality (the school owner, which pays the heating). A kit costs about €15–25 per room [unverified: component prices]. It scales room by room.
- **One impact number:** "% of lesson time above 1,000 ppm: from X% to Y%" (plus kWh of heat not wasted, estimated).
- **Jury objections:**
  1. *"CO₂ monitors already exist."* Yes, and they only beep. Ours tells you how long to air the room, per room and per the weather, and proves the effect with our data. Adapting a proven model is the point.
  2. *"Is this really sustainability?"* Heating is most of a school's energy bill [unverified]. Airing by the rule instead of all day cuts heat loss, and we show the temperature data.
  3. *"Will teachers follow it?"* The pilot measures compliance. The LED is ambient, with no app required, and the school gets a weekly one-line report.

### 2.2 Dūmų radaras: smoke-season early warning for asthmatic kids and PE (16/18)
- **Pitch:** "Every October our streets fill with chimney smoke. For a kid with asthma that's a trigger. We built cheap sensors and an AI that predicts tonight's smoke hours, so families and PE teachers can plan around them."
- **Problem + LT evidence:** PM2.5 spikes every heating season in small towns and private-house districts; wet wood and burnt waste are key causes [prev-verified: NVSC via Jonavos žinios, `local-data.md`]. Childhood asthma prevalence in LT: find an NVSC/Higienos institutas figure [unverified].
- **Proven model:** **AsthmaSense (Desmond College, Ireland) won SfT UK & IE 2025/26 (14–16)** with an air-quality early warning that predicts asthma attacks [verified]. Airly (PL) dense low-cost PM networks [unverified]. Guardian Grid (US 2026) shows that "AI sees smoke → alert" wins nationally [verified].
- **Prototype / demo:** an ESP32 + PMS5003/SPS30 PM sensor node. On stage: light a match or incense near it, the reading jumps, and the phone gets the alert "Smoke episode, keep the inhaler handy / run indoors". A map shows our 5 nodes and last week's evening peaks.
- **AI/IoT:** a forecast model combining the sensor history, weather (temperature, wind, inversion at dusk; free weather API) and time of day predicts the next 12 h of PM2.5 per street. It's useful because the smoke is *predictable* (cold, calm evenings), yet nobody tells families.
- **Mini-pilot by Nov 6:** 5 nodes (at school, at team members' homes in a private-house district) × 4 weeks from the start of the heating season. Compare with the nearest official AAA station [unverified: station coverage]. Survey the school about asthma and allergies (anonymous, n ≥ 150), and have 5–10 families use the alerts. Test forecast accuracy with our own data.
- **Audience and reach:** families of asthmatic children, school nurses, PE teachers, then the municipality's environment department (the data can target heating-advice campaigns).
- **One impact number:** "the forecast catches X% of smoke episodes ≥ 3 h ahead" plus "Y exposure hours avoided by pilot families".
- **Jury objections:**
  1. *"Official stations exist."* There are few, and they sit in big cities; smoke is hyper-local street by street. We show the difference with our own data.
  2. *"Are cheap sensors accurate?"* We co-locate with an official station for calibration, and we need trends, not lab-grade values.
  3. *"Does it cut pollution, or only warn?"* Phase 2 gives the municipality a street-level heat-map for a targeted "dry wood" campaign. That is the policy link.

### 2.3 Energijos detektyvai 50/50: AI hunts the school's wasted electricity (15/18)
- **Pitch:** "At 22:00 our school is empty but still using power. We find out where, fix it, and the school gives half the saved money back to students."
- **Problem + LT evidence:** schools are municipal buildings. The EU Energy Efficiency Directive requires public bodies to cut energy use about 1.9%/yr [unverified]. The team's *own measured night/weekend base load* is the evidence.
- **Proven model:** Treeva (AI cuts device energy) won SfT UK 2024 [verified]. Euronet 50/50 and Hamburg's fifty/fifty schools programme, where the school keeps 50% of savings, across hundreds of schools [unverified].
- **Prototype / demo:** a CT-clamp logger on the main board + 5 smart plugs (projector, vending machine, PC lab). On stage: plug in a "forgotten" device, the dashboard flags it as an anomaly, and the AI names the probable culprit ("PC lab, standby since 16:10").
- **AI/IoT:** anomaly detection on the load curve (expected vs actual by timetable), plus disaggregation of which device class causes a step. Useful because nobody reads a 15-minute meter curve.
- **Mini-pilot by Nov 6:** 2 weeks of baseline, then 2 weeks of fixes (switch-off rota, timers). Measure kWh and €. Get a letter from the director and the municipal property manager agreeing to a 50/50 trial.
- **Audience and reach:** school directors, municipal education and property departments. Replicable to every municipal school.
- **One impact number:** "night/weekend base load −X%" → €Y/yr per school.
- **Objections:** (1) *"This is a caretaker's job."* Nobody does it, and the 50/50 gives students a reason to. (2) *"Savings are small."* We show the measured €, scaled to all the municipality's schools. (3) *"Smart plugs are off-the-shelf."* The AI layer and the incentive model are what's new, and the proof is our data.

### 2.4 Antras telefono gyvenimas: drawer phones become school sensors (15/18)
- **Pitch:** "Half of Lithuanians keep an old phone in a drawer. We turn them into air and noise stations for classrooms, and collect the rest for recycling."
- **Problem + LT evidence:** 53% of Lithuanians keep old phones in a drawer, only 9% recycle, and LT misses the EU 65% e-waste collection target [prev-verified: LRT, `local-data.md`].
- **Proven model:** Liova (FR) won in Milan 2025 with a power bank built from old devices' components [verified]. Samsung's own "Galaxy Upcycling" programme [unverified] is a Samsung-native story.
- **Demo:** an old Android + USB CO₂/PM sensor running our app as a room station (it naturally powers #1 or #2), and a collection-drive counter.
- **AI:** the phone runs on-device inference (CO₂ forecast / smoke detection); a phone-model/battery-health triage decides reuse vs recycle.
- **Mini-pilot:** a school collection drive over 3 weeks (count phones, kg), with 5 phones deployed as stations.
- **Impact number:** phones collected per 100 students, and € saved vs new sensors.
- **Objections:** old-phone batteries (handled: we remove the battery and run on USB power); security (factory reset protocol); "is it a gimmick?" (it's a delivery vehicle for #1/#2, so **use it as the hardware story of #1 or #2, not as a standalone idea**).

### 2.5 Švilpukas ant riešo: the AI whistle wristband for deaf players (14/18, best demo, sport)
- **Pitch:** "A deaf basketball player keeps playing after the whistle and gets a foul. Our wristband *hears* any referee's whistle with on-device AI and buzzes within a fraction of a second. No transmitter, no special whistle."
- **Problem + LT evidence:** deaf and hard-of-hearing students in mainstream PE and clubs miss whistles; this is documented abroad (a water-polo player was ejected for not hearing the whistle, which inspired eWhistle) [verified: Patient Innovation]. LT: basketball culture; Lithuanian deaf sport organisations and the Vilnius deaf education centre as partners [unverified: names and medals must be checked].
- **Proven model:** eWhistle (2016), NC School for the Deaf's Enforcer Whistle (LED on the hoops), an ESP8266 wristband paper, "Ref for the Deaf" (a teen, 2008) [verified]. All of them need the **referee's** whistle to be replaced or paired. Ours doesn't, which is the reason for the AI.
- **Prototype / 2-minute demo:** an ESP32-S3 / Arduino Nano 33 BLE + microphone + vibration motor + LED in a wristband. On stage: play crowd noise from a speaker, blow a real whistle, and a judge wearing the band feels it. Talking and clapping don't trigger it.
- **AI:** a TinyML audio classifier (Edge Impulse) trained on whistles vs gym noise. It's genuinely necessary because simple frequency thresholds false-trigger on sneaker squeaks.
- **Mini-pilot by Nov 6:** a lab test of 200 whistles + 200 noise clips (accuracy and latency), then one training session with a deaf or hard-of-hearing team or students. Measure reaction time with and without the band.
- **Audience and reach:** deaf-sport clubs, deaf schools, mainstream PE teachers with hard-of-hearing students; later also noisy workplaces.
- **Impact number:** "reaction to the whistle: X s → Y s" and "missed whistles per game: X → 0".
- **Objections:** (1) *"The audience is small."* Inclusion wins (VisionNex, InSightEd, Poseidon), and it transfers to any hearing-impaired PE student. (2) *"It already exists."* Existing systems need referee hardware; ours works with any ordinary whistle. (3) *"Theme fit and the team's link?"* The fit is Sport & tech inclusion. **Team authenticity is weak unless the team knows a deaf athlete**, so find that contact first (a go/no-go test).

## 3. Summary
- **What wins:** (W1) a named vulnerable user + wearable; (W2) a local hazard + cheap sensor + early warning; (W3) a school-wellbeing app with a safe channel (the Baltic jury favourite); (W4) AI personalised learning/PE; (W5) a circular or energy object you can hold; (W6) AI cutting a building's waste in €; (W7) a physical hook; (W8) a visible pilot/partner/policy path. No gamified step app won anywhere in 2023–26.
- **The Baltic jury picks school-life apps, while Milan and the US pick hardware + named user + AI**, so aim for an entry that is both.
- **Top picks:** **#1 Klasės oras** and **#2 Dūmų radaras** (both 16/18, sustainability, piloted in the heating season we're in now, the device reacts live on stage; #4 old phones can serve as their hardware story). Next are **#3 Energijos detektyvai 50/50** (15) and the sport wildcard **#5 Švilpukas ant riešo** (14, best 2-minute demo).
- **Verify before use:** every [unverified] item in §2, especially the UK CO₂ monitor programme, HN 21, childhood asthma prevalence in LT, the Aranet/SAF link, Euronet 50/50 and the LT deaf-sport partners.
