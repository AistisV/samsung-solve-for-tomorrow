## 0. Bottom line

The strongest teen-life sport/health ideas are **safety and injury ideas with a physical gadget**. The jury feels them straight away ("my teammate sprained her ankle three times", "would you know what to do if a player collapsed?"). Each has a **published trial or national programme abroad** with a hard number, and each gets a **physical prototype that a jury member can touch on stage**. That is closer to the pan-European 2025 winners (a smart shoe for diabetics, AI glasses) than to the saturated "movement app" pile.

**Top picks:**
1. **HeartSquad**: a €25 DIY CPR-feedback manikin plus a phone-camera AI coach, so every PE class in Lithuania can train CPR. Denmark model: bystander CPR rose from 20% to 77% after it was made mandatory in schools.
2. **GrowthGuard**: a smart height station that spots each young athlete's growth spurt and warns the coach to cut jump load. Premier League model: Growth & Maturation screening.
3. **AnkleUp**: a sensor wobble board plus an adaptive balance game for school basketball and volleyball teams. Dutch model: the "Strengthen your ankle" RCT, where recurrent sprains fell from 33% to 22%.
4. **GymAir** (runner-up): a CO₂ and heat monitor for school gyms and changing rooms that tells the teacher when to ventilate. UK model: the SAMHE project (1,300 schools). It also fits the Sustainability theme.
5. **FlashPE** (runner-up): DIY reaction-light pods whose AI auto-levels difficulty, so strong and weak kids can play in the same PE game.

## 2. Full profiles

### ⭐ 1. HeartSquad: "Every PE class a lifesaver class"

**Pitch:** A €25 CPR-training manikin we build ourselves, with a phone app whose AI coach checks your compressions and arm position in real time. Any PE teacher can run a 20-minute resuscitation lesson without buying €300+ professional manikins.

**Relatable problem ("we noticed this ourselves"):** In a match or PE lesson a player collapses. Would anyone in the gym know what to do? Most of us wouldn't. We had one first-aid talk and never pressed on a chest. Everyone remembers the Christian Eriksen collapse at Euro 2020, when teammates and medics started CPR within seconds [unverified: background knowledge].

**Lithuanian evidence:**
- Only **4 in 10** surveyed people could perform adult CPR, and **1 in 5** could use a defibrillator [search summary: ld.lt / diena.lt articles in [this search](https://www.ld.lt/naujienos/kiekviena-praleista-minute-mazina-galimybe-isgyventi-10-proc-gydytoja-akcentuoja)].
- About **3,000–4,000** people/yr die of sudden cardiac arrest outside hospital in LT. Ambulance response for life-threatening calls averages **9–10 min**, and each minute of delay cuts survival by 7–10% [search summary: [ld.lt](https://www.ld.lt/naujienos/kiekviena-praleista-minute-mazina-galimybe-isgyventi-10-proc-gydytoja-akcentuoja), [LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/naujienos/sveikata/682/1698296/lenktynes-su-mirtimi-kokie-zenklai-rodo-kad-zmogu-butina-pradeti-gaivinti-nedelsiant-ir-kokia-kiekvienos-uzdelstos-minutes-kaina)].
- **Policy momentum:** SAM (Ministry of Health) proposes a mandatory public AED network, including **sports halls and schools**. It estimates LT needs ~10,000 AEDs and is missing ~9,400 [verified: [Alkas.lt, Nov 2025](https://alkas.lt/2025/11/28/sam-siulo-plesti-viesai-prieinamu-defibriliatoriu-tinkla/)]. AEDs without people who dare to use them do nothing, and that is the gap we fill.

**Proven model abroad:**
- **Denmark** made CPR training mandatory in primary schools (2005) and for driving licences (2006). Bystander CPR rose from **~20% (2001) to 77% (2018)**, and 30-day survival rose from ~4% to ~16% [verified: [HealthManagement summary](https://healthmanagement.org/c/icu/issuearticle/denmark-increased-bystander-cpr-decreased-mortality-in-out-of-hospital-cardiac-arrest), [JAMA Netw Open 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2802521), [19-year school survey](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666520425001766)].
- Feedback devices improve compression quality in training [verified: [PMC6962160](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6962160/)]. Accelerometer-only depth/rate feedback reaches 2.5–6.3% median depth error [verified: [PLOS One 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0150139)]. Arduino CPR feedback builds exist [verified: [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/395701223_Design_and_Implementation_of_an_Arduino-Powered_CPR_Feedback_Device)].
- The WHO/ERC "Kids Save Lives" statement recommends 2 h of CPR/yr from age 12 [unverified: background knowledge].

**The prototype and the live stage demo:**
- **Hardware:** a foam torso (or a cheap training torso) with a spring or foam block inside. A VL53L0X ToF distance sensor or load cell measures compression depth, and an ESP32 sends the data to the phone over BLE. Parts cost about €20–30 [unverified estimate].
- **App:** a live depth/rate gauge ("push harder", "faster: 100–120/min", "let the chest rise"). The **phone camera + pose AI** (MediaPipe) checks locked elbows, shoulders over hands and hand position. At the end it gives a score and a "certificate".
- **On stage (2 min):** "Who in the jury has done CPR in the last year?" A jury member does 30 compressions and the screen shows a live score (for example 62%). After 20 seconds of AI coaching they try again and reach 90%. Then the team shows its pilot chart.

**How AI/IoT is used and why it's useful:** A PE teacher can't watch the depth and rate of 25 pupils at once. The sensor measures what the eye can't (depth in cm, compressions per minute, recoil). The pose AI catches the most common error, bent arms, and the app turns all of this into instant personal coaching. That makes a cheap self-built torso as good a teacher as a Laerdal QCPR manikin.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6:**
- Build 3 manikins in weeks 1–3.
- Train **100–150 students** in PE lessons in weeks 4–5, capturing *before* and *after* compression-quality scores automatically.
- Survey 200 students: "Would you start CPR?" (confidence 1–5, before and after).
- Get a letter from the PE teacher, the school's public-health specialist, or Lithuanian Red Cross / a first-aid trainer.
- **Output:** "X% of our students did correct-depth CPR before, Y% after 20 minutes."

**Target audience and reach:** grades 7–12 in PE lessons. Delivery routes: PE teachers, school public-health specialists (VSB), the civil-safety curriculum and LRK. Scale is **one kit per school**; LT has roughly 1,000 general schools [unverified]. It is open-source and can be built in school technology classes.

**The one impact number:** % of students able to deliver correct-depth CPR (pilot before vs after). Long term: the LT "4 in 10 could do CPR" figure moving toward Denmark's level.

**Top 3 jury objections and answers:**
1. *"Is this sport?"* The theme asks to make sport **accessible, educational and meaningful**. Sudden cardiac arrest on sports grounds is exactly why SAM wants AEDs in sports halls. We frame it as "safe sport: every team knows CPR". **Honest caveat:** this is the biggest risk. The pitch must anchor on sports halls, PE and teams, or the team can pitch it as a general health project.
2. *"Laerdal already sells feedback manikins."* Yes, and that's the proof it works. They cost hundreds of euros, so schools don't have them [unverified price]. Ours is €25, open-source and phone-based, and the AI pose coaching adds arm-technique feedback.
3. *"Is a DIY device accurate?"* We calibrate it with a ruler and a metronome and report the error (the published accelerometer method reaches ~2.5–6% depth error). It's a training tool, not a medical device.

**Exists in LT?** First-aid courses and commercial training AEDs/manikins are sold ([sirdiespagalba.lt](https://sirdiespagalba.lt/lt/defibriliatoriai-ir-priedai/mokomieji-defibriliatoriai/)). I found no low-cost AI-feedback school CPR kit and no mandatory school CPR programme like Denmark's [unverified: absence in 2 searches].

**Score: 17/18.**

### ⭐ 2. GrowthGuard: "your knees hurt because you grew 9 cm this year, and your coach doesn't know"

**Pitch:** A wall-mounted "smart height station" in the club or school gym. Kids scan a card, stand and then sit. Every month the app computes each player's growth speed and predicted growth spurt, and warns the coach: "these 4 players are in their growth spurt, cut jumps and sprints this month."

**Relatable problem:** Every basketball or volleyball team has a 13–14-year-old with "growing pains": knee pain below the kneecap (Osgood-Schlatter) or heel pain (Sever's). They keep jumping, it gets worse, and some quit.
- Osgood-Schlatter affects **~9.8% of 12–15-year-olds**, rising to **21.2% of athletes vs 4.5% of inactive teens** [verified: search summary of [Kujala 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4025675/) / [physio-learning](https://physio-learning.com/en/ressources/la-maladie-dosgood-schlatter/)].
- In academy players, a growth rate **>7.2 cm/yr** is linked to more injuries [verified: [PMC6293374](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6293374/)].

**Lithuanian evidence:** Basketball and volleyball are jump-heavy sports with huge youth participation in LT [unverified: common knowledge]. About 1,000 ACL injuries/yr in LT, peaking at age 15–25 [verified in round 3: [rimtautasgudas.lt](https://rimtautasgudas.lt/priekinio-kryzminio-raiscio-plysimas/)]. **Weakness:** I found no LT data on growth-related overuse injuries (round 3 found no youth concussion protocol either).

**Proven model abroad:**
- **Premier League EPPP Growth & Maturation Screening:** every academy player is measured regularly and loads are adjusted during peak height velocity (PHV) [verified: [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/footballandcommunity/youth-development/EPPP/elite-performance)].
- **Bio-banding** (grouping by maturity, not birth year) is used in English academies such as Brentford [verified: [Sportsmith](https://www.sportsmith.co/articles/bio-banding-brentford-fc-academy-players-age-is-just-another-number/), [JSAMS 2025](https://www.jsams.org/article/S1440-2440(25)00437-2/fulltext)].
- A Delphi expert consensus names "during and 12 months after PHV" as a top injury-risk factor [verified: [PMC11556685](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11556685/)].
- Tools exist for elite clubs: Kitman Labs and the Hylyght Growth Tracker (%PAH, the method used in the EPPP) [verified: [Kitman](https://www.kitmanlabs.com/blog/growth-maturation-coaching-development/), [Hylyght](https://hylyght.com/en/academy/e-learning/growth-tracker/tracking-growth-and-maturity-what-it-is-why-it-matters-and-how-to-do-it-right)].
- **Our pitch:** "Elite academies do this; the other 95% of kids in regional clubs don't. We bring it to every club for €30."

**The prototype and the live stage demo:**
- **Hardware:** a ToF/ultrasonic sensor above the head on a wall bar, a sit-box of known height (so it measures sitting height and leg length), a bathroom-scale load cell, an NFC/QR card reader and an ESP32.
- **App:** Mirwald maturity-offset and Khamis-Roche %PAH models plus a growth-velocity curve per player. The coach dashboard shows players coloured by pre-, circa- or post-PHV, with suggested load changes.
- **On stage:** a team member stands, then sits, and the height appears instantly (a jury member can try it too). Then the pilot dashboard shows the real team (anonymised): "4 of our 14 U14 players are in their growth spurt right now; 3 of them reported knee or heel pain."

**How AI/IoT is used and why it's useful:** The IoT station makes monthly measurement take 10 seconds per child with no tape-measure errors. That's the part coaches never do. The prediction model (regression-based maturity offset, which we can improve with an ML model trained on published growth curves) turns raw heights into "who is in the danger window". A coach can't see a growth spurt by eye, because kids grow a few mm a week.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6:**
- Measure **60–120 players** from 2–3 youth teams (school team plus a partnering club) twice, 4 weeks apart. Add an injury/pain questionnaire (knee, heel, back) with parental consent.
- **Output:** "% currently in the PHV window; pain prevalence in PHV vs non-PHV kids; the coach changed training for N kids."
- Get a coach's letter.

**Target audience and reach:** coaches of 10–16-year-olds in basketball, volleyball, football and athletics clubs, plus PE teachers. Reach runs through NVŠ-accredited clubs (the state funds these through the NVŠ voucher; see local-data), sports schools (sporto mokyklos) and LSU. It can also feed into the national fitness testing already done in every school (round 3).

**The one impact number:** share of youth players whose coach knows their growth-spurt status (from ~0% to 100% in pilot clubs). Longer term: fewer overuse-injury training days lost.

**Top 3 jury objections and answers:**
1. *"A tape measure does this."* In theory yes, but nobody does it monthly, nobody computes velocity or maturity offset, and nobody passes it to the coach. The station plus model makes it automatic, the same pattern as the EPPP.
2. *"Health data about minors (GDPR)?"* Parental consent, height and weight only, stored by pseudonymous card ID, with the coach seeing only the traffic light. No photos.
3. *"Can you prove fewer injuries in 6 weeks?"* No, and we say so honestly. The pilot proves *detection* (who is in PHV, and the pain link). The effect of load management is proven by the Premier League and published research, and we cite it.

**Exists in LT?** Elite tools (Kitman, Hylyght) are commercial and aimed at pro academies. Whether any LT sports school does routine maturity screening is **[unverified]**; top basketball academies may.

**Score: 15/18.**

### ⭐ 3. AnkleUp: "sprain once, sprain again, unless you do this 8-week game"

**Pitch:** A wobble board with a motion sensor and a phone game. Players with a past ankle sprain do the evidence-based 8-week "Strengthen your ankle" programme as a game. The AI scores their balance and adapts the difficulty, and the coach sees who is actually doing it.

**Relatable problem:** In school basketball, almost everyone has "rolled an ankle". Then they roll it again. In a 3-year study of adolescent basketball players the ankle was **48%** of all injuries, and **28%** of injuries were recurrent, most of them ankle sprains [verified: [PubMed 28033652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28033652/)]. A previous ankle injury gave a **~5× higher** chance of another [verified: search summary of the same result set].

**Lithuanian evidence:** Lithuania is a basketball country [unverified: common knowledge]. **Weakness:** I found no LT ankle-sprain statistics. The Hygiene Institute's injury publications exist but aren't split by sport [verified: [hi.lt](https://www.hi.lt/sveikatos-statistikos-leidiniai/)]. The team should run its own school survey ("how many of you sprained an ankle? how many twice?"), which becomes the local number.

**Proven model abroad:**
- **Hupperets et al., BMJ 2009 (NL):** 522 athletes aged 12–70, unsupervised 8-week home proprioceptive programme. Recurrent sprain in **22% vs 33%** of controls, about a one-third relative reduction [verified: [PubMed 19589822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19589822/), [PDF](https://cdle.colorado.gov/sites/cdle/files/Hupperets_2009.pdf)].
- The follow-up "Strengthen your ankle" **app** RCT (220 athletes) found the app as effective as the booklet. **Compliance is the main problem** [verified: [PubMed 27988093](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27988093/), [JMIR process evaluation](https://rehab.jmir.org/2018/2/e13/)].
- **This is our hook:** the exercises work, kids don't do them, and a game with sensor feedback fixes compliance.

**The prototype and the live stage demo:**
- **Hardware:** a plywood disc on a half-ball, an ESP32 + IMU (MPU6050), a BLE link and a phone/TV screen.
- **Game:** tilt to steer a ball through a maze, or stand on one leg while the board keeps a "calm zone" (like a spirit level). The AI measures sway area and jerk and raises the level (eyes closed, head turns, ball catches) as you improve. It follows the 6-exercise, 3×/week, 8-week structure.
- **On stage:** a jury member stands on one leg for 20 seconds, and the screen shows a live sway trace, a score and the rank in our school team.

**How AI/IoT is used and why it's useful:** The IMU measures *balance quality* (sway area, correction speed), which a coach can't see and the booklet/app version can't measure. The adaptive AI keeps the task "just hard enough", the gamification lever for compliance. The coach dashboard shows who trained, which solves the documented compliance failure.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6:**
- Survey 200 students on sprain history (this gives the local number).
- Recruit 20–30 team players with a past sprain for **4–5 weeks** of the programme (half the full length), measuring sway score before and after plus compliance (sessions done vs prescribed).
- Compare compliance with a paper-booklet group if possible.
- **Output:** "compliance X% vs Y%; sway improved Z%."

**Target audience and reach:** school basketball/volleyball/handball teams, club coaches and school physio or PE teachers. One board per team (~€30). Reach runs through school sport (MKL school league, from round 3) and NVŠ clubs.

**The one impact number:** recurrent-sprain rate (target: the published one-third reduction). Pilot proxy: % of prescribed sessions completed.

**Top 3 jury objections and answers:**
1. *"Smart balance boards exist (BoBo, Wii Balance Board)."* [verified: [search](https://sidekicktool.com/products/axisboard), [Wii trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01449760)]. They are rehab/fitness products. Ours is a €30 school-team kit that runs a proven *prevention* protocol with coach compliance tracking.
2. *"Is this AI?"* It's adaptive difficulty plus movement-quality classification from IMU data. Modest but useful. **Honest:** of the top 3, this has the weakest AI.
3. *"You can't show fewer injuries by November."* True. We show compliance and balance gains and cite the BMJ trial for the injury effect.

**Exists in LT?** No LT school-team ankle-prevention kit found [unverified: not specifically searched in LT].

**Score: 15/18.**

### 4. GymAir (runner-up; also fits Sustainability)

**Pitch:** A sensor in the school gym and changing room shows CO₂, heat and humidity on a traffic-light display. It predicts when the air will go bad and tells the teacher "open windows now". Over weeks it builds evidence for the school to fix ventilation.

**Relatable:** the stuffy, sweaty gym after two lessons in a row, the changing room you can't breathe in, headaches in the lesson after PE.

**LT evidence:**
- In Vilnius schools, **9 of 11 classrooms exceeded 2,000 ppm CO₂**, with a peak of **5,152 ppm** against a 1,500 ppm norm [verified: [sildymas-vedinimas.lt](https://sildymas-vedinimas.lt/naujienos/vilniaus-mokyklose-oro-kokybe-neatitinka-normu/), [Diena](https://m.diena.lt/naujienos/sveikata/sveikata/istyre-lietuvoje-vaikai-mokyklose-kencia-nuo-deguonies-trukumo-950178)].
- KTU warns that renovated (insulated) schools need ventilation too [verified: [KTU](https://ktu.edu/news/ktu-tyrejai-primena-mokykloms-neuztenka-tik-apsiltinti-reikia-pasirupinti-ir-vedinimu/)].
- Gym-specific LT data: **[not found]**.

**Proven model:** UK **SAMHE** sent AirGradient monitors to **1,300+ schools**. In cold periods, >25% of monitors exceeded the 1,500 ppm daily threshold [verified: [SEI](https://www.sei.org/perspectives/uk-schools-classroom-air/), [SAMHE 2024/25 report](https://samhe.org.uk/resources/SAMHE_Full_Year_Report_24_25_Final.pdf)].

**Demo:** the team breathes into the box and the light goes red. The app forecasts "red in 12 minutes" and shows 3 weeks of our own gym data.

**Pilot:** 3 weeks in our gym and changing rooms: % of lessons above 1,500 ppm, and what window-opening changed.

**Why runner-up:** strong local data and a proven model, but the round-2 pool already rejected an outdoor air/heat version. Its link to "getting students active" is weak. It is *better pitched under Sustainability* ("ventilate smart, not heat the street": energy vs air quality).

**Score: 15/18.**

### 5. FlashPE (runner-up)

**Pitch:** Eight DIY light pods (ESP32 + LED + touch sensor) turn any PE drill into a reaction game. The AI gives each player targets matched to their level, so the best basketball player and the kid who hates PE can play the same game and both find it hard.

**Relatable:** PE is boring, and the strong kids dominate every game.

**Evidence:**
- A longitudinal study found that **enjoyment of PE, not MVPA during PE, predicts future MVPA** [verified: [PMC8470923](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8470923/)].
- The commercial model is **BlazePod** school sets, with teacher reports of kids "asking to use the pods" [verified: [Gopher](https://blog.gophersport.com/blazepod/)]. That is anecdotal, not a trial.
- LT hook: >50% of inactive kids "don't see the point" (local-data.md).

**Demo:** the jury plays against the team on stage. Great wow factor.

**Why runner-up:**
- The proven model is a product, not an outcome trial.
- The "adaptive AI" is thin.
- The jury may file it next to the "gamified" entries.

**Score: 14/18.**

## 3. Notes for the team

- **Theme framing:** HeartSquad, GrowthGuard and AnkleUp all fit "make sport **safe** and accessible", which is the sport theme seen through the teen-injury lens. HeartSquad has the strongest local policy hook (SAM's AED plan) but the loosest link to "sport". GrowthGuard and AnkleUp are unmistakably sport.
- **Girls dropping out:** the UK data is strong. **43%** of once-sporty teenage girls disengage vs 24% of boys, and **78%** avoid sport during their period [verified: [Women in Sport](https://womeninsport.org/news/more-than-1-million-teenage-girls-fall-out-of-love-with-sport/)]. Across HBSC 2022, 15% of girls vs 25% of boys meet 60 min/day [verified: [HBSC via X](https://x.com/HBSCStudy/status/1793420824776769612)]. I found no tech idea here whose tech isn't a wrapper (see #9 in the table). Use the fact as a *pilot-survey angle* in any pick, for example by reporting AnkleUp or GrowthGuard results for girls separately.
- **PE exemption abuse** is confirmed again [verified: [tv3](https://www.tv3.lt/naujiena/lietuva/vaikui-susirgus-pakanka-tevu-rastelio-o-kas-atleis-nuo-kuno-kulturos-n1011470), [Švietimo naujienos](https://www.svietimonaujienos.lt/kuno-kulturos-pamoka-atleisti-nereikia-dalyvauti/)], but it is already covered by S1/S2 in `ideas/sport.md`.
