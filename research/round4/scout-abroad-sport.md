# Scout (round 4): proven abroad, adapted for Lithuania (SPORT & ACTIVE LIVING)

> Angle: youth sport and physical-activity programmes that **have measured results abroad** but don't exist in Lithuania, or barely do. The pitch shape is: "It worked in X with result Y; here is the Lithuanian version, with a working prototype and AI."
> Budget: **20/20 WebSearch calls used**. WebFetch not used. Every claim is tagged **[verified: source]**, meaning it was confirmed in a search snippet this session, or **[unverified]**, meaning background knowledge or an assumption the team must check before Oct 16.
> I avoided the rejected patterns on purpose (phone-on-tripod form checker, step/points apps, AR city quests, screen-time-for-steps). Where an idea comes close to one of them, the table says so.

---

## 0. Bottom line

The strongest "proven abroad" sport stories are **not apps. They are school routines**: the Daily Mile (UK), Schools on the Move (FI), Schools in Motion / Liikuma Kutsuv Kool (EE), bike buses (ES/global) and physically active lessons (NO/DK). They have hard numbers and a generalist jury gets them in 10 seconds. None of them has a visible national presence in Lithuania (searched; see tags). **Estonia, our Baltic neighbour, reaches 46% of its schools with Schools in Motion**, and that one line is a strong local hook for a Baltic jury.

The winning move is to **take one proven routine and build the small piece of physical tech + AI that makes it run without a teacher having to organise it**. My top 4:

1. **PertraukųDėžė (RecessBox)**: a smart, app-free equipment locker plus an AI "game master" for active breaks. Built on Schools on the Move, Schools in Motion and Sportbox. Best overall (16/18).
2. **Traukinukas (Walking/Bike Bus)**: teen-led walking and bike buses to school with a GPS "locomotive" tracker and AI route-building. Built on the bike-bus movement and Dutch/Belgian school streets. Sport and sustainability crossover (15/18).
3. **Mylia (Daily Mile LT)**: the 15-minute daily run, with an automatic lap-counting gate and an AI weather/air go/no-go plus class insights (15/18).
4. **JudriPamoka (Active Lesson light)**: a classroom CO₂ + sitting-time sensor that triggers AI-generated, subject-linked 3-minute movement breaks. Built on Norway's ASK, physically active learning meta-analyses and Estonia (14/18).

## 3. Full profiles (top 4)

### 1. PertraukųDėžė (RecessBox): "A break is 15 minutes. Give us something to do in it."

**One-sentence pitch:** Finland (90%+ of schools) and Estonia (46% of schools) doubled active breaks by making movement *the easy option at recess*. We built the Lithuanian version: a smart equipment locker any student unlocks with a card, and an AI game master that turns "5 kids, 1 ball, rain, corridor" into a game in 3 seconds.

**Relatable problem + LT evidence**
- Every student and parent has seen it: breaks = sitting on a bench or on a phone. Teens are online 4.5 h per school day and **56% want to cut down** [verified: LRT via local-data.md].
- Only ~5% of grade 9–13 students are active enough; more than 50% of inactive kids "don't see the point" [verified: local-data.md]. The problem is *friction and ideas*, not facilities. The balls are locked in the PE teacher's storeroom.
- Lithuania restricted phones in schools (from 2024) [**unverified**, check]. If true: "phones are gone from breaks, and nothing replaced them."

**Proven model abroad**
- **Finnish Schools on the Move**: recess physically active play 30→49%; girls meeting PA recommendation 18→29% (2010–18); >90% of schools [verified: [PMC 6029642](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6029642/)]. Equipment lending and student-led recess activities are core FSM tools [unverified detail, background knowledge].
- **Estonia, Schools in Motion**: ≥20 min active recess 37%→67% of schools in 2 years; 222 schools [verified: [liikumakutsuvkool.ee](https://www.liikumakutsuvkool.ee/english/)]. Pitch line: *"Estonia did this at half its schools. Lithuania has no equivalent."*
- **Sportbox (DE/CH/NL)**: app-unlocked, camera-secured, solar lockers lending sports kit for free [verified: [sportbox.de](https://sportbox.de/en/)]. This proves the self-service locker hardware concept works in public.

**Prototype / 2-minute live demo**
- A wooden or plywood locker (3–4 compartments) with ESP32 + solenoid locks + NFC reader (student card / phone NFC) + a load cell or reed switch per compartment (item back?) + a small screen.
- On stage: a judge taps a card. The screen asks "How many of you? Where?" and the judge answers by voice ("four, indoors"). The AI game master shows a 30-second game ("Corridor keep-up: 4 players, 1 soft ball, …") and the right compartment unlocks. The judge takes the ball out; the dashboard shows "borrowed 10:42". They put it back and the item is marked as returned.
- Dashboard: borrowings per break, per class and per weather, plus a "most-played games" list.

**How AI/IoT is used, and why it's useful**
- **IoT**: unlock and return detection means no teacher is needed to hand out kit (the real barrier: "the storeroom key is with the PE teacher"). Auto-logging creates data nobody has today: *how many students actually move at break*.
- **AI game master** (LLM with a curated rulebook of ~100 FSM/Schools in Motion recess games; speech-to-text in Lithuanian): picks or adapts a game to group size, space, equipment in stock and weather. This solves the ">50% don't see the point / don't know what to play" problem. Later, **AI usage forecasting** (weather + timetable) tells the school which kit to add and when to restock.
- Honest framing: the AI isn't deep, but it's the part that turns a box of balls into *an organised activity* without an adult.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6 (6 weeks)**
- Week 1: baseline survey of ~200 students ("what do you do at break?", how many minutes of movement) plus a 3-day observation count at breaks (students tally how many are moving in 2 zones, SOPLAY-style snapshots, no cameras).
- Weeks 2–3: build the box; seed it with ~€100 of kit.
- Weeks 4–6: run it at school for 2–3 weeks. Numbers: borrowings/day, unique users, % of girls, observed "moving" share in the zone before vs after, and a post-survey.
- A partner letter from the school director, plus an email to the Univ. of Tartu Move Lab (Schools in Motion) asking for a quote or advice. That's a strong "research into prior efforts" point and a Baltic link.

**Target audience and reach:** grades 5–12 in a school (users); school director / PE department (buyer, ~€300–500 per box, a guess [unverified]). Reach through the Higienos institutas "Aktyvi mokykla" network and municipal public-health bureaus (VSB). Scales via NVŠ/municipal health budgets.

**The one impact number:** *% of students physically active during breaks (observed), before vs after* (with borrowings/day as the hard log).

**Top 3 jury objections**
1. *"It's just a box of balls."* → The box is the easy part. The problem Finland and Estonia solved is *access without an adult* and *ideas*. Unlock-and-return tracking solves access; the AI game master solves "what do we play". And we measure it, which nobody in LT does.
2. *"Stuff will get stolen or broken."* → Each borrowing is linked to a student card and return is detected. Sportbox runs camera-secured *public* lockers in Germany, the Netherlands and Switzerland. The pilot will report the actual loss rate.
3. *"Kids will play for a week and stop."* → That's why the AI rotates games and the log shows decay. The pilot reports week 1 vs week 3 honestly. Estonia's gains held over 2 years with the same "make it easy" principle.

**Exists in LT?** No national active-recess programme with equipment lending found [unverified absence; searched "Daily Mile"/recess terms only]. The Higienos institutas "Aktyvi mokykla" network exists; its content is unverified. **Must check before Oct 16.**

### 2. Traukinukas (Walking & Bike Bus): "The school run, without the car"

**One-sentence pitch:** Barcelona's "Bicibús" spread to 400+ routes worldwide. We're launching Lithuania's first teen-led walking/bike bus: high-schoolers are the "drivers", a GPS tracker is the "locomotive", and AI draws the safest routes from where families actually live.

**Relatable problem + LT evidence**
- Kids driven to school **+200% over 30 years**; only 5/10 travel independently vs 7/10 in the 1990s; **182 children injured within 100 m of schools 2022–25** [verified: Vilnius.lt, 15min via local-data.md]. Every parent knows the morning traffic jam at the school gate.
- It's both **sport and sustainability**: kids get 2 × 10–20 min of activity a day and car trips drop. That fits the team's sustainability leaning.

**Proven model abroad**
- Bike bus: 403 routes worldwide in 2023, average 10 children + 7 adults over 3 km [verified: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/abs/pii/S1556831824000534)]. Review evidence of effects on cycling trips, self-efficacy and MVPA is mixed but positive in several studies [verified: [PMC 7661215](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7661215/)].
- School streets (BE/NL/UK): 30-min car-free windows at the gate [verified concept: [Mobiel 21](https://www.mobiel21.be/en/insights-and-case-studies/a-school-street-this-is-how-you-do-it)].
- Walking school buses (UK/NZ/AU) are long-established [unverified, background knowledge].
- Known weakness: middle-class dominated [verified, same source]. Our AI route-builder targets *all* addresses, and that's our answer.

**Prototype / 2-minute live demo**
- "Locomotive" tracker: ESP32 + GPS + LTE-M/LoRa (or just a phone app in leader mode) on the leader's hi-vis vest, plus a big button for "departed / arrived / problem".
- Parent app/web: a live map with the bus moving and "arriving at your stop in 3 min" notifications. At school a teacher sees "Line 2 arrived, 9 children checked in" (BLE tags on the kids' bags auto-check-in [optional]).
- On stage: a team member walks across the stage wearing the vest. A judge's phone shows the bus moving and gets the "arriving" push. Second screen: type 30 anonymised addresses (grid squares) and the AI draws 3 routes with stops, avoiding the accident hotspots.

**How AI/IoT is used, and why it's useful**
- **AI route optimisation**: clustering of home locations (k-means / vehicle-routing solver) + walking-safety scoring from OpenStreetMap (crossings, speed limits) + the municipal child-injury locations. Designing routes by hand is exactly what stops parent volunteers; bike-bus founders cite route planning and volunteers as the barriers [unverified].
- **IoT tracker**: parents' #1 fear is "where is my child now?" A live location plus arrival confirmation replaces the car's feeling of control.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6**
- Survey of parents at the team's school, or a partner primary school (~150–200 families): mode of travel, distance, "would you let your child join if you could see the bus live?". This is the key pre-pilot number.
- Run **one walking-bus line, 2–3 mornings per week for 3 weeks** in October with 5–10 primary kids (teacher and parent consent needed; the team members are the leaders). Log: trips, children per trip, km walked, car trips avoided.
- Weather/darkness: October mornings in LT are still usable; November gets dark. Walking bus first, bike bus from spring (plan).

**Target audience and reach:** primary pupils (grades 1–4) + their parents; high-school students as volunteer leaders (they earn volunteering hours [unverified whether LT schools require social activity hours; many do]). Reach: municipal mobility/transport departments (Vilnius runs child road-safety studies), schools' parent committees, and "Dviračių kultūra" (an LT NGO) as partner.

**The one impact number:** *car trips to school replaced per week* (and minutes of active travel per child).

**Top 3 jury objections**
1. *"Lithuanian winter?"* → Walking bus first; hi-vis vests + tracker. Finland's FSM reported *more* active winter commuting in programme schools [verified: FSM search snippet]. So cold countries do it.
2. *"Safety and liability of teens leading little kids."* → An adult parent rotates as co-leader (the bike-bus norm is ~7 adults per 10 children [verified]). Teens assist, and the route avoids injury hotspots. Legal check with the school is part of the plan.
3. *"Isn't this just an app?"* → The tracker is hardware, the routes are real, and a pilot line runs. The measurable output is physical: kids walked, cars removed.

**Exists in LT?** No bike/walking bus found [unverified absence]. Vilnius runs child road-safety research and campaigns [verified via local-data.md].

### 3. Mylia (Daily Mile LT): "15 minutes outside, every day, and the gate counts for you"

**One-sentence pitch:** The Daily Mile, 15 minutes of running or walking in class time, gave Scottish children +9.1 min of MVPA a day and 5% better fitness, and it's in 14,000 schools in 87 countries. We bring it to Lithuania with a lap gate that counts automatically and an AI that decides "go/no-go" and tells teachers what's working.

**Relatable problem + LT evidence:** ~5% of teens are active enough; PE MVPA is below 50% (LSU study); teachers have no time to organise extra activity [verified: local-data / round-3 scout-sport]. Everyone remembers dreading "the run" in PE. The Daily Mile is non-competitive and self-paced, the opposite.

**Proven model abroad:** [BMC Medicine 2018](https://bmcmedicine.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12916-018-1049-z): +9.1 min/day MVPA, −18.2 min sedentary, fitness +5%, fat −4% (391 children) [verified]. Scale 3M children / 14k schools / 87 countries [verified as summarised]. Laps are counted and pushed to a class dashboard. ### 4. JudriPamoka: "Your classroom tells you when to move"

**One-sentence pitch:** Norway (ASK: 5 min/day classroom activity breaks) and 54 studies on physically active lessons show kids move more without hurting learning. Our classroom box notices when the class has sat too long or the air is stale, and AI serves a 3-minute movement break tied to *today's topic*.

**Relatable problem:** 6–7 hours of sitting, stuffy classrooms and afternoon drowsiness. Every student feels it. Adding **CO₂** gives a sustainability/health bridge: a stale-air alert means open the window *and* stand up.
**Proven model:** ASK Norway, 1,129 children, 57 schools [verified]. Honest: no academic effect in the primary analysis [verified]. Meta-analyses: active lessons improve PA time and don't harm, and sometimes help, maths [verified: [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1651883/full)]. Estonia's Schools in Motion promotes physically active learning [verified].
**Prototype/demo:** ESP32 + SCD40 CO₂ sensor + PIR/mmWave presence + LED ring (green/amber/red) + speaker. When CO₂ > ~1,200 ppm or it's been 25 min since the last break, the ring turns amber. The teacher taps and the AI (LLM + curated movement library) generates "Maths: stand up if the answer is even, squat if odd" for the current timetable subject (read from the e-diary timetable or picked manually). On stage the judges do the 60-second break; the CO₂ curve is shown live (breath into the sensor for effect).
**AI:** generates subject-linked breaks (teachers' #1 barrier is "no ideas/no time" [unverified]) and learns which breaks classes accept (teacher 👍/👎). IoT sensing triggers them.
**Mini-pilot:** 4 classes × 3 weeks. Log breaks/day, a CO₂ profile (a real finding by itself: "our classrooms hit 2,000 ppm by 3rd lesson" is a likely, very nod-worthy number [unverified until measured]) and a student alertness survey.
**Impact number:** *movement minutes per school day in class* (+ % of lesson time above the CO₂ threshold).
**Objections:** (1) "Teachers won't stop lessons." → A 3-minute break, subject-linked, teacher-triggered; ASK did 5 min/day. (2) "The CO₂ monitor already exists." → Yes, which is why it's cheap. The new part is turning the signal into an action that moves kids. (3) "AI is just ChatGPT." → A curated, age-graded library with LLM adaptation + feedback learning. Keep the claim modest.
**Exists in LT?** Classroom CO₂ monitors appear in some schools [unverified]. No active-break programme found [unverified].
