## How I judge (the juror's lens)

I have seen dozens of pitches. Three things decide my top 5:

1. **The two-minute moment.** Does something happen on stage that I remember that evening? For example, *I* press on a chest and my score jumps, or a band buzzes on *my* wrist.
2. **"I've seen this before."** The Baltic finals are full of apps. The worst reaction a pitch can get from me is "another CO₂ box" or "another points app". To survive déjà vu, a pitch needs a twist that I can repeat in one sentence.
3. **Samsung story fit.** Does this help me tell the story internally and at the European stage? That means Galaxy phones and watches, SmartThings, AI, upcycling, inclusion, health, and "tech for good". A Galaxy device that is **essential** to the idea is worth more than a logo on a slide.

I also check theme fit hard. The stage-1 screening panel will ask whether the entry is really "Sport & tech" or really "Sustainability", and an entry that stretches the theme loses points early.

Key for the table: R = relatable in 10 s · T = team authenticity · P = proven model · W = working prototype in 6 weeks · A = AI/IoT at the core · M = own mini-pilot numbers by Nov 6 · Au = audience and reach · I = one impact number · L = local hook. Each is scored 0–2 (max 18). **Gut** is "would I put this in the Baltic top 5?", scored 1–10.

## F1: Klasės oras, the classroom air and heat coach (17/18, gut 6.5 → 8 as a hybrid)

**Strongest points**
- **Every juror has sat in a stuffy room.** The local evidence is concrete: a peak of **5,152 ppm against a 1,500 ppm limit** was measured in Vilnius schools [scout-verified: sildymas-vedinimas.lt, emokykla.lt].
- **The pilot is easy and happens right now, in the heating season.** It produces a clean before/after chart.
- **An old Galaxy phone as the classroom display is the best Samsung brand story in the whole shortlist.** Samsung's own Galaxy Upcycling turned old phones into IoT sensors [scout-verified: Samsung US newsroom], and 53% of Lithuanians keep an old phone in a drawer [scout-verified: LRT via local-data.md]. The pitch line is "we finished Samsung's idea for Lithuanian schools".

**Killer weaknesses**
- **Déjà vu.** A classroom CO₂ monitor is one of the most common school STEM projects worldwide. There are science-fair guides, EPA teaching kits, and SchoolAIR and SAMHE citizen-science frameworks [verified: co2meter.com science-fair blog, MDPI SchoolAIR, US EPA]. Aranet, a very common CO₂ monitor, is Latvian [unverified], so the Latvian jurors know the product category well. If the pitch opens with "we built a CO₂ sensor", I have mentally scored it by second 20.
- **The AI is thin.** "It predicts when the room will pass 1,500 ppm" is linear extrapolation. A sharp juror will ask "why not a threshold?"
- **Is it health or sustainability?** If the heat/€ angle is not front and centre, it reads as a health project entered in the sustainability theme.

**What fixes it**
1. **Don't call it a CO₂ monitor.** Call it "the device that stops our school heating the street". Lead with € and kWh: the window left open while the heating runs, then the short full airing, and the heat saved.
2. **Merge F2's 50/50 as the money mechanism.** The municipality returns half the heat saved. This gives the "clear plan" and "local policy" slide.
3. **Make the Galaxy upcycled phone the hardware, not an option.** The phone collection drive becomes pilot number 2.
4. **Add F5's outdoor air.** The coach then also knows when *not* to open the window, for example "smoke outside, wait 20 min". That is a real AI decision a threshold can't make, and it kills the "just a threshold" objection. (See Hybrid A below.)

## F2: Energijos detektyvai 50/50 (13/18, gut 5)

**Strongest points:** a proven EU model with real money. Euronet 50/50 reached 500+ schools in 13 countries, with savings of at least 8% per building [verified: interregeurope.eu, euronet50-50max.eu]. The municipality is a natural buyer.

**Killer weaknesses**
- **No emotional hook.** "Electricity at 3 a.m." makes jurors nod politely.
- **Authenticity and safety.** A 16-year-old putting clamps on the school's distribution board is an electrical-safety problem. The caretaker and an electrician must do it.
- **Plugs are too small.** Ten SmartThings plugs measure a small share of a school's energy, and heating is the big item.
- **The pilot is hard.** Getting a municipal 50/50 letter of intent in 6 weeks is optimistic.
- **Prior art.** Energy Sparks (UK) already does this [scout-verified].

**Fix:** don't enter it alone. Fold the 50/50 incentive and the SmartThings "energy mirror" into F1 as the business model and scaling plan.

## F3: DušoSargas / ShowerCoach (16/18, gut 6)

**Strongest points**
- The "Mum yells that I shower too long" line gets a laugh in 5 seconds, which counts for a lot in a pitch.
- The proven effect is clean: **−23%** in the Swiss amphiro study [scout-verified].
- The local hooks are real: **18% of Lithuanians could not keep their home warm (2024)** [scout-verified: Eurostat], and hot water costs €6.88–7.99/m³ [scout-verified: tv3, housecare].

**Killer weaknesses**
- **amphiro is already a commercial product.** A juror's first thought is "buy one for €60–80?" [price unverified].
- **The AI is weak.** Worse, "the AI recognises which family member is showering" sounds creepy to a room of adults. It is a privacy red flag in a bathroom product, so drop it.
- **Reach is fuzzy.** Who hands out the devices? Utilities? The plan has no owner.
- **Theme fit is fine, but the demo is small.** Water poured through a pipe on stage is not memorable.

**Fix:** reframe it as energy poverty ("the cheapest heat saving a family can make is in the shower"). Name a utility or municipal energy-poverty programme as the channel. Make the AI part a monthly bill forecast plus leak detection, not identifying people. It is still unlikely to beat F1 or F5 on "wow".

## F4: Šilumos detektyvai, the heat detectives (16/18, gut 6)

**Strongest points**
- **Thermal images are visually spectacular on a projector.** Red and blue walls read instantly from the back of the hall.
- The AI does real expert work, because laypeople can't read thermal images.
- **The library loan model is a lovely reach story** [scout-verified: Cardiff, East Herts, Cambridge Carbon Footprint]. I found **no free library thermal-camera loan in Lithuania**; only commercial rentals turned up [verified absence, 1 search: nuomopolis.lt, alio.lt listings]. So it would be "first in LT".

**Killer weaknesses**
- **Training a leak classifier in 6 weeks on the team's own few hundred images is hard.** An LLM looking at a picture looks like a wrapper, which is the "test strip" problem the team already rejected.
- The impact number is modelled, not measured.
- The demo needs a 10 °C inside-outside difference, which is hard to fake on stage beyond an ice pack.

**Fix:** keep it as a **feature of F1**, or as the "phase 2: from school to home" slide. It is a weaker standalone entry.

## F5: Dūmų radaras, the smoke radar (14/18, gut 7)

**Strongest points**
- **A new policy hook this season.** From **1 May 2026**, burning coal, peat and lignite is banned in Vilnius, Kaunas, Klaipėda, Šiauliai, Panevėžys, Alytus and the resort towns (Birštonas, Druskininkai, Nida, Palanga). Fines are €60–300, and residents are asked to report violations to the municipality [verified: am.lrv.lt, alkas.lt 2026-04-10, 15min, infa.lt]. **October 2026 is the first heating season under the ban.** "Is the ban working in our street? Our sensors answer that" hits the "local policy" criterion squarely.
- **The pattern is proven at Samsung.** AsthmaSense (IE) won SfT UK & Ireland 2025/26 [scout-verified: D&T Association].
- The stage demo is good: a match next to the sensor makes the reading spike.
- PM2.5 is known to trigger asthma in children, and it peaks in the coldest spells [verified: hi.lt, pamatyti.lt].

**Killer weaknesses**
- **Relatability depends on the town.** In a flat in central Vilnius few jurors smell chimney smoke. It needs a team from a private-house district or a small town.
- **The ban doesn't cover wood.** The ban is on fossil solid fuel only, while much of the smoke is wet wood and burnt waste. Be precise, or a juror from the ministry will correct you.
- **A forecast needs data.** Four weeks of data from mid-October is thin for a credible forecast by Nov 6.
- **"Report your neighbour" is ugly.** If the pitch sounds like surveillance, it loses the NGO jurors. Show street-level aggregates only.
- **Prior art.** Low-cost PM networks (sensor.community, Airly) are common [unverified in this session].

**Fix:** make the pitch "the first heating season of the coal ban: we measured whether our street's air got better". The asthma alert becomes the user-facing feature. Or merge the outdoor sensor into F1 (Hybrid A).

## F6: RecessBox (PertraukųDėžė) (17/18, gut 7.5)

**Strongest points**
- **The timing is perfect, and verified.** From **1 Sep 2026** every Lithuanian school *must* have a phone-use procedure. The ministry's recommendations specifically target **breaks**, and "for most children the biggest change will be during breaks" [verified: tavovaikas.lt, smsm.lrv.lt, edtalk.lt, 77.lt]. The pitch writes itself: "This September phones left our breaks. Nothing replaced them."
- **A Baltic neighbour hook.** Estonia's Schools in Motion reaches 46% of Estonian schools [scout-verified: liikumakutsuvkool.ee]. A juror from Estonia will smile, and a Lithuanian juror will feel the gap.
- **It is a physical object, not another gamified app.** That differentiates it from last season's pile of movement apps.
- The pilot numbers are easy to get: borrowings per day, the share of girls, and observed movement before and after.

**Killer weaknesses**
- **The LLM "game master" feels bolted on.** A laminated card with 30 games does the same job. A juror will ask this.
- **It is sport, which the team feels less for.** They must pitch it with passion or not at all.
- **Samsung fit is weak** unless the locker opens with a phone or Galaxy Watch NFC, or a Galaxy tablet serves as the locker screen.

**Fix**
- Make the AI the part that **learns from the log**: which games and kit actually get used, by whom (girls and boys, age groups) and in what weather. It then restocks and rotates, and it gives the school the first real data on how active breaks are.
- Add a **sustainability bridge**: stock the box with donated outgrown sports gear (the scouts' "gear loop" idea), so it fits both themes.

## F7: Traukinukas, the walking/bike bus (12/18, gut 4)

**Killer weaknesses**
- **The pilot is almost impossible by Nov 6.** It needs primary-school kids, parental consent and liability cover, with teens leading children in dark November mornings.
- **"GPS tracker for kids" is a very common app category.**
- The route AI is a nice extra, not the core.

**Fix:** no realistic fix within 6 weeks. Park it for a spring project.

## F8: HeartSquad, CPR in PE (17/18, gut 8, minus 0.5 for theme risk)

**Strongest points**
- **The best juror-participation demo on the list.** "Who here has done CPR this year?" A juror does 30 compressions: 62%. Twenty seconds of AI coaching later: 90%. Every juror will remember it, and the educators and NGO members will love it.
- **A hard proven number.** In Denmark, bystander CPR rose from about 20% to 77% after mandatory school training [scout-verified: JAMA Netw Open, HealthManagement].
- **Pilot numbers are easy and strong.** Before and after compression quality for 100–150 students is captured automatically.
- **The AI is genuinely useful.** Pose AI catches bent arms, which a teacher can't watch in 25 students at once.
- **It transfers across Europe.** The European Resuscitation Council's 2025 guidelines say CPR training should start at age 4–6 and be repeated yearly in schools [verified: militra.lt summary of ERC 2025]. That fits the pan-European stage well.
- **A local gap.** In Lithuania, first-aid training (including CPR) is mandatory for *teachers* every 5 years, but no mandatory pupil programme turned up [verified: militra.lt, sdg.lt; the absence is from 1 search].
- The Ministry of Health (SAM) wants AEDs in sports halls and schools [scout-verified: alkas.lt Nov 2025].

**Killer weaknesses**
- **Theme fit.** The Sport theme is "make sport accessible/educational/meaningful; get students active". CPR is safety and health, not getting active. A strict stage-1 screener may mark it off-theme.
- **Prior art.** Arduino CPR-feedback builds are well documented (Hackster, Arduino Project Hub, ResearchGate) [verified]. That doesn't matter to a generalist jury, but the pitch must not claim to be the first.
- **The local statistic is soft.** The "4 in 10 Lithuanians can do CPR" figure is a search summary, not a primary source [unverified]. Find the survey behind it.

**Fix**
- Frame it as **"Safe sport: every team, every PE class, a lifesaver squad"**. Anchor it on cardiac arrest in sports halls, the AEDs SAM wants in gyms, and sports clubs.
- Use the Christian Eriksen story, which football-loving jurors remember [unverified details; check before use].
- **Galaxy tie:** run the pose AI on a Galaxy tablet in the gym.
- **Policy ask:** "Kids Save Lives in the Lithuanian PE curriculum", which fits the "local policy" criterion.

## F9: Švilpukas ant riešo, the whistle on the wrist (14/18, gut 7; 8.5 with a partner)

**Strongest points**
- **A 10-second wow.** Crowd noise plays from a speaker, a real whistle blows, and the band buzzes on the juror's wrist. Claps and sneaker squeaks don't trigger it.
- **It matches the Milan pattern exactly:** a named vulnerable user plus a wearable plus AI (VisionNex, CuraStep).
- **A killer local hook, now verified.** **Lithuania's men's deaf basketball team won gold at the Tokyo 2025 Deaflympics**, beating Venezuela 70:64. The Lithuanian delegation took 10 medals [verified: LRT, 15min/24sek]. "Basketball nation, deaf basketball world champions" makes the jury nod.
- **The AI is essential.** Prior systems (eWhistle, the NC School for the Deaf LED whistle, the 2024 Honduras ESP8266 wristband) all need a special whistle or a transmitter from the referee [verified: Patient Innovation, Scientific.Net/ResearchGate]. An on-device audio classifier that works with *any* whistle is the honest improvement.

**Killer weaknesses**
- **Team authenticity.** Without a deaf teammate, relative or club contact, it is "hearing kids designing for deaf people". NGO jurors notice that.
- **The audience is small, and the pilot needs deaf players** by Nov 6.
- **Theme fit is good** (sport, accessibility), but it is **not sustainability**, and the team leans towards sustainability.

**Fix**
- **Build it as a Galaxy Watch app.** The watch has a microphone, a vibration motor and on-device ML, and Samsung loves an accessibility story on its own hardware. Keep an ESP32 band as the cheap version.
- **Go/no-go test before Oct 16:** get the Lithuanian deaf sports federation or the deaf basketball team to agree to test it. With that letter, this jumps into my top 3.

## F10: Pulso kūno kultūra, heart-rate PE (14/18, gut 5)

**Strongest points**
- Students feel that PE grading is unfair to non-athletes.
- **Naperville (US) is a famous model** [scout-verified].
- **The Galaxy Watch is essential**, which gives the best Samsung-native sport fit.
- Lithuania is adding physical-fitness assessment in all schools (Feb–May, main PE group) [verified: diena.lt], so PE grading is a live topic.

**Killer weaknesses**
- **This is the wearable-in-PE genre the team already dismissed.** It feels like 2010.
- **Health data of minors used for grading is a GDPR special category.** Educators and parents on the jury will raise surveillance concerns.
- **The AI is mostly a formula** (heart-rate zones).
- **The pilot needs watches.**
- A juror will ask "can't kids just run on the spot for a 10?"

**Fix:** only as a feature ("effort mode") inside a bigger sport idea. Not a winner on its own.

## My ranked top 5 (as a juror)

1. **F8 HeartSquad.** It has the most memorable demo, a hard proven number (Denmark 20% → 77%), the easiest strong pilot, genuinely useful AI and European transferability. The one risk is theme fit, and the "Safe sport / lifesaver squad" framing handles it. It would do well at both the Baltic final and the European stage.
2. **Hybrid A: F1 + F2 + F5, "Oro treneris" (air coach) on upcycled Galaxy phones.** This is the best **sustainability** entry, and the one that fits Samsung's brand story best. Alone, F1 is déjà vu (6.5). As "old Galaxy phones that stop our school heating the street, know when the air outside is smoky, and give half the saved € back to the school" it is 8.
3. **F6 RecessBox.** The Sep 2026 phone rule is the most timely local hook in the shortlist, it has a Baltic proof (Estonia 46%), and it is a physical object rather than an app. Its AI needs to be the part that learns from the usage log, not a chatbot.
4. **F9 Whistle on the wrist (Galaxy Watch version).** The biggest "wow" per euro, plus the Deaflympics gold hook. It is conditional on a deaf-sport partner. If the team gets one, move it to 2nd.
5. **F5 Dūmų radaras.** The first heating season under the coal ban makes it a policy story. It works standalone only for a team from a private-house district; otherwise use it as part of Hybrid A.

**Not in my top 5:** F3 ShowerCoach (relatable, but commercial prior art, weak AI and privacy creep), F4 Heat detectives (better as a phase-2 slide), F10 HR PE (feels dated, privacy), F2 standalone (no hook), F7 (no pilot possible).

## Recommended merges and hybrids

- **Hybrid A, "Oro treneris" (sustainability; my pick if the team stays with sustainability).**
  - **Hardware:** an old Galaxy phone on the classroom wall with an SCD40 CO₂ sensor, plus one outdoor PM2.5 node per school.
  - **AI decisions:** *when* to air (predicted CO₂ crossing, timed to the next break), *how long* (learned decay curve against outdoor temperature), and *not now* (outdoor smoke or PM spike). It also flags "window open with heating on" in kWh and €.
  - **Business model:** Euronet 50/50, where the municipality returns half the heat saved.
  - **Pilot numbers:** % of lesson time above 1,500 ppm (before/after), window-open heating hours, drawer phones collected, and a student survey.
  - **Pitch arc:** stuffy room (relatable) → heat out of the window (sustainability) → drawer phones (circular, Samsung) → coal-ban smoke outside (policy).
  - **Risk:** scope creep. Build the classroom unit first; the outdoor node and 50/50 are slides if time runs out.
- **Hybrid B, "Safe sport" (sport):** F8 CPR + an AED locator layer ("where is the nearest AED to this gym?" from the SAM network data). Only if time allows; F8 is strong alone.
- **Hybrid C, "RecessBox + gear loop":** F6 stocked with donated outgrown sports gear, so it credibly bridges both themes.
- **F9 on a Galaxy Watch:** cheap to prototype and very Samsung. Keep it as the wildcard if a deaf-sport partner says yes.
