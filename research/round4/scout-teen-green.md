## 2. Full profiles

### #1 KlasėsOras: "we can't breathe in our classroom, and the heat is going out the window"

**Pitch:** A €30 box per classroom and an AI that tells the class exactly when to air the room for 5 minutes, instead of sitting in stale air or leaving the window tilted open all winter. Our student energy team turns the saved heat into money for the school.

**Relatable problem and LT evidence.** Every student knows the 6th-lesson headache in a stuffy room, and the fight between "open the window, it's hot" and "close it, I'm freezing".
- 11 Vilnius schools were measured, with a peak of **5,152 ppm CO₂ against a 1,500 ppm limit** [verified: sildymas-vedinimas.lt].
- KTU researchers warn that renovated schools are insulated but not ventilated [verified: KTU].
- The legal norm is 18–22 °C in winter [verified: NVSC], yet news stories report classrooms at 11 °C and student "strikes" over stuffy rooms [verified: tv3, 15min].
- German research (Univ. Bremen, Tiesler) found attention drops after about 20 minutes without airing, and CO₂ above 1,500 ppm causes perception problems [verified: search snippet, [sauter-controls.at](https://www.sauter-controls.at/gute-luft-gutes-lernklima-dank-co%E2%82%82-ampel/)].
- The "we noticed ourselves" line: *"We measured our own chemistry room: X ppm by the end of the lesson."* The team gets this number in week 1.

**Proven models abroad**
- **SAMHE (UK):** about 2,000 classrooms with AirGradient monitors (CO₂, temperature, RH, PM2.5), the world's largest school air study. Schools report better awareness and concrete ventilation actions [verified: [SEI](https://www.sei.org/projects/samhe/), [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666165923001485), [AirGradient](https://www.airgradient.com/professional/schools/stem/)]. AirGradient's page says schools report 10–20% energy savings from ventilation optimised for occupancy [verified as a *vendor claim*; not independently checked].
- **CO₂-Ampel (DE/AT):** traffic-light CO₂ monitors are widespread in German and Austrian classrooms. The Umweltbundesamt publishes airing guidance, and a Fraunhofer project studies classroom ventilation concepts [verified: [UBA](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/richtig-lueften-in-schulen), [Fraunhofer](https://www.iaqip.wki.fraunhofer.de/de/themen/lueftungskonzepte-in-klassenraeumen.html)]. One finding: after using the light for a while, classes keep the airing habit even without it [verified: snippet].
- **Euronet 50/50 (EU):** schools keep half of the money saved through pupils' behaviour change. More than 500 schools took part in the MAX project, with **at least 8% savings per building**, more than 1,100 MWh/yr saved and 339 t less CO₂ [verified: [Interreg Europe](https://www.interregeurope.eu/good-practices/5050-energy-saving-methodology-saving-energy-in-european-schools), [euronet50-50max.eu](http://www.euronet50-50max.eu/en/about-euronet-50-50-max/project-results-and-success-stories)].
- **Pitch line:** "SAMHE proved the sensor, Euronet 50/50 proved the money. We join them for Lithuanian schools."

**The prototype and the live 2-minute demo**
- **The box:** ESP32, an NDIR CO₂ sensor (e.g. Sensirion SCD40/41), a temperature and humidity sensor, a magnetic reed switch on the window, and a 3-colour LED ring. Optionally an **old Galaxy phone from a drawer** serves as the classroom display and hub. Samsung's own *Galaxy Upcycling* turned old phones into IoT devices [verified: [Samsung](https://news.samsung.com/us/samsung-galaxy-upcycling-programrepurpose-galaxy-smartphones-smart-home-devices/)], and it ties to the 53% "drawer phone" fact.
- **The web dashboard:** a school map coloured by room, plus the weekly energy team report.
- **On stage:**
  1. Put the box under a clear box or bag with a team member breathing into it (or a small CO₂ source). The ring goes green → yellow, and the AI says "red in about 4 min; open the window at the bell".
  2. Lift the lid (the "window"); CO₂ falls on the live graph.
  3. Leave the "window" open with a heater on. The dashboard flags "heat escaping: approx. X kWh / €Y wasted this hour".
  4. Show the real pilot chart from their own school.

**How AI and IoT are used, and why it helps**
- **Predictive airing.** Each room gets a small learned model of CO₂ rise rate against occupancy and timetable (Tamo/Eduka lesson times). It predicts minutes until 1,500 ppm and times the airing to the *next break*, so lessons are not interrupted. A plain threshold light only reacts once the air is already bad.
- **Heat-loss detection.** Pattern recognition on the window-switch and temperature traces finds "window open more than 15 min during the heating season", and a physics estimate converts that into kWh and €.
- **Overheating finder.** Rooms that sit above 22 °C for hours are flagged for the caretaker, because overheating wastes heat.
- **Weekly summary in plain Lithuanian** (LLM), written for the principal and the energy team.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6** (6 weeks; the heating season starts in October [unverified exact date; LT district heating typically starts when outdoor temps fall])
- **Weeks 1–2:** build 6–10 boxes (about €30–45 each [unverified price]).
- **Week 2:** baseline, with lights hidden, in 6–10 classrooms.
- **Weeks 3–5:** lights and advice switched on.
- **Metrics:**
  - % of lesson minutes above 1,500 ppm (before against after)
  - minutes with the window open during heating
  - rooms above 22 °C
  - a survey of about 200 students on headaches, sleepiness and "too hot/too cold"
  - a letter of support from the principal and ideally the municipal education or energy department.

**Target audience and reach**
- **Users:** students and teachers in the classroom.
- **Payer and adopter:** school owners, i.e. municipalities, which pay the heating bill; the Kaunas solar project shows they track school energy [verified].
- **Channel:** a municipal "50/50" agreement, the school's eco-committee or Eco-Schools network [unverified LT membership], and ŠMSM's healthy-school programmes.
- **Scale:** every LT school, since the box is cheap and open-source.

**The impact number:** "**Minutes per lesson above the 1,500 ppm limit: from X to Y**" (health), paired with "**window-open heating hours cut by Z%**" (energy). Project to €/school/yr using the ≥8% 50/50 benchmark [verified].

**Top 3 jury objections**
1. *"CO₂ monitors already exist and are cheap."* Yes, and that is the proof: 2,000 UK classrooms. Ours adds the parts that were missing in LT: prediction timed to breaks, detection of heat lost through open windows, and the 50/50 money mechanism that makes a municipality want it.
2. *"Will teachers bother?"* It is a light, not an app, and the airing duty goes to a student on rotation. German experience says the habit persists after the light is removed [verified snippet], so the device doesn't even need to stay forever.
3. *"Airing wastes heat, doesn't it?"* The opposite is true: short full airing loses less heat than a window tilted open for hours. That is exactly what the AI teaches [unverified as quantified; standard building-physics guidance, e.g. UBA "Stoßlüften", check before the pitch].

**Does it exist in LT?** Research measurements exist (VU thesis, sildymas-vedinimas.lt, KTU) and CO₂ meters are sold retail. **No LT school programme combining monitors with energy saving (50/50) was found** [unverified: 1 search].

### #3 ShowerCoach (Dušo laikrodis): "my mum yells that I shower too long; now we know what it costs"

**Pitch:** A clip-on shower meter that shows, live, how many litres and euros your shower costs. The same idea cut shower energy by 23% in Swiss homes, and we are testing it in Lithuanian apartment blocks.

**Relatable problem and LT evidence.** Every teen has had the "get out of the shower!" argument.
- In apartment blocks, hot water is metered and costs **€6.88–€7.99+/m³** [verified: tv3, housecare].
- 18% of Lithuanians can't keep their home warm [verified: Eurostat].
- Heating water is a large part of the utility bill (the per-household share is [unverified]; ask a utility).

**Proven model abroad.** **amphiro** (ETH Zurich spin-off, CH).
- In a Swiss Federal Office of Energy study, households with the meter saved **23% on average: about 440 kWh of heat and 8,500 L of water per year** [verified: [amphiro](https://www.amphiro.com/en?v=79cba1185463), [Green Business Award](https://greenbusinessaward.ch/en/nominierte/smart-shower-meter/)].
- Hotel guests saved 15% with no incentive [verified: snippet].
- A 3-person household can save about 645 kWh/yr [verified: snippet].

**Prototype and live demo**
- **Hardware:** a 3D-printed inline housing with a Hall-effect flow sensor (e.g. YF-S201), a waterproof temperature probe, an ESP32 and an LED ring. A micro-turbine for self-powering is a phase-2 option.
- **Phone app:** per-shower history for the family, and **€ per shower** calculated from the family's own utility tariff.
- **On stage:** pour warm water from a kettle or jug through the sensor into a bowl. The LED ring goes green → yellow → red as litres rise, and the screen shows "this shower: 38 L, €0.29, the same energy as charging your phone X times" [calculate the equivalence live; unverified numbers here].

**AI and IoT (honest assessment: IoT is the core, AI is supporting)**
- IoT: live metering.
- Temperature and energy calculations.
- **Personal targets.** The app learns each family member's typical shower (by time-of-day clusters) and sets gradual goals.
- **Anomaly detection** for dripping or running taps.
- **Month-end bill forecast.**

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6.** Recruit 15–25 families from the school.
- **Weeks 1–2:** display off (baseline).
- **Weeks 3–4:** display on.
- **Metrics:** litres per shower, °C and kWh, compared baseline against feedback. This mirrors amphiro's study design.
- Add a survey of about 200 students: "how long do you shower?"

**Audience and reach:** families in apartment blocks, reached through schools. The team can also offer it to heat utilities (Vilniaus šilumos tinklai, Kauno energija) or to municipal energy-poverty programmes as a giveaway.

**Impact number:** "**Litres (and €) per shower: from X to Y**", scaled to "if every family in our school did this: €Z/yr".

**Objections**
1. *"Shower timers cost €5."* A sand timer doesn't know temperature, flow or euros. The amphiro evidence is specifically about *live energy feedback*.
2. *"Where is the AI?"* Mostly IoT, which is still in the bonus list. AI adds personalisation and leak detection. **This is its weakest point against the formula.**
3. *"Won't people stop looking after 2 weeks?"* The amphiro data are from real long-term use [unverified duration]. Our pilot measures week 1 against week 2 of feedback to show whether it persists.

**Exists in LT?** No LT seller or programme was found [unverified: not specifically searched].

### #4 Šilumos detektyvai (Heat Detectives): "we took a thermal camera home and found where our heat escapes"

**Pitch:** The school library lends a €200 phone thermal camera, and our AI app turns the images into a family "heat-leak report" with fixes ranked by euros. Every student becomes an energy auditor of their own home, just as in UK libraries.

**Relatable problem and LT evidence**
- Old Soviet blocks cost **€104 against €61** for a renovated flat per January [verified: tv3].
- **18%** can't keep warm, the 3rd worst rate in the EU [verified: Eurostat].
- Families feel draughts at windows and balcony doors but can't *see* them.

**Proven model abroad**
- UK councils and libraries lend thermal cameras free: Cardiff, East Herts (winter 2025), Tonbridge & Malling, Forest of Dean, and Cambridge Carbon Footprint's programme [verified: [Cardiff](https://www.cardiffnewsroom.co.uk/releases/c25/37072.html), [East Herts](https://www.eastherts.gov.uk/latest-news/2025/borrow-thermal-imaging-camera-free-winter), [CCF](https://cambridgecarbonfootprint.org/what-we-do/thermal-imaging/)].
- Octopus Energy also lends FLIR cameras to customers [verified: [Octopus](https://octopus.energy/blog/flir-thermal-cameras/)].
- The known weakness: images are hard to read, and they need about 10 °C inside-outside difference [verified: snippet]. **Our AI solves the "hard to read" part.**

**Prototype and live demo**
- **Hardware:** a phone thermal camera (e.g. InfiRay P2/FLIR One class [unverified price €200–300]), or a cheap MLX90640 array on an ESP32/RPi.
- **App:** detects cold anomalies, classifies them (window seal, balcony door, radiator-back wall, thermal bridge, gap under door) and outputs a ranked fix list: "sealing tape €6, saves about €X/winter" [estimates must be sourced or modelled].
- **On stage:** a mock window frame with a deliberate gap, an ice pack behind it and a warm lamp in front. Point the camera at it, the AI boxes the leak and the report appears. Before and after: tape the gap and re-scan.

**AI:** image segmentation and classification of heat-leak types (trainable on the team's own scans plus public thermal datasets [unverified availability]). An LLM then turns the findings into a plain-language family plan. **Risk:** judges may call it "photo → AI" (see the team's rejection of test strips). The answer is that thermal images are genuinely hard for a layperson to read, which is the documented UK problem, so the AI does the expert part.

**Mini-pilot by Nov 6.** It is the ideal season, because heating starts in October.
- 20–30 families scan their homes.
- The app logs leaks found and fixes done.
- A €10 temperature logger is placed in one room for before and after.
- Survey the families on fixes made and comfort.
- Get a partner letter from the school library or the municipal library.

**Reach:** school and municipal libraries (a loan item like a book), homeowner associations (bendrijos) deciding on renovation, and the APVA renovation programme as a "first step" [unverified partner interest].

**Impact number:** "**X leaks found in Y homes; Z% fixed within 2 weeks; room temperature +N °C**".

**Objections**
1. *"Renters or poor families can't renovate."* Most fixes are €5–20 draught-proofing jobs, and the report is also evidence for the bendrija's renovation vote.
2. *"Thermal cameras are expensive."* One camera per school, lent out like a book, costs about €10 per family over a season.
3. *"Is the saving measurable?"* It is harder than for #1 or #3. Indoor temperature and self-reported fixes are the pilot metric, and the kWh estimate is modelled.

**Exists in LT?** No LT library loan scheme was found [unverified: not specifically searched; worth one check].

### #5 Radiniai: AI lost-and-found that turns forgotten hoodies into a school swap

**Pitch:** Our school's lost-property pile is photographed automatically. You search "black Nike hoodie" and find it, and whatever nobody claims in 30 days goes to the school swap instead of the bin.

**Problem and evidence**
- The lost-property pile is universal. Unclaimed items are usually thrown out at year end [verified: [RFID Journal](https://www.rfidjournal.com/news/rfid-helps-parents-find-lost-school-uniforms/69007/), [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/what-happens-to-the-lost-and-found-mound-at-the-end-of-the-year/2024/05)].
- LT hooks: textile separate collection became mandatory in 2025 and textile in mixed waste hit a record [verified]; a uniform costs about €100 [verified]; Vinted is Lithuanian [verified].

**Proven model:** **RagTagd (UK)**, with about 100 schools in its first 2 years, uses RFID tags on uniforms and texts parents when an item lands in the lost-property box [verified: RFID Journal, [ragtagd.com](https://www.ragtagd.com/)]. Ours needs no tags because vision AI does the job.

**Demo:** a small photo booth or shelf. Drop a hoodie, the AI tags type, colour and brand, a student types a description and gets the match, and a "claim" button appears.

**AI:** CLIP-style image and text matching, which is genuinely useful and core to the idea.

**Pilot:** catalogue the school's current pile, count items returned against the historical baseline, and weigh kilos diverted.

**Why it isn't higher**
- The sustainability impact is small in kg, so a jury may see it as "cute".
- High schoolers lose less than younger kids.
- Scale it by pitching it to all schools in the municipality, including primaries.

---

## 3. Recommendation

1. **KlasėsOras (17/18) is the top pick.**
   - Every juror has sat in a stuffy classroom, and the team can pilot it in its own school within days.
   - Three proven models to cite: SAMHE, CO₂-Ampel and Euronet 50/50.
   - A hard LT number (5,152 ppm) and a live physical demo.
   - It has both health and energy impact, with a municipal payer via 50/50.
   - The main risk is that "CO₂ monitor" is a common student project. Win on the **energy-money mechanism plus prediction plus the pilot data**, not on the sensor.
2. **Heat Detectives (16)** and **ShowerCoach (16)** are the "home" versions under the same LT hook (18% can't keep warm; €104 against €61).
   - They fit well as **one family-energy product with two modes**: scan the home in autumn, coach the shower all year.
   - Either could also be KlasėsOras's "phase 2: take it home".
3. **Avoid canteen food waste** despite its high formula score: Minova won with food waste in 2022, and Alytus already runs smart canteen scales [verified].

### Open checks for the final verification pass
- The LT heating season start date and whether any LT school runs a 50/50-style saving-sharing scheme.
- A source for "short airing loses less heat than a tilted window" (e.g. UBA).
- Whether any LT library or municipality lends thermal cameras.
- The share of hot water in a typical LT apartment utility bill.
