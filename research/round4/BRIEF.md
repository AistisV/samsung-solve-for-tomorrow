# Round 4 brief: optimise for WINNING, not for novelty

## What we learned (read this first)
Rounds 1–3 (see `RECOMMENDATION.md`, `research/round3/`) optimised for *technical novelty and depth*. That was the wrong target. The team's verdict on the round-3 finalists (moose thermal sentinel, garbage-truck battery fire alarm, satellite drainage AI) was "not sure these are winning". They are adult, institutional problems a teen team can't credibly own and a generalist jury won't *feel*.

**What actually won Solve for Tomorrow Baltics** (see `research/past-winners.md`, `research/round3/scout-global.md` part A):
- 2024 winner (Vilnius Lyceum): an "AI learning app that adapts to your learning style". Not original at all.
- 2nd place (Parksepa, EE): QR codes on library books → an AI animation teaser + points, for grades 1–3 reading. Simple.
- 2025 3rd place (Orientr, EE): AI-generated virtual orienteering routes. Existing concept; won because of "a ready prototype and a well-thought-out development plan".
- 2022 winner (Minova, Klaipėda): AI demand forecasting against restaurant food waste. An existing commercial category.
- Pan-European 2025 winners: a smart shoe for diabetics' feet, AI glasses for blind people, a power bank from old devices. A named user, a physical prototype, inclusion/health.

**The jury is generalists** (Samsung staff, educators, NGO and independent experts), not scientists. They reward a problem they instantly get, a working prototype, AI, a clear plan, a confident pitch and a credible team story. **Originality is a bonus, not a requirement.** "It's proven in Sweden; here's the Lithuanian version" is a *strength*, and it counts as the "research into prior efforts" the criteria ask for.

## Contest facts
- Teams of 3–5, grades 9–12, LT/LV/EE, one teacher. One idea. Theme: **Sport & tech** (make sport accessible/educational/meaningful; get students active) **or Sustainability** (tech for a greener tomorrow / living more sustainably).
- Stage 1 idea form **Oct 16 2026** · stage 2 developed concept + visual presentation **Nov 6** · local jury TOP 5 per country · mentoring · **Baltic final in December, pitched in English**. Prize: €5k cash + €5k Samsung devices for the winner.
- Official criteria, stage 2: technology usable · creativity + research into prior efforts · quality/realism · local relevance · completeness · wider impact / local policy. Final: best use of tech · clear, detailed plan · link to global problems / transferable · feasible (resources, law) · sustainable impact · **reaches the target audience** · **bonus for AI / IoT / VR / AR**.

## The WINNING FORMULA: score every idea on these
1. **Relatable in 10 seconds**: a generalist jury instantly *feels* the problem. Best: something students or their families experience ("we noticed this ourselves").
2. **Team authenticity**: a 16-year-old can credibly own it and pitch it with passion.
3. **Proven model to adapt**: ideally it has worked somewhere (another country, a product, a pilot) → "we adapt X to Lithuania". Proof of results abroad = credibility.
4. **Working prototype in 6 weeks**: an app and/or device that works live on stage in 2 minutes. Hardware is welcome if it makes the demo cooler.
5. **AI (or IoT/AR/VR) naturally at the core**, not bolted on. It doesn't have to be deep tech; it has to be clearly *useful*.
6. **Own mini-pilot numbers by Nov 6**: e.g. a survey of 200 students, a test in our school, 30 users, a before/after, a partner letter.
7. **Clear target audience and reach**: who uses it, and how it reaches them (schools, a municipality, an existing app/org).
8. **Measurable impact story**: one number that changes.
9. **Local hook**: a Lithuanian fact or statistic that makes the jury nod.

## Team preferences (from conversations)
- Leans towards **sustainability/nature** (felt cooler and more measurable than sport ideas so far), but it's open to sport if it's clearly a winner.
- Wants **realistic deployment and scaling** (rejected: "a box on 1,000 trees", "can't imagine it being used").
- Rejected as **overused/obvious**: "phone on a tripod, AI checks your exercise form", gamified step/points apps, AR city quests, screen-time-for-steps (last year's finalists were full of these).
- Rejected as a **tech wrapper**: photographing test strips (the tech must be *useful*, even if simple).
- The team can code and build hardware.

## Research constraints
- **The session's WebSearch budget is ~200 calls total across ALL agents.** Budget it: each research agent ≤ 25 searches; keep ≥ 40 for final verification.
- WebFetch is blocked for many domains (lrt.lt, most .lt news, frontiersin, springer, and others); rely on search results.
- Don't invent statistics; tag claims verified/unverified.
