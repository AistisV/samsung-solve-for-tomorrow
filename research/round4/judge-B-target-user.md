# Judge B: the real target users

**My role:** I play the people who would have to use each finalist and keep using it after the launch week. That means a teacher in a stuffy, cold 1970s school; the director and the municipal official who pay the bills; a parent in a Soviet-era block; a PE teacher; a 12-year-old; a deaf teen athlete and their coach; a caretaker.

**Questions I asked of every finalist:**
- Would I still use this in week 3?
- What does it cost me in money, time, hassle, privacy and consent?
- What would I use instead?
- Who pays?
- How does it get to 100+ schools or 1,000+ families?

### F1: Klasės oras (17/18, adoption 7)

**The real users**
- The **teacher** sees the light and decides whether to open the window.
- A **student "air duty"** opens it.
- The **director** answers to NVSC under HN 21.
- The **municipality** pays the heating bill and buys the boxes.
- The **caretaker** fixes the reed switch when it falls off.

**As a teacher in week 3:** A light I don't have to operate is about the only tech I will still look at in November. What I really need is not "the air is bad" (I can feel that). I need **"open it now, at the bell, for 4 minutes, and it will be fine until the end"**, because opening it mid-lesson makes the kids by the window complain about the cold. The bell-timed prediction is the feature I would actually use. A plain threshold light annoys me by going red in the middle of my explanation.

**As a director:** HN 21 makes the 1,500 ppm lesson average a legal limit [verified]. That cuts both ways.
- Upside: the box is a cheap compliance tool.
- Blocker: **a logged dataset that proves my school breaks HN 21 every day is a liability.** If a parent gets it, NVSC gets it.
- I would adopt it only if the reporting is framed as "improvement since the start" and the data stays internal by default.

**As the municipality:** Klaipėda is already buying CO₂ meters for its schools [verified]. So the payer exists and the budget line exists. But I have **already bought a meter**. I will not buy a second box. I would buy software or an add-on that makes the meters I own change behaviour and show heat saved.

**Costs to the user**
- About €30–45 per room [unverified].
- No personal data, so **no GDPR or consent problem**. This is the cleanest finalist on privacy.
- A reed switch on every window is a maintenance chore; make it optional.

**What they'd use instead:** an Aranet4 or a cheap NDIR monitor from pigu.lt [verified retail], or Klaipėda's installed meters. These are the honest substitutes, and the team must beat "just buy an Aranet".

**Is the impact number believable?** Yes. "% of lesson minutes above 1,500 ppm, before vs after" is measured directly, and the hidden-display baseline makes it credible. The **kWh/€ of heat saved** is modelled, not metered, so present it as an estimate.

**Changes that would make them actually adopt it**
1. **Make it brand-agnostic.** The AI layer should read any BLE/Wi-Fi monitor (Aranet4 has BLE [unverified API details]; AirGradient is open) as well as the team's own €30 box. Pitch line: "Klaipėda already bought meters; we make them work."
2. **Time airing to the bell** from the timetable. Never tell a teacher to open windows mid-lesson unless the room will exceed the limit before the bell.
3. **Give the airing to a student air duty** with the old Galaxy phone as the display.
4. **Keep reports internal and improvement-framed**, with an HN 21 summary for the director.
5. **Try to pilot in Klaipėda** schools that already have meters. Their before-data could exist already.

### F2: Energijos detektyvai 50/50 (13/18, adoption 4)

**The real users**
- The **school caretaker** and the **director**.
- The **municipal property and energy department**, which must sign a 50/50 deal.
- Students, only as an energy patrol.

**As a caretaker in week 3:** Ten smart plugs on the staff fridge and printers, and a dashboard I have to open. I won't. The printer on standby costs tens of euros a year [unverified order of magnitude], which is not worth my time.

**As a director:** Where the money really is (heating), I have no control and no sub-metering. Electricity out of hours is real, but the fix is usually one timer or one caretaker habit, found once, not a permanent AI.

**As the municipality:** A 50/50 deal means a council decision, a baseline method and a budget rule. That cannot happen by Nov 6. The best the team gets is a letter of intent.

**What they'd use instead**
- The school's own electricity smart-meter data from ESO, if a smart meter is installed (ESO is rolling them out; the school's coverage is [unverified]). It costs nothing and needs no hardware, which is why Energy Sparks works in the UK.
- A one-off student audit with a €15 plug meter.

**Is the impact number believable?** "Night base-load −X%" can be believable if the team gets the main meter's 15-min data. Savings from 10 plugs will look tiny next to the heating bill, and the jury will notice.

**Change that would make them adopt it:** Don't sell it as a product. **Fold it into F1** as the "€ layer": the heat saved by correct airing plus the electricity base-load found at night, packaged as a 50/50 proposal to the municipality.

### F3: DušoSargas / ShowerCoach (16/18, adoption 6)

**The real users**
- The **parent** who pays €6.88–7.99/m³ for hot water [prev-verified].
- The **teen** who takes long showers.
- Scale depends on a **heat utility or municipal energy-poverty programme** that hands devices out.

**As a parent in a Soviet-era block:** I would clip it on. There is a real family argument and a real bill, and in most blocks hot water is individually metered, so savings do reach my bill [unverified share of metered flats]. The Swiss evidence says the effect doesn't fade [prev-verified].

**What stops me**
1. **Safety.** Home-built electronics in a shower. It must be battery-powered, sealed (IP67 class), with no mains anywhere. If it needs charging every week, I stop in month 2. The a1's self-powering turbine solved this, and a school team probably can't [unverified].
2. **Privacy.** **"The AI recognises which family member is showering" is a deal-breaker for a 15-year-old.** It means a bathroom device reporting a teen's showers to parents. It is creepy, and it creates sibling and parent conflict. Keep per-person stats opt-in, self-selected with a button, and never pushed to parents.
3. **Adapter fit.** Old Soviet mixers and non-standard hoses need adapters (½" is standard, but old fittings vary) [unverified].

**Who pays at scale:** The retail market already failed once. amphiro moved away from the consumer a1 towards B2B with Oras [verified]. So the realistic channel is **utilities or municipalities buying devices as an energy-poverty or awareness giveaway** (LT has 18% who can't keep warm [prev-verified]), or school loans. A family won't buy it retail.

**What they'd use instead:** a €5 sand timer, a shower playlist, a stepped-flow shower head, or nothing.

**Is the impact number believable?** Yes: litres per shower, before and after, with a hidden-display baseline. It is the most rigorous design of the home ideas. Show € per family per month honestly; it will be about €2–5 [calculation, prev-scout], which is small.

**Change that would make them adopt it**
- Sealed battery unit.
- Drop automatic person identification.
- A utility or municipality partner letter as the distribution plan.
- Combine it with F4 into one home kit borrowed from the school.

### F4: Šilumos detektyvai (14/18, adoption 5)

**The real users**
- The **parent in an unrenovated block**.
- The **bendrija (homeowners' association) chair**.
- The **school library** that lends the camera.

**As a parent:** I'd borrow it once in the heating season. That's fine: it is a **one-off audit, not a habit**, so "week 3 use" doesn't apply. The honest problem is what happens next. In a Soviet block the big losses (panel joints, balcony slabs, the roof) are **collective**, and only the bendrija's renovation vote fixes them. Many flats already have replaced PVC windows [unverified share]. What I can do alone is draught strips and radiator foil, worth a few euros.

**Costs:** The camera costs €300 [verified Topdon TC001 €299.99]. One per school library is fine. Loss or damage risk is real, so it needs a deposit or teacher sign-out.

**What they'd use instead:** a candle or hand test for draughts, an energy auditor (paid), or the utility's free advice.

**Is the impact number believable?** It is weak. "Leaks found / % fixed / +°C" is self-reported and modelled.

**Change that would make them adopt it:** Make the **bendrija the user**. The class scans a whole stairwell, and the AI report becomes evidence for the renovation vote (APVA programme). Or make it the "find" step of a home kit with F3. Standalone, it is a nice lesson, not a movement.

### F5: Dūmų radaras (13/18, adoption 3)

**The real users**
- **Parents of asthmatic children** in private-house districts.
- **PE teachers**.
- The municipal environment department.

**As a parent of an asthmatic kid:** I'd install the alert, but what do I actually do? Close the windows and keep the child in during the evening, which I already do when I smell smoke.

**As a PE teacher:** Smoke peaks in the evening on calm, cold days, while PE runs in daytime hours, so "run indoors today" rarely matches the smoke window.

**As the municipality:** Vilniaus rajonas already runs **PM sensors in 22 seniūnijos with public data** [verified], and Google Maps shows air quality [verified]. Five student nodes add little.

**Blockers**
- A list of which children have asthma is **special-category health data under GDPR**, so alerting "families of asthmatic kids" via the school requires consent processes.
- Four weeks of data is too little to validate a street-level forecast.
- The team is unlikely to have a personal link (a family asthma story) unless one of them does.

**What they'd use instead:** the Vilniaus r. sensor map, Google Maps AQI, the AAA stations, or their nose.

**Change that would make them adopt it:** Make it a **forecast layer on existing public sensors** (no hardware), aimed at the municipality's "burn dry wood" campaign. That is a different, less jury-friendly project. I would not pick it.

### F6: RecessBox / PertraukųDėžė (17/18, adoption 6)

**The real users**
- **Students in grades 5–8** (the 12-year-old).
- The **PE teacher**, who owns the kit.
- The **director**, who worries about injuries and breakage.
- A **public-health specialist** from the municipal VSB.

**As a 12-year-old:** Yes, in week 1 everyone crowds the box. By week 3 it depends on whether the kit is still there and whether there is a space to play. I won't talk to a box in a noisy corridor, so **the voice AI game master is a gimmick to me**. A screen with 3 suggested games for "4 players, corridor, soft ball" is enough.

**As a director:** Who is liable when a kid gets hurt playing in the corridor? Many schools forbid running in corridors [unverified]. Who replaces broken balls? Most breaks are 10 minutes, with only one or two long ones [unverified exact LT timetables]. I would want it **outdoors or in the gym lobby**, plus a named student "kit captain".

**As the PE teacher:** Honestly, a basket of balls left unlocked does 70% of this. The card lock matters only if things go missing. Student NFC cards are not universal in LT schools [unverified], so this needs a fallback (a QR code on the student's e-diary account or a PIN).

**The strong part:** A channel exists. The **Aktyvi mokykla / health-promoting schools network of almost 700 schools** [verified] already promotes active breaks, and municipal VSBs have health budgets. The phone-restriction hook is real but nuanced: "most schools restrict phones, so breaks are now empty" [verified with nuance].

**Is the impact number believable?** "% of students moving during breaks, observed, before vs after" with borrowing logs is believable and measurable by Nov 6. It is the best-measured sport idea.

**Change that would make them adopt it**
- Replace the voice agent with an AI that learns which games actually get played by this age group and in this weather, and rotates suggestions.
- Add a restock and loss report for the PE teacher.
- Put it outdoors or in the lobby, run by student captains.
- Pitch it to the Aktyvi mokykla network as its "kit" component.

### F7: Traukinukas walking/bike bus (13/18, adoption 3)

**The real users**
- **Parents of 7–10-year-olds**, who give consent.
- The children.
- **Teen leaders**, who must also reach their own lessons at 8:00.
- The school and municipality, which carry liability.

**As a parent:** I would not hand my 7-year-old to a 16-year-old at 7:30 on a dark November morning. The KET "organised pedestrian group" frame assumes **accompanying persons** [verified], meaning adults in practice.

The GPS "where is my child" need is **already met by kids' GPS watches** (Xplora-class, common in LT) [unverified market share].

A system storing live locations of minors on a student-built server is a **GDPR and child-safety red flag**. It needs a DPIA-level answer (a data protection impact assessment) that a teen team can't give by Nov 6.

**As the teen leader:** It works only if the high school and the primary school are the same building (a progimnazija or gymnasium), and only while volunteers keep coming. Bike buses abroad rely on committed adults, averaging about 7 adults per 10 children [prev-verified].

**Pilot reality:** One line of 5–10 kids for 3 weeks needs individual parental consents, school approval and an adult co-leader. That is plausible but fragile, and the numbers will be tiny ("12 car trips replaced").

**Change that would make them adopt it:** Drop live GPS tracking. Make it a **route-planning and volunteer-rota tool for parent committees**, with the AI routes and a "school street" car-free window. That is a lighter tech story. I would not pick it.

### F8: HeartSquad CPR in PE (17/18, adoption 7)

**The real users**
- The **PE teacher** or school **public-health specialist**, who runs one 20–40-minute session per class per year.
- **Students**.
- The **VSB**, the Red Cross or first-aid trainers as partners.

**As a PE teacher:** This is the one sport idea I would **actually use every year**. It is a lesson-in-a-box with no daily habit required, which is exactly why adoption is realistic. My own first-aid certificate is mandatory every 5 years [verified snippet], so I already know the basics. What I lack is kit that shows 25 pupils whether they pushed deep enough. Laerdal's Little Anne QCPR does precisely that, with an instructor app for 6 learners [verified]. It costs hundreds of euros [unverified price], so most schools have none or one [unverified].

**Costs and blockers**
- Hygiene: compression-only avoids mouth contact, and wipeable covers handle the rest.
- DIY accuracy: calibrate and report the error.
- **No personal data is needed** beyond an anonymous score.
- Theme fit is the jury risk, not the user risk. The frame is "safe sport / every team knows CPR", linked to SAM's proposed AEDs in sports halls [prev-verified].

**What they'd use instead:** a Laerdal manikin if the budget allows, a Red Cross visit, or a YouTube video with no feedback.

**Is the impact number believable?** Yes: "% of students hitting the correct depth and rate, before vs after 20 minutes", logged automatically by the device, for 100–150 students by Nov 6. It is very credible.

**Change that would make them adopt it**
1. Publish it as an open build that **technology classes build** (every school makes its own manikins).
2. Tie it to the civil-safety curriculum and the AED rollout ("where's our AED, and who dares to use it?").
3. The pose AI must work on a propped-up phone without an extra person.

### F10: Pulso kūno kultūra (14/18, adoption 4)

**The real users**
- The **PE teacher** who grades.
- **Students**, especially the unsporty ones it is meant to help.
- **Parents**, who must consent to heart-rate collection.

**As a PE teacher:** LT schools **already run Polar heart-rate PE lessons** (Vilkija, Palanga) [verified]. If I want this, I buy Polar's school system, which is the established product. A student-built Galaxy Watch dashboard doesn't beat that.

**What stops me**
- Heart rate is **health data of minors** (special category under GDPR), so it needs parental consent.
- A **projected class HR board** can shame overweight kids, which is the opposite of the goal.
- Changing the grading rule is a department-level decision, not a gadget.

**As an unsporty 15-year-old:** Being graded on effort instead of sprint time is genuinely fairer, and I'd like it. But I don't want my pulse on the big screen.

**Change that would make them adopt it:** Make it private by default (each student sees only their own zone; the teacher sees aggregates), and pitch it as open software for the Polar straps schools already own. Even then, it is an "existing product" story with weak novelty and a thin pilot (5 watches, n≈50).

## 2. My ranked top 5 (target-user lens)

1. **F1 Klasės oras, with F2's € layer merged in** (17/18, adoption 7).
   - It is the only finalist where every real user wins with near-zero effort: the teacher gets a light, the student gets a duty, and the director gets an HN 21 compliance tool.
   - The payer already exists and is already spending (Klaipėda is buying CO₂ meters).
   - No GDPR issues.
   - Its survival condition: make it an **add-on/AI layer for meters schools already have**, timed to the bell. A standalone "another box" loses to the Aranet4.
2. **F8 HeartSquad** (17/18, adoption 7).
   - It is a once-a-year lesson kit, which is the easiest adoption pattern in schools.
   - It has cheap hardware, an automatic before/after pilot of 100+ students, and no privacy issue.
   - Its only real risk is the jury's theme fit (sport), not users.
3. **F6 RecessBox** (17/18, adoption 6).
   - There is a verified channel (the Aktyvi mokykla network of about 700 schools) and a measurable observed-activity number.
   - It needs a simpler AI (drop the voice agent), outdoor or lobby placement and a loss/restock plan.
   - This is the best choice if the team goes for sport.
4. **F3 DušoSargas** (16/18, adoption 6).
   - It has the most relatable family story and strong Swiss evidence.
   - It must drop automatic person identification (bathroom privacy) and be sealed and battery-powered.
   - Its scale depends on a utility or municipal giveaway, because retail failed (amphiro pivoted to B2B).
5. **F4 Šilumos detektyvai** (14/18, adoption 5), but only as part of a home kit with F3, or re-aimed at the bendrija's renovation decision.

**Below the line**
- F10 (Polar already in LT schools, health-data consent).
- F2 standalone (the caretaker won't open a dashboard; fold it into F1).
- F9 (small user base; go only if a real deaf athlete co-designs it).
- F5 (sensor networks already exist; the alert has no useful action).
- F7 (parents won't hand small children to teens plus GPS on minors; KET expects adults).

### Merges and hybrids I recommend

- **"Klasės oras 50/50"** (F1 + F2, with F1's upcycled-phone display story).
  - The classroom AI airing coach works with any CO₂ meter.
  - The heat not lost through tilted windows, plus the night electricity base-load, are rolled into one €-per-school figure.
  - It is offered to the municipality as a Euronet-style 50/50 deal.
  - Impact numbers: "% of lesson time above the HN 21 limit, X → Y" and "€Z per school per winter".
  - This is my overall pick.
- **"Namų energijos rinkinys" (home energy kit)** (F3 + F4): a school-library loan box.
  1. The thermal camera finds the leaks this week.
  2. The shower meter stays for a month.
  3. The AI writes one family report in €.

  The channel is the heat utility's energy-poverty programme. This is the home-side alternative if the team prefers a family story over a school one.
